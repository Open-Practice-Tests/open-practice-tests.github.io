Resources

Recommended SAT prep resources

You do not need an expensive prep course or a long list of subscriptions. Start with official College Board material, then add one or two focused resources when you need more explanation, structure, or practice.

This list is intentionally short. The goal is to recommend resources that add something useful without requiring an expensive prep program.

Resource and book links open in a new tab. Check the edition and shipping options before buying.

Official and free

Start here. Official material is the best reference for what the SAT actually looks and feels like.

Start here · Official · Free

College Board: Bluebook and Educator Question Bank

Best for: Realistic full-length practice and official SAT questions.

Bluebook has full-length digital practice tests that use the SAT testing app. The Educator Question Bank lets you filter official questions by assessment, section, domain, skill and difficulty, then export them with answers and explanations. It requires no account or sign-in. The Student Question Bank also offers official questions through College Board’s My Practice. Select Exclude Active Questions in the Educator Question Bank to keep questions from current Bluebook practice tests out of your practice sets.

Official SAT Prep · Free

Khan Academy Official SAT Prep

Best for: Learning or reviewing a skill before doing more official questions.

Free lessons, videos, hints, and practice for SAT Math and Reading and Writing. It is especially useful after a practice test shows a skill you need to strengthen or relearn.

Live tutoring · Free

Schoolhouse.world SAT Bootcamps

Best for: Students who benefit from live explanations, structure, and accountability.

Free small-group SAT tutoring led by trained peer tutors in partnership with College Board. Students can focus on Math or Reading and Writing while working through official SAT practice material.

Books worth considering

A good book can be valuable when you want a more systematic explanation of a subject. Use these for learning and targeted practice, then return to official College Board questions to check your progress.

A simple way to use these resources

  1. Diagnose with official practice. Take a Bluebook practice test and review the questions you missed or took too long to solve.
  2. Fix one weakness at a time. Use Khan Academy, Schoolhouse, one of the books, or a focused video lesson to understand the underlying skill.
  3. Check your progress with official questions. Export a PDF with answers and explanations from the Educator Question Bank, then use our SAT test builder to create practice tests you can download and take offline. Return to Bluebook regularly to measure your progress in the official test format.

You do not need to use all seven resources. Pick the smallest set that addresses your current weaknesses, and spend most of your time actively solving and reviewing questions.
