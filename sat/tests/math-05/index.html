Math · Module 2 · Question 19 of 22

Nonnegative real numbers x and y satisfy 2x + y ≤ 18 and x + y ≥ 10. What is the least possible value of 2x + 3y?

Answer: B. 22 Subtract the second bound from the first to obtain x ≤ 8. Since x + y ≥ 10, this also gives y ≥ 2. Rewrite the target as 2(x + y) + y ≥ 2(10) + 2 = 22. The point (8, 2) satisfies both original inequalities with equality and attains 22.

Watch for: Prove a lower bound and exhibit a feasible point that achieves it.

Math · Module 2 · Question 20 of 22

For what value of k does |x − 2| + |x + 4| = k have infinitely many real solutions?

Answer: B. 6 For every x between −4 and 2, the left side is (2 − x) + (x + 4) = 6. Thus k = 6 gives every point in that interval as a solution. Outside the interval, the expression equals either −2x − 2 or 2x + 2, so a fixed other k yields at most two solutions.

Watch for: The sum of distances to two fixed points is constant between them; it is not constant everywhere.
