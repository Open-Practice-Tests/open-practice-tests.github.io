Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

A pyroclastic flow is not uniform from top to bottom. Its dense lower portion carries coarse volcanic fragments along the ground, while a turbulent cloud of ash and gas rises above it. Thus, the flow ______ both a ground-hugging component and an elevated one.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: comprises

The passage identifies two parts of a pyroclastic flow: a dense lower portion and an ash-and-gas cloud above it. The final sentence summarizes that division. “Comprises” means includes or consists of, so it expresses the relationship between the whole flow and its components. Nothing suggests that the flow hides, comes before, or limits those parts; they constitute the flow being described.

A: “Precedes” establishes an earlier position in time or sequence. The two components are parts of the flow itself.

C: “Restricts” means limits. No limit on the location, extent, or movement of either component is asserted in this summary.

D: “Obscures” means hides. The passage explains what the flow contains, not whether either of its components can be seen.

Watch for: When a sentence summarizes listed parts, identify the relationship between the whole and its components before considering stronger meanings.

Indifferent modifies the things Cynthia discusses, not Cynthia herself. Her quiet conversation about ordinary, unimportant matters conceals the serious troubles Molly suspects beneath her composure. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

Pigeons learned to assign breast-tissue images to categories defined by the presence or absence of malignancy. Their accuracy remained high on images they had not encountered during training. This transfer weakens an explanation based solely on remembering individual pictures: the result is consistent with an ability to Underlined: abstract End underlined. recurring visual patterns from particular examples and apply those patterns to new cases.

Correct choice: derive

The unfamiliar images matter because they cannot be identified by recalling those particular pictures from training. The researchers’ interpretation is that the birds derived recurring visual information from individual examples and used it beyond those examples. Abstract has that sense here. Condense concerns making information shorter or more compact, reproduce means copy, and classify means assign to groups; none names the derivation of patterns that enables the described transfer.

A: Condense would mean compress information into a smaller or shorter form; the passage instead concerns deriving recurring features from individual examples.

B: Reproduce would mean copy an existing pattern, whereas unfamiliar examples require applying information extracted from other images rather than reproducing those images.

Which choice best describes how the speaker develops the comparison in the text?

The speaker first recalls brushing away the fly's summer activity with a “thoughtless hand.” The questions then connect the fly to the speaker. Finally, the speaker imagines a “blind hand” ending the speaker's own dancing, drinking, and singing. This movement from an incident to a parallel emphasizes shared vulnerability. B: The opening does describe interrupted activity, but the poem develops an analogy about vulnerability rather than a rule for outdoor behavior.

D: The imagined “blind hand” threatens the speaker, so the comparison undermines human security rather than celebrating unusually long human life.

Watch for: Track what each stanza adds to the previous one; an accurate structural answer must account for the ending as well.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

TEXT 1

Councillor Ina Vey urges a town to finish converting an old warehouse into a public craft school. Most of the construction budget has already been spent, she notes, and the remaining bill is much smaller. Stopping now would waste the money already invested. That earlier expenditure, Vey argues, is therefore a strong reason to complete the work.

TEXT 2

Councillor Rami Dell notes that the payments already made cannot be recovered whether the school is completed or not. Dell favors comparing the benefits and additional costs of finishing with those of other available uses of the remaining funds. The small remaining bill may favor completion, Dell argues, but the irreversible earlier expenditure cannot distinguish the choices now available.

Based on the texts, how would Dell (Text 2) most likely respond to Vey's argument (Text 1)?

Correct choice: By distinguishing the relevant cost of work still needed from money already spent under either available choice

Dell separates expenses that depend on the current decision from expenses already incurred regardless of that decision. The remaining bill can affect whether completion is worthwhile, so Dell does not reject every part of Vey's comparison. The earlier payments, however, cannot be recovered by either completing or stopping the project. Dell therefore disputes treating those payments as an additional reason favoring completion, and instead compares the future benefits and costs of the available choices.

A: Dell does not challenge the estimate or introduce overruns. The objection concerns how already-incurred expenses should enter the current decision.

C: Including past payments as a distinguishing cost repeats the approach Dell rejects; the current comparison concerns additional costs and future benefits.

Key takeaway: Distinguish costs shared by every current option from future costs that actually change depending on which option is chosen.

The following passage is from an original work of fiction.

Leon had remembered the courthouse clock as enormous. Returning after twenty years, he found a modest dial above a narrow door. Which choice best states the main idea of the passage?

Correct choice: A familiar object prompts Leon to recognize a difference between its physical dimensions and the importance it held in his childhood experience.

The clock appears small in the present but was enormous in Leon's memory. The unchanged chipped numeral links the present object to the one he watched as a child, while the memory of long afternoons explains its former significance. His final thought connects the clock's perceived size to the time and attention he invested in it. The passage therefore examines the relationship between an object's physical scale and its remembered importance, rather than documenting restoration or claiming that Leon can regain his childhood experience.

A: Restoration is an explanation Leon considers, not an established fact. The chipped numeral redirects him toward his earlier experience of time.

B: He is late for an appointment, but the text does not show that attachment makes him unwilling to go. The focus is perception and remembered waiting.

C: Leon notices a contrast in experience; he does not recover or feel reassured about regaining his childhood sense of time.

Key takeaway: In literary main-idea questions, distinguish a character's provisional explanation from the understanding toward which the passage moves.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

Three apprentices in a seal-cutting workshop used different personal marks. Their surviving practice impressions sometimes combine a mark normally associated with one apprentice and a border normally associated with another. A historian proposes that the apprentices exchanged partly completed seal matrices rather than independently copying one another's finished designs.

Which finding would most directly support the historian's account of matrix exchange?

Correct choice: An unfinished matrix bearing one apprentice's mark has a border cut with another apprentice's distinctive damaged tool.

The historian proposes that the same physical matrix passed between apprentices before completion. A single unfinished matrix that combines one apprentice's mark with another's distinctive tool trace directly supports work by both on that object. Shared motifs or similar borders could instead arise from copying or common models. A common clay supply concerns the impressions, not the cutting and exchange of the matrices themselves.

A: Separate seals with similar patterns remain consistent with independent copying; they do not provide evidence that one matrix changed hands.

B: Common clay explains the material of impressions but does not identify who cut a matrix or whether apprentices exchanged it.

C: A shared design collection could explain similarity through common models without any exchange of partly completed physical matrices.

Watch for: Evidence for shared designs is weaker than evidence for shared work on one object; match the finding to the proposed mechanism.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 13 of 27

Which statement identifies a grille opening that meets both design requirements?

Correct choice: The 12 mm opening qualified, with 80% airflow and 10% water-entry trials.

Both requirements must hold for the same opening width. At 12 mm, airflow reaches the required 80%, while water enters in only 10% of trials, below the 20% maximum. The smaller opening excludes water more effectively but restricts airflow too much. The larger openings provide enough airflow but admit water too often. Accurate percentages alone do not make a choice satisfy the complete design rule.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 14 of 27

Which conclusion is most strongly supported by the text?

The reconstruction establishes that a crack present during immersion would allow dye to color the cloth beneath it under the workshop's conditions. Yet cloth beneath several current cracks is uncolored, and subsequent dye removal is excluded. Those cracks therefore cannot all have been open during the immersion. The supported inference is temporal: at least some formed afterward. No additional coating or different immersion duration is needed to explain the observation.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 15 of 27

Two shore blowholes open into the same sea cave. Microphones beside both openings record a low-frequency sound during each wave cycle. To locate its source, a team caps the northern opening. The sound disappears at both microphones, although measured cave pressure remains unchanged. In a separate calibration, a loudspeaker at a fixed position inside the cave emits a tone at the same frequency; installing the cap also makes that tone inaudible at both microphones. Therefore, the disappearance of the natural sound ______

Which conclusion most logically completes the team’s interpretation of the blowhole experiment?

Correct choice: does not establish that the northern opening generates the sound, because the cap can also alter transmission to the microphones.

In the calibration, the source continues to be the fixed loudspeaker, yet capping makes its tone inaudible. The cap can therefore change whether a sound reaches the microphones without identifying where the natural sound originates. The matching disappearance during wave cycles cannot distinguish altered generation from altered transmission. Constant measured pressure does not resolve that ambiguity, and the calibration neither locates the natural source nor proves that an opening has only an outlet function.

B: Fixing the calibration source reveals an effect on transmission; it does not fix or identify the natural source’s location.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 16 of 27

A young tuatara has a light-sensitive structure on top of its head commonly called a third eye. Initially visible beneath a translucent patch, the structure eventually becomes covered by ______ does not mean that the structure itself has vanished.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: pigmented scales; this change

The first clause states that the structure becomes covered by scales. The next states that this change does not mean the structure has vanished. Both clauses can stand independently, and the semicolon joins them correctly. The introductory modifier beginning Initially visible belongs to the first clause and does not change the status of the second clause or remove its need for a boundary.

A: This choice supplies no boundary between the statement about the covering and the separate statement interpreting that change.

B: The material after the comma has its own subject and finite verb, so this choice incorrectly joins independent clauses with only a comma.

C: Although makes the second clause dependent; a semicolon cannot attach that dependent clause to the preceding complete statement in this construction.

Watch for: Ignore introductory modifiers temporarily and test whether the material following a proposed semicolon can stand as a sentence.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 17 of 27

The Anglepoise lamp uses springs to hold its movable arms in position. Its heavy cast-iron base anchors the ______ the aluminum arms and shade remain light enough to reposition readily.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: assembly; meanwhile,

Two complete clauses describe complementary material choices: the base anchors the assembly, and the arms and shade remain light. A semicolon separates those clauses. Meanwhile introduces the second clause as a conjunctive adverb and is followed by a comma. The adverb cannot itself join independent clauses with a comma or with no punctuation, and a second semicolon would wrongly isolate it from the clause it introduces.

A: Meanwhile is a conjunctive adverb, not a coordinating conjunction; commas around it cannot repair the comma splice between the two complete clauses.

B: Without a clause boundary, the two complete statements run together; meanwhile can express their relationship but cannot itself join them grammatically.

C: The second semicolon isolates meanwhile from its clause; an introductory conjunctive adverb should be followed by a comma in this construction.

Watch for: Conjunctive adverbs describe relationships but do not perform the grammatical joining work of coordinating conjunctions such as “and” or “but.”

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 21 of 27

Correct choice: accounts

The two subject units are "the gradual widening of the underwater channels" and "the sudden displacement of the nearby ridges." Their heads, "widening" and "displacement," are both singular, and they are joined by "neither ... nor." The sentence therefore requires the singular finite verb "accounts." Present perfect, future, and present progressive forms do not fit the sentence’s stated relationship between a publication in 1869 and a milestone in 1880.

A: Is already appearing is present progressive, which conflicts with the completed historical publication placed eleven years before another past milestone.

C: Has already appeared is present perfect, but the sentence locates the appearance before a separate past event rather than relative to the present.

D: Will already appear places the appearance in the future, contradicting the explicit 1869 date and its position before the 1880 event.

Watch for: Use explicit dates and the reference event to distinguish an earlier past action from an action connected to the present.

Barkcloth served as clothing in many Pacific societies. Its uses, however, were not limited to dress. ______, in Fiji, printers used barkcloth as a surface for newspapers, giving the material a role in the circulation of written information.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The second sentence makes a general claim: barkcloth had uses beyond clothing. The newspaper example supplies a specific case of such a use, so “For instance” fits. The passage does not compare two printing materials or summarize an extended argument.

A: “Even so” would introduce a concession, but using barkcloth for newspapers illustrates rather than qualifies the claim that its uses extended beyond clothing.

B: The newspaper detail is new supporting evidence, not a concluding restatement of information already developed earlier in the passage.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 24 of 27

Orson Squire Fowler’s book on octagonal houses presented general design principles and encouraged readers to invent many details themselves. Someone seeking a ready-to-build plan could reasonably find its few examples a practical limitation. ______, that limitation does not by itself show a failure to provide the kind of guidance Fowler intended: filling in the details was partly the reader’s assigned role.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: Nevertheless

The second sentence concedes a practical limitation for readers seeking complete plans. The final sentence limits the inference that can be drawn from that fact: it does not by itself establish failure relative to Fowler’s intended purpose. Nevertheless marks that qualification. The sentence does not prove that the book fulfilled its purpose; it explains why the stated limitation alone is insufficient evidence that the book failed.

A: Subsequently introduces a later event, but the final sentence evaluates the meaning of a limitation rather than describing something that happened afterward.

B: Similarly would introduce an analogous assessment, whereas the final sentence limits the implication of the negative assessment just granted to certain readers.

C: Consequently would present the final judgment as a result of the practical limitation, whereas the sentence qualifies what can be inferred from that limitation.

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • That mold can be reused to make multiple wax copies of the model.
  • Each wax copy is coated with a heat-resistant outer shell.
  • The wax is melted out, leaving a cavity into which molten metal is poured.
  • The outer shell is removed to release the metal casting.

The student wants to resolve the apparent contradiction between losing a wax model and producing multiple castings of one design. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct choice: A reusable mold supplies new wax copies, so losing each copy during casting does not prevent repeated production of the design.

The process contains two distinct molds and separates a design from any one wax copy of it. A reusable mold made from the original design can produce fresh wax copies; each copy then enters a casting process in which its wax form is lost. Multiple castings are therefore compatible with the loss of each individual copy. The key resolves the apparent contradiction without making the wax itself or the final shell reusable.

A: The notes say wax is melted out and the shell is removed; they do not describe an intact wax model preserved through repeated pouring.

C: The wax copy is melted out rather than reused as an intact model, while the outer shell is removed after casting, not melted as described.

D: The wax form disappears before metal is poured, so a finished casting cannot retain that intact copy to shape another casting.
