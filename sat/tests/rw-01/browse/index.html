Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

An acoustic consultant was asked to assess a proposed factory near a residential area. Her report delineates the tasks that would need to occur before construction: measuring the existing nighttime noise, modeling the factory's likely contribution, and evaluating possible barriers. It leaves the results of those tasks open rather than predicting that the factory will or will not meet the town's requirements.

As used in the text, what does the word "delineates" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Outlines

The report identifies and describes the tasks to be carried out before construction. It does not carry them out or settle their results. "Outlines" captures this planning function of "delineates": laying out the relevant components clearly. The following list supplies the content of that outline, while the final sentence reinforces the distinction between specifying an investigation and announcing what the investigation will find. The distinction determines the relevant meaning.

A: Resolving the tasks would mean settling the issues they address. The report leaves the eventual findings open.

C: The report specifies future tasks but does not itself measure noise, run the models, or evaluate actual barrier performance.

D: Restricting would mean limiting the tasks. The text presents a description of required work rather than limits imposed on its scope.

Key takeaway: Use both the examples and the stated limit on a document's purpose to select the appropriate word sense.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

When the excavation team proposed that a set of shallow ceramic bowls had held pigments, historian Elian Voss pointed to the absence of colored residue. The bowls had been scrubbed during an early restoration, however, making such residue unlikely to survive. Voss was willing to ______ that the missing residue did not rule out the team's interpretation, while continuing to question whether the bowls' shapes supported it.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: concede

Voss initially treats the missing residue as a reason to doubt the pigment interpretation. The restoration history weakens that objection, and Voss accepts this particular point while retaining a different objection about shape. To "concede" is to acknowledge a point, often reluctantly or in response to an argument. The word captures Voss's limited acceptance without implying that Voss endorses the excavation team's entire interpretation. That acknowledgment is deliberately limited.

A: Anticipating concerns expecting something beforehand. Voss is responding to an explanation of existing evidence, not predicting a later result.

C: Discovering emphasizes finding previously unknown information. The sentence describes acknowledging the force of a counterargument rather than independently uncovering a fact.

D: Maintaining emphasizes continuing to assert a position. Here Voss accepts a limitation on the objection Voss had initially raised.

Key takeaway: A concession can accept one point while leaving the larger disagreement unresolved.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

The apprentice expected the thick ceramic cup to withstand repeated heating better than the thin one. Yet the thick cup developed cracks after three cycles, while the thin cup remained intact after twenty. The test results ______ the thick cup's appearance of greater durability: its substantial walls concealed a weakness at the joint between its base and sides.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: belied

The thick cup looked more durable, but it failed much earlier than the thin cup. The results therefore contradicted the impression created by its substantial appearance. "Belied" expresses that relationship: the actual performance showed an appearance to be misleading. The explanation after the colon identifies the concealed weakness that made the thick-looking construction less durable in this test, rather than simply adding a minor qualification to the expected advantage.

B: "Anticipated" would place the results before or in expectation of the appearance. The results instead expose the earlier impression as misleading.

C: "Reinforced" would mean strengthened the impression. The thick cup's early cracking instead undermines that impression.

D: "Qualified" would limit or modify a claim. Here the results reverse the apparent durability advantage rather than merely narrowing it.

Key takeaway: Distinguish a finding that reverses an impression from one that merely limits the scope of an otherwise supported claim.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 4 of 27

To compare three ways of sealing seams in a rain canopy, technician Laila Enor stitched each test seam in fabric cut from one continuous roll. She used the same needle, thread, and stitch spacing for every sample. These precautions reduced the chance that differences in the underlying seam would be mistaken for differences in the sealants' performance. Enor then subjected the sealed samples to identical sprays and measured how much water passed through each seam.

Which choice best describes the function of the bolded sentence in the text as a whole?

Correct choice: It explains how features of the procedure help isolate the factor being compared.

The bolded sentence explains the purpose of keeping the fabric and stitching procedure the same. Those features reduce alternative explanations for later differences in water leakage, allowing Enor to compare the sealants more clearly. The sentence thus justifies controls in the experimental procedure. It neither reports the outcome of the rain-resistance test nor explains why sprayed water is the particular exposure used. It establishes the comparison's fairness.

A: The precautions reduce a possible source of confusion; they are not presented as an unresolved weakness that invalidates the comparison.

C: The sentence addresses the fabric and stitching before testing. It does not explain the choice of a water spray.

D: The text has not yet reported results. The sentence explains the design of the comparison rather than summarizing performance.

Key takeaway: Separate an explanation of experimental controls from the results those controls may help establish.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

A town once relied on its courthouse bell to announce meetings, but new buildings now block the sound in several streets. One proposal would simply increase the bell's volume. Acoustic consultant Ivo Serrin objects that doing so would make the sound uncomfortable near the courthouse while leaving some sheltered streets poorly served. He recommends several quieter speakers instead, arguing that distributing the sound sources would address the uneven coverage that a louder central source would preserve.

Which choice best describes the overall structure of the text?

Correct choice: It identifies a problem, evaluates one proposed response, and presents an alternative designed to address that response's limitation.

The text first identifies the loss of reliable bell coverage caused by new buildings. It then considers raising the volume and explains why that response would leave the coverage problem partly unresolved while creating discomfort. Finally, Serrin proposes distributed speakers, connecting that alternative to the limitation of the first proposal. The organizing relationship is problem, inadequate response, and better-targeted alternative, rather than a comparison of explanations for the problem.

A: The town still needs meeting announcements. The text changes the proposed delivery system, not the purpose of the announcements.

B: Blocked sound is the given cause. The competing ideas are responses to the problem, and no distinguishing experiment is described.

C: The text offers a rationale for distributed speakers but provides no evidence from another location and no adoption conditions.

Key takeaway: Track the jobs performed by successive parts of the passage, not merely their shared subject.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 6 of 27

The following passage is from an original work of fiction.

Nessa had practiced cutting cloth until her scraps fell into neat, obedient stacks. On her first morning in the shop, she expected the master tailor to inspect them. Instead, he handed her a coat and asked where its owner kept his keys. Nessa searched the empty pockets. Only when she noticed the pale crescent worn into the lining did she understand that the coat had already answered. Her clean practice cuts suddenly seemed to belong to a conversation she had learned only half of.

Which choice best describes the function of the bolded sentence in the passage?

Correct choice: It marks Nessa's recognition that an object's wear can communicate information beyond its present contents.

Nessa initially searches the pockets for the keys themselves. The worn crescent then leads her to recognize that the coat preserves evidence of how it has been used, even though the pockets are empty. The sentence marks this change in how she reads the object. The final comparison to a half-learned conversation broadens that realization: precise cutting is only part of the knowledge needed for the work.

B: The tailor asks a revealing question, but the evidence is visible in the coat. The text does not portray him as concealing it.

C: The crescent indicates use, not necessarily faulty construction. Nessa learns to interpret wear rather than condemn the workmanship.

D: Nessa's cutting practice does not produce the realization. The passage contrasts that practiced skill with a different kind of observation.

Key takeaway: For a sentence-function question in fiction, connect the local discovery to the character's developing understanding.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 7 of 27

TEXT 1

Linguist Dara Pell argues that a map showing one pronunciation boundary can be useful even when speech varies gradually across a region. If the map states which feature it tracks and how the boundary was selected, it makes a particular pattern easy to compare with settlement routes. The line is a tool for an explicit analytical purpose, not a claim that neighboring speakers differ in every respect.

TEXT 2

Linguist Sen Ralo observes that readers often treat sharply drawn pronunciation maps as pictures of naturally separate speech communities. Ralo favors displaying the underlying survey values alongside any boundary line, since a written explanation of how a line was chosen may not prevent the visual appearance of a sharp division from dominating a reader's interpretation.

Based on the texts, how would Ralo (Text 2) most likely respond to Pell's argument (Text 1)?

Correct choice: By accepting a possible use for boundaries while questioning whether verbal qualifications adequately prevent their misinterpretation

Pell permits a boundary when its feature, selection method, and analytical purpose are explicit. Ralo's concern is that even a written explanation may not counteract the visual impression of sharply separated communities. Ralo would therefore challenge the sufficiency of Pell's qualifications while leaving open the possible usefulness of a boundary. Showing survey values addresses that concern without requiring the abandonment of boundaries or of comparisons with settlement routes.

A: Ralo discusses how maps can mislead their readers, not whether pronunciation and settlement patterns can meaningfully be compared.

B: Ralo explicitly favors adding underlying values because written explanations may be insufficient; this response contradicts that recommendation.

D: Ralo worries that a line exaggerates separation. This choice reverses the direction of that concern.

Key takeaway: A second author may challenge the sufficiency of a safeguard without rejecting the method it is meant to safeguard.

Venn uses the combination to distinguish the style from the broader set of local quilting practices from which it developed.

A definition limited to a particular combination of visible features, Tal suggests, would exclude works that participants in the tradition considered continuations of it.

Which statement best describes the relationship between the two texts?

Correct choice: Tal questions whether a definition based only on Venn's visible features can include all later works accepted as members of the style.

Venn uses a visible combination of features to recognize the style's beginnings. Tal does not dispute the described early quilts; instead, Tal considers later works whose accepted membership depends partly on continuity among makers and practices. Those works may lack one of Venn's visible features. Tal therefore questions whether a definition restricted to those features would include all accepted later members of the tradition, rather than simply proposing a different date of origin.

A: Tal does not contest Venn's observations about the early quilts; the texts focus on different stages of the tradition.

The following passage is from an original work of fiction.

On his last afternoon as the building's porter, Cal gave his replacement a notebook labeled Repairs. The pages held few measurements. Instead, they advised that Mrs. Renn disliked a knock before nine, that a loose stair made the upstairs child afraid, and that the tenant behind the green door would say a leaking pipe could wait when it could not. The notebook records information about residents' habits, fears, and understatement of problems. Its lack of diagrams contrasts that knowledge with technical information the replacement expects. Cal's final thought explains his purpose: physical defects will become apparent, but the residents' less visible needs may not. The passage therefore centers on transmitting a kind of professional knowledge that a conventional repair record could miss, not on Cal's regret or an assessment of the replacement's technical competence.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

Archaeologist Yara Fen argues that a change from broad pouring lips to narrow spouts in a community's ceramic vessels reflects increased demand for accurately measured small quantities of liquid. A competing account attributes the change to a manufacturing technique that made narrow spouts easier to produce. Fen does not deny that the new technique was used, but she contends that the practical advantage in pouring helped determine which of the newly feasible shapes makers adopted.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support Fen's contention?

Correct choice: Among shapes equally easy to produce with the new technique, makers favored those that dispensed small quantities most accurately.

Fen accepts that a new technique made narrow spouts feasible but argues that precise pouring influenced the choice among feasible shapes. If makers favored the most accurate small-quantity dispensers even when production difficulty was held equal, that pattern would support the proposed practical selection criterion. It would distinguish Fen's account from one based solely on ease of manufacture, rather than merely confirming that the new technique and new shape appeared together.

A: Less finishing work supports the manufacturing explanation. It does not isolate an advantage in accurately dispensing small quantities.

B: Shared clay rules out one material difference but does not connect the chosen shape to measuring or pouring accuracy.

C: This timing supports a connection to the technique but does not establish that precise pouring influenced the selection of shapes.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 12 of 27

After a transit agency revised its emergency announcements, listeners recalled the required action more accurately. The revised messages were shorter, and they placed the required action first rather than after an explanation. Linguist Toma Iven attributes the improvement primarily to the shorter duration, arguing that listeners had less information to hold in memory. Another researcher suggests that placing the action first, rather than reducing duration, was the more important change.

Which finding, if true, would most directly weaken Iven's explanation relative to the alternative?

Correct choice: In controlled trials, moving the action first improved recall without shortening messages, whereas shortening messages with the action still last produced no improvement.

The trial separates the changes that occurred together in the agency's revision. Moving the action first improves recall even when duration is unchanged, while shortening alone fails to improve recall when the action remains last. This pattern directly favors action placement over reduced duration as the explanation for the observed gain. The other findings describe the revised messages or their length without comparing the two proposed causes independently.

A: This links word count to duration but supplies no evidence about which feature caused better recall of the required action.

B: Remembering both parts is compatible with either explanation. It does not compare the effects of the two changes.

D: A favorable rating confirms that listeners prefer the revision, but it does not distinguish the effects of shorter duration and action placement.

Key takeaway: When two features change together, seek evidence that varies them separately and measures the outcome named in the claim.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 14 of 27

In one resist-dyeing method, a wax coating prevents dye from reaching the cloth beneath it. A laboratory reconstruction established that, under the conditions used by one historical workshop, dye entered every crack present in the wax during immersion and colored the exposed cloth. On a surviving sample made by that workshop, several cracks in the wax lie above completely uncolored cloth. The sample has not been washed or otherwise treated in a way that could remove dye since its production.

Which conclusion is most strongly supported by the text?

Correct choice: At least some of the cracks now visible in the wax formed after the sample's immersion in dye.

The reconstruction establishes that a crack present during immersion would allow dye to color the cloth beneath it under the workshop's conditions. Yet cloth beneath several current cracks is uncolored, and subsequent dye removal is excluded. Those cracks therefore cannot all have been open during the immersion. The supported inference is temporal: at least some formed afterward. No additional coating or different immersion duration is needed to explain the observation.

A: The passage supplies no evidence about a shorter immersion; its stated reconstruction result applies to the workshop's conditions.

B: The reconstruction states that dye entered every crack present during immersion. This choice contradicts that premise rather than drawing an inference from it.

D: A second coating is not established. It would not by itself explain why cloth beneath cracks present during dyeing remained uncolored.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 16 of 27

Sound-absorbing panels reduced echoes in the rehearsal room, but the musicians still struggled to hear one another. The engineer identified a different ______ the panels had weakened useful reflections along with unwanted ones.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: problem;

The blank joins two independent clauses: "The engineer identified a different problem" and "the panels had weakened useful reflections along with unwanted ones." A semicolon can join these closely related complete clauses. No conjunction is needed. The second clause explains the problem identified in the first, and both remain grammatically complete when considered separately. The semicolon supplies the required boundary without adding an incorrectly placed comma.

A: With no punctuation, the two independent clauses would run together improperly at the words "problem the panels."

B: A comma alone cannot join these two independent clauses; this construction would create a comma splice.

C: The comma immediately after "and" incorrectly separates the coordinating conjunction from the subject of the clause it introduces.

Key takeaway: Identify the complete clause on each side of a blank before choosing punctuation for the boundary.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 25 of 27

Before recommending a raised doorway sill for public buildings, researchers evaluated two independent requirements. In rain tests, the sill prevented wind-driven water from entering. ______ in access tests, people using wheeled mobility aids crossed it comfortably.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: In addition,

The first finding concerns protection from rain, while the second concerns accessibility. Both findings show that the design satisfies an independently established requirement. "In addition" introduces the second favorable result alongside the first. C: "An assessment of" is a noun phrase, not a verb parallel to "record" and "note" in the required action sequence.

Key takeaway: In a coordinated action series, identify the shared grammatical structure and preserve it through the final item.
