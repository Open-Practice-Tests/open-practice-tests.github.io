Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

When a dance company offered a neighborhood school free use of its rehearsal room, the school wanted to ______. Rather than offer a similar room, which the company did not need, the school made its unused storage area available for the company's costumes. The two institutions thus exchanged different but equally welcome forms of assistance.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: reciprocate

The school wants to return a favor: the company has offered rehearsal space, and the school responds by offering storage. To reciprocate is to respond to another's action with a corresponding action. The services need not be identical. Indeed, the phrase "different but equally welcome forms of assistance" establishes a reciprocal exchange while explaining why the school does not simply copy the company's original offer.

A: Imitating would mean copying the company's action. The school deliberately supplies a different service to return the company's favor.

B: Negotiating involves discussing terms. The passage describes the school providing a benefit in response to one already offered, not bargaining over an agreement.

C: Expediting means making a process happen faster. Nothing indicates that the school's aim was to accelerate the rehearsal-room offer.

Key takeaway: A reciprocal action returns or responds to another action; it need not reproduce that action in exactly the same form.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

The new delivery schedule eliminated overnight storage, but it could exact a considerable cost from the drivers. Instead of making a predictable circuit, they would have to wait at several depots for loads assembled at irregular intervals. The dispatch manager therefore evaluated the schedule in terms of both warehouse savings and demands on drivers' time.

As used in the text, what does the word "exact" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Require

Here, "exact" is a verb meaning to demand or require a payment, effort, or sacrifice. The schedule could require drivers to sacrifice considerable time while waiting for irregularly assembled loads. The final sentence confirms this meaning by referring to "demands on drivers' time." Although "exact" can be associated with precision in other uses, neither measurement nor precise description fits the action that the schedule itself performs in this sentence.

A: Repaying would compensate drivers for a cost already incurred. The proposed schedule instead imposes an additional cost on their time.

C: Specifying means stating something precisely. The schedule could impose a burden even before anyone states its precise size.

D: The manager evaluates costs, but "exact" describes what the schedule could demand from drivers, not the act of calculating that demand.

Key takeaway: Test a word's grammatical role and its object before selecting a familiar meaning associated with the same spelling.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

Early scholarship treated the patterned endpapers in inexpensive books as a ______ feature: attractive packaging with little bearing on how readers interpreted the works. Historian Leena Das argues, however, that repeated visual motifs on those pages prepared readers to recognize themes developed in the main text. For Das, the endpapers belong within an account of the books' meaning, not merely their manufacture.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: peripheral

The early scholars considered the endpapers decorative packaging with "little bearing" on interpretation. A peripheral feature is one considered secondary or outside the central concern. Das challenges that assessment by connecting the visual motifs to themes in the main text. The contrast is therefore between a feature treated as peripheral to meaning and a feature that should be included in an interpretation. The word need not describe the endpapers' physical position at the ends of a book.

A: Persistent would describe the feature's continued presence over time. The relevant contrast concerns interpretive importance, not how long patterned endpapers remained common.

B: A unifying feature would help connect elements of a work. That resembles Das's position, whereas the blank describes the earlier view she challenges.

C: The passage contrasts an earlier judgment of limited importance with Das's account of significance. It does not say the earlier scholars disagreed among themselves.

Key takeaway: Identify whose judgment the blank expresses; a word that fits the later argument may contradict the earlier position being summarized.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 4 of 27

The blank leaves at the end of several nineteenth-century account books initially appear to be unused paper. Yet their placement is unusually consistent: each follows a completed annual balance rather than an arbitrary page number. Some also bear a faint penciled instruction to begin the next year on the facing page. These features suggest that the blank leaves helped separate accounting periods. Their emptiness was part of the books' organization rather than evidence that the books had been abandoned.

Which choice best describes the function of the bold sentence in the text as a whole?

Correct choice: It supplies an additional observation supporting an explanation for the deliberate placement of the blank leaves.

The text first notices consistent placement of blank leaves after annual balances and then adds the penciled instructions. Both observations support the conclusion that the leaves intentionally separated accounting periods. In particular, "begin the next year on the facing page" gives the blank space a function tied to a change of year. The bold sentence therefore supplies corroborating evidence; it neither presents the mistaken initial interpretation nor complicates the final explanation with a counterexample.

A: The penciled instructions support deliberate organization. The initial appearance of unused paper, not those instructions, creates the impression of incompleteness.

B: The instructions agree with the placement after annual balances. They reinforce the separation explanation rather than identify an exception.

D: The text describes features of one collection. It does not establish a comparison group using a different accounting method.

Key takeaway: For a sentence-function question, connect the sentence to the claim it supports, challenges, narrows, or explains.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

A choreographer reconstructing an old duet first asked the original dancers to reproduce its steps. Their versions differed, and neither matched the surviving diagram exactly. She then asked each dancer to perform the sequence leading into the disputed passage. Several previously missing movements returned. This result did not establish that either new version was historically exact. It did suggest that remembering a dance can depend on reenacting its transitions rather than retrieving its movements as an isolated list.

Which choice best describes the overall structure of the text?

Correct choice: It presents an unsuccessful approach, describes a modified approach, and draws a limited conclusion from the improvement.

The first request produces incompatible and incomplete accounts. The choreographer changes the task by adding the preceding sequence, and missing movements return. The final two sentences distinguish improvement in recall from proof of historical accuracy. Thus, the text develops from an initial difficulty through a modified procedure to a qualified inference about contextual retrieval. The qualification matters: the modified approach provides evidence about how remembering may work without settling which version of the duet is correct.

A: The passage explicitly withholds a finding of historical exactness. It never questions the value of attempting a reconstruction.

B: The limited inference follows the experience rather than precedes it, and the result does not confirm a complete or exact reconstruction.

C: The choreographer does not select either dancer's account as more reliable. Historical accuracy remains unresolved after the modified approach.

Key takeaway: Track changes in method and preserve the author's stated limit on what a successful result can establish.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 6 of 27

The following passage is from an original work of fiction.

When the neighborhood council asked Imani to write its minutes, she imagined herself as a pair of obedient hands. At the first meeting, two members argued for an hour over where to plant the new trees, and neither yielded. Yet by the time she had reduced their debate to three neat sentences, everyone in her draft appeared to have agreed. The tidy paragraph was the least faithful thing she had written all evening. She opened a fresh page and began with the question that had never been answered.

Which choice best describes the function of the bold sentence in the text as a whole?

Correct choice: It marks Imani's recognition that the orderly form of her summary has obscured the unresolved disagreement it was meant to record.

The council members have not agreed: they argue and neither yields. Agreement appears only after Imani condenses their debate into a neat draft. Calling the paragraph her least faithful writing therefore registers a conflict between orderly presentation and accurate representation. The sentence also motivates her fresh start with the unanswered question, which restores the unresolved issue to the record. It changes her understanding of the supposedly passive job she accepted: arranging others' words can alter what those words appear to establish.

A: The problem is the summary's misleading appearance of agreement. The passage does not establish that the council members used imprecise language.

B: Imani states no preference about where the trees should go. Her distortion comes from tidying the debate, not promoting her own proposal.

D: She restarts the record with an unanswered question. Nothing indicates that she intends to persuade the members or resolve their disagreement herself.

Key takeaway: To identify a sentence's function, connect the realization it expresses to both the earlier assumption and the later action it changes.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 7 of 27

TEXT 1

In experiments on detached root segments, increasing the abundance of transporter P increased the rate at which the segments absorbed a dissolved nutrient. Botanist Ana Velasquez therefore proposes that raising P abundance in a whole plant should increase the plant's total nutrient uptake.

TEXT 2

Velasquez's segments were continuously supplied with a sugar solution. In an intact plant, however, root cells depend on sugars delivered from the leaves, and operating transporters consumes energy obtained from those sugars. Increasing transporter abundance may accomplish little if sugar delivery is already limiting transporter activity.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to the proposal in Text 1?

Correct choice: Its success could depend on an energy supply that the detached-segment experiment kept available but the whole plant might limit.

Text 1 generalizes from sugar-supplied root segments to whole plants. Text 2 identifies a condition held favorable in the experiment: access to the sugars that power transporter activity. In intact plants, delivery from leaves could limit that energy supply. Consequently, adding transporters would not necessarily add usable transport capacity. Text 2 does not deny the experimental result; it questions whether a condition enabling that result would be present in the proposed application.

B: Such a failure would weaken the proposed transporter effect. Text 2 instead asks whether the favorable energy conditions extend to intact plants.

C: Text 2 raises a difference in energy supply, not uncertainty about whether detached and intact roots absorb the same nutrient.

D: Text 2 distinguishes sugar-derived energy from nutrient absorption. It does not dispute which substance transporter P absorbs.

Key takeaway: When transferring a result to a new setting, identify the enabling condition that may differ between the experiment and its application.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

TEXT 1

Readers' handwritten comments in old books offer access to literary judgments that circulated outside formal criticism. Even when several readers copied a similar phrase, the marks they chose to preserve can reveal how a work was evaluated in private reading.

TEXT 2

Some identical comments in student-owned copies follow the sequence of a surviving lecture almost word for word. In those cases, the marks may record a teacher's evaluation rather than an independently formed judgment. Still, a student's decision to add a question beside a dictated comment can reveal a response that the comment alone does not.

Which choice best describes a point on which the authors of the two texts would most likely agree?

Correct choice: Handwritten marks can provide evidence of readers' responses, although the evidentiary value of a particular mark depends on how it was produced.

Text 1 treats handwritten comments as evidence of responses outside formal criticism, including choices about what to preserve. Text 2 cautions that a dictated comment may belong intellectually to a teacher, yet it also identifies an added question as evidence of a student's response. Both therefore permit inferences from handwritten marks while making their interpretation depend on production and context. Agreement does not require Text 2 to accept every comment as an independent judgment.

A: Text 2 distinguishes recording a teacher's view from independently endorsing it and specifically notes that an added question may indicate a separate response.

C: Text 2 gives an explicit counterexample: repeated wording can derive from a lecture. Text 1 does not require independence of phrasing.

D: Neither text ranks these sources for establishing literary meaning. Both discuss evidence of responses to a work rather than its correct interpretation.

Key takeaway: Find an agreement broad enough to include both positions while retaining any limitation explicitly introduced by either author.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 9 of 27

A transit agency added spare buses at its central terminal to recover from delays. This worked when disruptions occurred near the terminal: a replacement bus could enter service quickly. On long outer routes, however, getting a spare bus to the affected location took nearly as long as waiting for the delayed bus. Assigning the same spares to outer depots improved recovery there but left fewer available at the center. The agency's problem was therefore not simply the number of spare vehicles; their usefulness depended on where a disruption occurred relative to where they waited.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Correct choice: The value of a fixed reserve of buses depends on its placement, so improving recovery in one part of a network can reduce flexibility elsewhere.

The example holds the reserve fleet constant and changes its location. Central spares help with nearby delays but arrive too slowly on distant routes; outer-depot spares improve those routes while reducing the central reserve. The main idea combines this spatial dependence with the resulting trade-off. A response that simply recommends centralization or decentralization misses the passage's point: neither placement is uniformly useful across all possible locations of a disruption.

B: The central location works for nearby disruptions but performs poorly on outer routes. The text does not establish it as the best general solution.

C: Moving spares to outer depots does improve recovery on those routes. The limitation is distribution of a fixed reserve, not the impossibility of improvement.

D: The same reserve is redistributed. The passage does not report reducing the operating fleet or the total number of buses required.

Key takeaway: A main idea should explain both sides of a trade-off rather than promote the option that succeeds in only one setting.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 10 of 27

The following passage is from an original work of fiction.

I remembered my grandmother saying, "Leave room for the unexpected," whenever she packed a suitcase. Or perhaps she said it only once, and I had distributed that single remark across a dozen departures. My brother offered to search her letters for the phrase. I asked him not to. I did not fear that the words would prove false; I feared discovering how little paper could tell us about the pauses around them. The uncertainty in my memory seemed, for once, more faithful than an exact transcript could be.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Correct choice: The narrator values a memory's uncertain emotional context over a documentary effort to establish the precise recurrence of its words.

The narrator is unsure whether one remark has become attached to many departures. Yet the refusal of the letter search rests on a different concern: paper may preserve words without preserving "the pauses around them." The final sentence values an uncertain memory as potentially more faithful to the experience than a transcript. This is a qualified judgment about this memory's emotional context, not a general claim that documents are useless or that accuracy is undesirable.

A: The narrator questions how often the phrase was spoken, not whether its wording changed. No deliberate variation by the grandmother is established.

B: The passage gives no disagreement about the advice's practical meaning. The difference concerns whether searching letters would illuminate the memory.

D: The narrator explicitly says that proving the words false is not the concern. The limitation of documentary evidence motivates the refusal.

Key takeaway: For literary main ideas, distinguish the narrator's explicit reason for a choice from a plausible motive the text expressly rejects.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

Art historian Oren Malik argues that the muted highlights in painter Isa Vale's fruit studies were a deliberate feature of the original paintings, not a result of later surface deterioration. The highlights are much less distinct than those in Vale's portraits, even when the fruits and faces appear to receive similarly strong light. Malik seeks evidence that bears specifically on when this difference became visible.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support Malik's argument?

Correct choice: A review written while Vale was exhibiting the newly completed fruit studies describes their unusually subdued highlights in comparison with her portraits.

Malik needs evidence that the distinctive highlights existed when the paintings were new. A contemporary review explicitly comparing the newly completed fruit studies with the portraits supplies that timing. The contrast therefore predates later deterioration and supports the claim that it belonged to the original appearance. It does not establish every aspect of Vale's artistic intention, but among the findings offered it most directly supports the proposed deliberate effect over the stated deterioration explanation.

A: Shared varnish does not establish their original appearance; different pigments, conditions, or treatment histories could still produce different later changes.

B: Relative prices could reflect subject, size, or demand. They do not establish whether the highlights were subdued before later deterioration occurred.

D: Planning object placement does not show whether muted highlights were originally present. It concerns composition rather than the timing of the visual difference.

Key takeaway: When a claim distinguishes original design from later change, seek evidence that locates the disputed feature early in the object's history.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 12 of 27

After a freight company introduced a shared booking system, the proportion of its departing vans that were filled to capacity rose sharply. Its managers attributed the rise to the system's ability to combine small orders into fuller loads. The company transported approximately the same total volume of goods on the same routes before and after the change. A consultant argues that these facts alone do not identify the booking system as the cause of the higher proportion.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support the consultant's argument?

Correct choice: At the same time the system was introduced, the company replaced many large vans with smaller vans that reached capacity with substantially less cargo.

The outcome is the proportion of vans reaching capacity, not the amount of cargo carried by an unchanged fleet. Smaller vans can reach capacity with less cargo, so replacing large vans could raise that proportion even without better consolidation. Equal total volume and unchanged routes do not control vehicle capacity. The finding therefore supplies a simultaneous change capable of producing the reported result, supporting the consultant's narrower point that the stated facts do not isolate the booking system's contribution.

A: Those orders still enter the same system, so this does not establish an alternative mechanism for the higher proportion of full vans.

B: Ordinary variation across both periods does not explain a sustained increase in the proportion filled. The passage already compares approximately equal total volumes.

C: An additional expected benefit does not challenge the proposed loading mechanism. It provides no alternative explanation for the observed proportion.

Key takeaway: Before interpreting a change in a percentage, check whether the units or thresholds used to calculate that percentage have also changed.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 14 of 27

The first serial installment of a fictional mystery included a scene in which a visitor concealed a railway ticket. When the story was collected into a single volume, the author removed that scene but left the final revelation unchanged: the visitor had secretly traveled to the town where the crime occurred. An editor later argued that readers of the serial and readers of the volume encountered different distributions of clues, even though they eventually learned the same solution.

Which choice most logically follows from the text?

Correct choice: Readers of the serial were given an earlier piece of information relevant to the visitor's secret journey that readers of the volume were not given in that scene.

The concealed ticket appears in the first serial installment and relates to the later disclosure of a secret journey. Removing that scene from the volume removes this particular early clue while preserving the ending. The supported inference is therefore a limited difference in what information the two groups of readers receive. It does not follow that the serial immediately reveals the whole solution or that the volume supplies no other basis for suspicion.

B: The ticket is relevant to a secret journey, but the passage does not establish that it identifies the crime's complete solution.

C: The passage identifies one omitted clue, not all clues in the volume. Other information could still support suspicion before the ending.

D: No motive for the revision or reader reaction is provided. The inference must concern the distribution of information, not an invented reason for editing.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 15 of 27

In a hypothetical cell line, protein R normally attaches a cargo vesicle to a docking site before the vesicle releases its contents. Cells lacking R still produce cargo-filled vesicles, but those vesicles rarely dock. Researchers added an artificial tether that attached the vesicles to the same docking sites without using R. The tethered vesicles released their contents at nearly the normal rate even though the cells still lacked R.

Which conclusion is most strongly supported by the text?

Correct choice: At least much of R's contribution to successful cargo release can be replaced by physically attaching vesicles to docking sites.

The absence of R disrupts docking even though cargo-filled vesicles are present. An artificial attachment that bypasses R restores release to nearly the normal rate. This result supports a replaceable attachment role: accomplishing the physical docking step by another means restores much of the process. The conclusion remains appropriately limited because "nearly" normal release does not establish perfect replacement, and the experiment need not reveal every function R performs under normal conditions.

A: The passage explicitly states that the cells still lacked R. Restoration therefore cannot be attributed to resumed R production.

C: Cells lacking R already produce cargo-filled vesicles. Nothing indicates that the tether changes the cargo or restores cargo formation.

D: The experiment establishes a docking-related contribution, not a preventive function. The text does not report premature release before docking.

Key takeaway: A successful bypass can identify a replaceable step in a process without proving that the bypassed component has only one function.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 16 of 27

The delivery company's first route map — a sheet only twelve centimeters ______ was designed to fit inside a driver's pocket. Despite its small size, it showed every depot in the network and the roads connecting them.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: wide —

The core sentence is "The delivery company's first route map was designed to fit inside a driver's pocket." The phrase beginning "a sheet" interrupts the subject and verb to describe the map. Because the text opens that interruption with a dash, a second dash must close it after "wide." The closing dash allows the sentence to resume with "was designed" while keeping the descriptive information clearly separate.

A: The phrase begins with a dash. A comma cannot serve as its matching closing mark before the main verb "was."

C: A semicolon cannot close the dash-delimited interruption, and the words before it do not form an independent clause.

D: Without a closing dash, the interruption runs into the main predicate. The opening dash requires a corresponding close in this sentence.

Key takeaway: Locate the main subject and verb, then close an interrupting phrase with punctuation that matches its opening mark.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 17 of 27

The first rehearsal used a rigid sequence of lighting ______ the second proceeded only after every performer had learned the revised cues. Comparing the two rehearsals helped the director separate difficulties caused by the choreography from those caused by the lighting schedule.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: changes;

"The first rehearsal used a rigid sequence of lighting changes" is an independent clause. So is "the second proceeded only after every performer had learned the revised cues." A semicolon can join these related independent clauses without adding a conjunction. The phrase "the second" stands for "the second rehearsal" and remains a complete subject; its shortened form does not turn the second clause into a fragment or a modifier.

B: The following clause already has a subject, and "proceeded" takes no direct object. "Which" therefore has no grammatical role in a relative clause.

C: Without punctuation or a coordinating conjunction, two independent clauses are fused: one begins "The first rehearsal," and the other begins "the second."

D: The words on both sides are independent clauses. A comma alone creates a comma splice rather than a complete boundary.

Key takeaway: An omitted repeated noun does not prevent a phrase such as "the second" from serving as the subject of an independent clause.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 18 of 27

To compare the two editions, the book historian focused on one readily measurable ______ the width of the printed text on each page. This choice allowed her to identify a change in page design without assuming that either edition used the original typesetting.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: feature:

The words before the colon form a complete clause: the historian "focused on one readily measurable feature." The following noun phrase specifies that feature: "the width of the printed text on each page." A colon appropriately introduces this explanation after an independent clause. The following material does not need to be a complete sentence after a colon, but it would need an independent-clause structure after a semicolon or as a sentence following a period.

Correct choice: them,

The introductory clause begins with "Although," so it depends on the main clause "the company kept its existing loading crew." The comparison "faster than workers could load them" remains inside that dependent clause; its additional subject and verb do not make the whole introduction independent. A comma closes the introductory dependent clause after "them" and connects it to the main statement. The other choices impose boundaries that require a complete clause before them.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 20 of 27

At the center of the staging plan ______ two movable platforms, each small enough to pass through the theater's narrow side entrance. Together, they let the performers change levels without requiring a permanent staircase on the stage.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: are

The sentence uses inverted order: a location phrase comes first, followed by the verb and then the subject. Reordering it gives "Two movable platforms are at the center of the staging plan." The plural subject "two movable platforms" requires "are." Neither "center" nor "plan" is the subject; both occur inside the opening prepositional phrase. The phrase "each small enough" describes the platforms individually but does not replace the plural subject.

A: The verb agrees with the plural subject "two movable platforms," which follows it. The singular noun "center" belongs to an introductory prepositional phrase.

B: "Has been" is singular and therefore does not agree with "two movable platforms." The location phrase does not determine the verb's number.

C: "Remains" is a singular verb form. The inverted word order does not change the plural number of the actual subject.

Key takeaway: In sentences beginning with a location, check after the verb for the subject before deciding whether the verb should be singular or plural.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 21 of 27

In a letter outlining her plans for a new theater, the designer wrote that construction ______ the following spring. When she sent the letter, the site had not yet been cleared; later records show that work started as she had predicted.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: would begin

The designer writes at a past moment about construction expected in the future relative to that moment. "Would begin" expresses that future-in-the-past relationship. "The following spring" identifies the expected later time, and the uncleared site confirms that construction had not already begun when she wrote. The later records then report fulfillment of the prediction. The sentence requires a finite verb that presents the beginning as anticipated, not already completed.

A: The participle alone cannot serve as the finite verb of the clause beginning "that construction." It also does not express the predicted future event.

B: The past perfect would place the beginning before the designer's letter. The text explicitly states that construction was still planned for the following spring.

C: The present perfect links an event to the present rather than expresses a future event viewed from the past act of writing.

Key takeaway: In reported plans, locate the future event relative to the past act of speaking or writing rather than relative to the present.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 22 of 27

Having compared the growth patterns of cells in both dishes, ______. Their report emphasized that cell shape alone could not establish whether the treatment had changed the rate of division.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: the researchers selected several colonies for closer observation

The introductory phrase "Having compared the growth patterns" describes whoever performed the comparison. That actor must be the subject immediately following the comma. "The researchers" can logically compare patterns and then select colonies, so the correct choice attaches the modifier to its intended actor. The distractors all mention researchers somewhere, but mentioning the actor later does not repair an opening modifier that grammatically attaches to colonies, an activity, or growth patterns.

A: The phrase would describe "the growth patterns" as having performed the comparison. The logical actor is the researchers, not the patterns.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 23 of 27

A delivery cooperative charges each shop for the time its parcels occupy warehouse space, rather than for the number of parcels stored. A large order collected within an hour can therefore cost less to store than a small order left for several days. ______, shops that can collect orders promptly have a financial reason to schedule frequent pickups even when each pickup contains relatively few parcels.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: Consequently

The pricing system ties cost to storage time. Frequent prompt pickups reduce that time even if each pickup carries few parcels. The final sentence identifies the resulting financial incentive, so "Consequently" correctly marks a conclusion from the preceding explanation. The relative size of the orders does not create a contradiction: the passage explicitly explains why a small order can cost more if it remains in storage longer.

A: The final sentence draws an incentive from the pricing rule; it does not present a separate, parallel pricing example.

B: Prompt collection lowers storage costs under the stated rule, so the incentive follows from the rule rather than holds despite it.

C: The passage does not contrast earlier and later policies. The relationship concerns a current consequence of how charges are calculated.

Key takeaway: Choose a consequence transition when the next sentence derives an incentive or outcome from the mechanism explained just before it.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 24 of 27

To shorten loading delays, a theater widened the doorway used to bring scenery onto the stage. The wider opening allowed two carts to pass side by side, but the crew still had to unload each cart at a single narrow workbench. ______, the total time needed to move and unpack a full production's scenery changed very little.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: As a result

The opening was widened, but unloading still occurred at one narrow workbench. The final sentence gives the overall outcome of this remaining bottleneck: little change in total handling time.
