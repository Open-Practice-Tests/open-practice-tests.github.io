Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

An octopus has many neurons in its arms, outside its central brain. These local networks allow an arm to perform some movements without detailed instructions from the brain. Such Underlined: autonomous End underlined. activity does not mean that the brain is uninvolved: experiments show that it can also use information obtained by an arm to guide that arm’s movements.

As used in the text, what does the word “autonomous” most nearly mean?

Correct choice: independent

The preceding sentence describes movements performed without detailed instructions from the brain. In this context, autonomous therefore means independent. The final sentence limits the independence: an arm can act locally while remaining subject to central guidance. This qualification does not reverse the word’s meaning; it prevents readers from treating partial independence as complete separation from the animal’s nervous system.

B: Accidental would mean unintended or unplanned; functioning without detailed central instructions does not make an activity accidental.

C: Stationary means remaining in place, whereas the activity being described explicitly includes movements made by an arm.

D: Delicate describes fineness or fragility; the passage instead distinguishes local control from instructions supplied by the brain.

Watch for: Use the explanation immediately before a technical adjective, then check whether the following sentence limits its scope.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

The following text is from Arnold Bennett’s 1907 story collection The Grim Smile of the Five Towns. Horace has inherited money; Sidney is his unwell brother.

And with part of the fortune he bought a house with a large garden up at Toft End, the highest hill of the hilly Five Towns, so that Sidney might have the benefit of the air. He also engaged a housekeeper and servants. With the remainder of the fortune he obtained a partnership in the firm of earthenware manufacturers for whom he had been Underlined: acting End underlined. as highly-paid manager.

As used in the text, what does the word “acting” most nearly mean?

Correct choice: serving

The phrase “as highly-paid manager” identifies a job Horace genuinely held before becoming a partner. In this setting, acting means serving in a professional capacity. Nothing suggests that Horace was pretending to hold the position, using gestures, or practicing a role in advance. The distinction depends on reading the entire phrase rather than importing the familiar theatrical meaning of the isolated word.

A: Gesturing refers to physical movements, but the following phrase names a professional role rather than a bodily action.

B: Rehearsing means practicing for a later performance or event, whereas the passage states that Horace already held a highly paid managerial job.

D: Pretending would imply that the managerial position was a sham; the passage presents it as Horace’s actual paid employment.

Watch for: A familiar word can change meaning when followed by “as” and a job title; read the complete phrase.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

In planning a natural-history diorama, a designer first placed a small specimen at the center of a broad painted landscape. The specimen was accurate but visually overwhelmed. Raising it against an uncluttered patch of background gave it a more Underlined: commanding End underlined. presence without increasing its size or changing any of its anatomical details.

As used in the text, what does the word “commanding” most nearly mean?

Correct choice: prominent

The revision changes how readily the specimen draws the viewer’s attention. It becomes visually prominent rather than overwhelmed by the background. Commanding can suggest authority in other settings, but nothing here gives the specimen power to direct anyone or establishes its expertise. The explicit preservation of its size and anatomy directs attention to visual presentation, not physical growth or scientific revision.

A: Obedient describes compliance with directions; the passage evaluates how noticeable a displayed specimen becomes, not how it responds to commands.

B: Authoritative would suggest expertise or recognized authority, neither of which changes when the specimen is placed against a simpler background.

C: Compulsory means required or obligatory; the redesign changes visual emphasis without imposing any requirement on the specimen or its viewers.

Watch for: Test a secondary meaning against the specific quality that changed; nearby descriptions often rule out another plausible dictionary sense.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 4 of 27

The Pontcysyllte Aqueduct's iron arches rest on tall, slender masonry piers. Using iron made it possible to combine strength with relative lightness, helping its designers ______ an ambitious structural vision that the difficult terrain might otherwise have frustrated.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: realize

Here, a “vision” is a proposed design, and the relevant achievement is making that design possible despite difficult terrain. “Realize” can mean bring something into actual existence, not merely become aware of it. The material's combination of strength and lightness enabled construction. Surveying, disputing, or inheriting a vision would describe different relationships to the design and would not express the accomplishment emphasized by the sentence.

A: “Dispute” means challenge or argue against. The designers were pursuing the structural vision, rather than contesting its merits.

B: “Survey” means examine or assess. The material choice enabled the designers to achieve their vision, not merely inspect it.

D: “Inherit” means receive from a predecessor. The passage gives no account of an earlier designer passing down the vision.

Watch for: For familiar words with secondary meanings, substitute a brief definition into the sentence and test the surrounding cause-and-effect relationship.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

The competition asked its judges to be impartial, but it did not ask them to distribute their praise evenly. A judge could consider one composition far stronger than all the others without violating that expectation. What mattered was whether the judgment rested on the submitted music rather than on friendship with a composer, loyalty to a school, or hopes of receiving a favor later.

As used in the text, what does the word "impartial" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Unbiased

Impartial means unbiased: the judges should evaluate the music without favoritism based on personal connections or expected benefits. The text explicitly distinguishes impartiality from an even distribution of praise. A fair evaluation can strongly favor one work if the musical evidence supports that judgment. The contrast therefore rules out meanings involving indecision, lack of enthusiasm, or lack of information, none of which is required for a judgment to be free from bias.

A: The judges must assess the submitted music. Freedom from favoritism does not mean a lack of knowledge about the material.

B: The text allows judges to make strong comparative judgments, so the required quality is not an inability or unwillingness to decide.

D: A judge may praise a composition highly; the issue is the basis of that praise rather than its intensity.

Key takeaway: A context may define a word partly by rejecting a common misconception; use that contrast to sharpen the intended meaning.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 6 of 27

The following text is from Alice Meynell’s poem “The Two Poets,” included in her 1913 collection Poems.

Whose is the speech
That moves the voices of this lonely beech?
Out of the long west did this wild wind come—
O strong and silent! And the tree was dumb,
Ready and dumb, until
The dumb gale struck it on the darkened hill.

Two memories,
Two powers, two promises, two silences
Closed in this cry, closed in these thousand leaves
Articulate. This sudden hour retrieves
The purpose of the past,
Separate, apart—embraced, embraced at last.

What is the main purpose of the contrast between the first stanza’s silences and the second stanza’s “cry”?

Correct choice: To present expression as something created by the meeting of two previously separate forces.

Both the tree and the wind are initially described as unable to speak. When they meet, their two silences become an articulate cry, and the stanza ends by emphasizing their embrace. The contrast therefore presents expression as a joint creation. The poem does not describe resistance, rank the forces by effectiveness, or identify a sound the speaker previously knew and had lost.

A: The references to the past concern the separate forces’ fulfilled purpose, not the speaker’s mistaken belief that a familiar sound had disappeared.

C: Both forces contribute powers and promises to the cry; the passage does not make the tree’s contribution ineffective or merely temporary.

D: The tree is called ready before contact, and the final embrace suggests cooperation rather than a deliberate attempt to resist.

Watch for: Track what changes between stanzas and which participants cause that change; avoid inventing a conflict where the imagery suggests combination.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 7 of 27

The following text is from Pauline Hopkins’s 1900 novel Contending Forces. Dora discusses a room she has rented at her family’s lodging house.

“I know that it is not business to let a good-paying room go under our usual price, but she’s a ‘steady’; she has the best of references. Which choice best describes the function of the underlined portion in the text as a whole?

Correct choice: It playfully casts Dora as profit-driven after she has described accepting less rent from a reliable lodger.

Mercenary means chiefly motivated by money, but Dora has just described charging less than the usual price. Her remark playfully labels her conduct in a way that does not fit that concession. The gay laugh and affectionate dance reinforce the teasing tone. Her hope of recouping the difference later shows practical awareness, but it does not turn the reduced rent into evidence of complete indifference to lodgers.

A: Dora praises the references and the lodger’s reliability; she does not warn that those references conceal a threat to the household.

B: The price concession contradicts the claim of indifference to every lodger, and the laughter signals that the self-description is playful.

D: Dora’s fatigue disappears as she jokes and dances; nothing indicates an intention to leave the business or seek different employment.

Watch for: Compare a speaker’s self-description with the action just described, then use accompanying behavior to test whether the tone is ironic.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

Text 1

Research on antibacterial insect wings has inspired surfaces covered with tiny pillars. One proposed explanation is that contact stretches bacterial membranes until they rupture. If fewer bacteria grow on a pillar-covered surface, it may therefore seem reasonable to infer that the surface has destroyed cells by causing their contents to leak out.

Text 2

A study of titanium dioxide nanopillars found deformed or penetrated bacterial envelopes but no accompanying collapse indicating leakage of cell contents. Bacterial division was inhibited, and signs of oxidative stress increased. These results suggest that the antibacterial effects of such surfaces can involve processes beyond the rupture-and-leakage mechanism.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to Text 1’s final inference?

Correct choice: Reduced bacterial growth alone does not establish that the cells’ contents escaped through ruptured membranes.

Text 1 moves from reduced growth to a specific explanation: rupture and leakage. Text 2 reports reduced growth without the expected leakage and identifies other associated effects. These observations undermine the claim that growth reduction by itself proves rupture. They do not deny a role for physical contact, require the removal of surface features, or make comparisons with insect wings inherently uninformative.

B: Neither text says surface features must be removed before oxidative stress occurs; the stress was observed with the nanopillars present.

C: Penetration is itself a form of physical interaction, so the reported findings cannot rule out contact as an important contributor.

D: Text 2 qualifies a proposed mechanism using an artificial surface, but it does not declare all comparisons with insect wings scientifically useless.

Watch for: A result can support an effect without establishing one exclusive mechanism; identify exactly which inferential step the second text challenges.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 9 of 27

The following text is from Charles W. Chesnutt's 1899 collection The Conjure Woman. The narrator has bought a vineyard after Julius told him a story involving a curse.

I found, when I bought the vineyard, that Uncle Julius had occupied a cabin on the place for many years, and derived a respectable revenue from the product of the neglected grapevines. This, doubtless, accounted for his advice to me not to buy the vineyard, though whether it inspired the goopher story I am unable to state.

I believe, however, that the wages I paid him for his services as coachman, for I gave him employment in that capacity, were more than an equivalent for anything he lost by the sale of the vineyard.

Which statement most accurately characterizes the narrator's judgments in this excerpt?

Correct choice: He identifies a financial motive for Julius's advice but remains uncertain whether that motive also prompted the story.

The narrator treats Julius's income from the vineyard as an explanation for his advice, using doubtless to signal confidence. He explicitly withholds judgment about whether that same interest inspired the story. The later statement about wages adds another confident assessment, but it does not erase the uncertainty about the story's origin. The key preserves that difference in certainty rather than turning the narrator's suspicion into an established accusation.

A: The narrator states that Julius derived revenue from the vines and does not accept the story as evidence against buying.

B: The narrator does not claim that employment motivated the story, and he believes the wages more than compensated Julius's loss.

D: The narrator explicitly assigns a financial motive to the advice; the passage gives no evidence that the employment offer caused the story.

Watch for: Track confidence markers separately: doubtless, unable to state, and I believe do not place every judgment on the same footing.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 10 of 27

Researchers compared ventilation in mounds built by two termite species. In both, daily solar heating produced temperature differences that drove air movement. Yet the internal carbon dioxide patterns differed: the previously studied Asian mounds developed distinct layers that were overturned during a daily cycle, whereas the African mounds under study had relatively uniform carbon dioxide concentrations. The researchers related this difference to differences in mound structure and exposure to sunlight.

Which choice best states the main conclusion supported by the comparison of the two mound types?

Correct choice: A shared source of ventilation can operate alongside different patterns in the internal distribution of gases.

Both mound types use daily solar heating to drive air movement, but their carbon dioxide distributions are not the same. The central lesson is that a common driving process can coexist with different outcomes inside differently structured and exposed systems. The alternatives either erase the observed difference or reject the common mechanism that the passage explicitly establishes. Shared cause does not require identical spatial patterns.

B: The passage reports different, not similar, carbon dioxide distributions, and it associates the difference with variation in structure and exposure.

C: Daily solar heating is explicitly identified as a driver in both types; the distinction concerns gas distribution rather than the existence of that driver.

D: The passage states that temperature differences drive air movement, so structural variation cannot be read as preventing that influence altogether.

Watch for: Hold constant what the comparison says is shared, and vary only what it actually reports as different between the systems.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

An instrument maker's repair accounts list work on the decorated cases and internal mechanisms of several clocks. A historian argues that a celebrated renovation changed one clock's appearance without substantially improving its timekeeping. Because the account describes both gilding and replacement parts, the mere presence of either phrase cannot decide the question. The historian needs evidence about what the replacements actually did.

Which discovery would most strongly support the historian's interpretation of the renovation?

Correct choice: The replacements secured decorative panels, while the unchanged timing mechanism performed no better in recorded tests after renovation.

The historian distinguishes cosmetic change from improved timekeeping. Evidence that the replacements held decoration rather than altering the timing mechanism identifies the parts' function, and unchanged test performance supplies a direct check on the claimed lack of improvement. Together those findings address both ambiguities in the account. Expensive decoration or prominent display would fit cosmetic interest, but neither would rule out simultaneous functional improvement.

A: Display location and payment schedule concern ownership and purchasing, not the function of replacement parts or measured timekeeping performance.

C: Costly gilding supports attention to appearance, but without identifying the replacements it cannot exclude a simultaneous improvement to timekeeping.

D: A general recommendation for periodic adjustment does not show what this renovation changed or whether the clock's actual performance improved.

Watch for: For competing interpretations of a mixed record, seek evidence that identifies the disputed components and independently checks the claimed outcome.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 12 of 27

In a study of northern bald ibises, researchers examined wingbeat coordination within a flying formation. Birds can fly behind and to one side of another bird or directly behind it. The researchers' findings supported a position-dependent account of coordination: the birds did not use one unchanging spatial relationship between their wingbeats and those of the bird ahead.

Which finding would most directly support this position-dependent account of ibis wingbeat coordination?

Correct choice: Birds flying behind and to one side used spatially in-phase wingbeats, whereas directly trailing birds used spatially opposite-phase wingbeats.

The claim concerns a change in wingbeat coordination with position. The correct finding compares the two positions and reports different spatial phase relationships, directly establishing that coordination is not fixed across them. A result for only the side-behind position does not demonstrate a positional difference. Differences in wake exposure may help explain why coordination changes, but they do not themselves report that the birds actually change their wingbeat relationship.

B: Wingspan is not the wingbeat phase relationship at issue, and a stable individual wingspan does not show coordination changing with flight position.

C: This describes coordination in one position only. Without the directly trailing comparison, it does not establish that coordination varies by position.

D: Different wake exposure concerns the aerodynamic conditions birds encounter; it does not report a corresponding difference in their wingbeat coordination.

Watch for: Evidence about why a response might vary is different from evidence that it does vary; match the measured outcome to the claim.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 13 of 27

Researchers measured the concentration of a harmless dissolved tracer at two ocean sampling stations after a single release near Station A. They sampled both stations at the same four times. A student claims that the observations are consistent with the tracer plume reaching Station B later than Station A, while noting that the measurements alone do not establish the exact time of either station's true maximum concentration.

Tracer concentration after a single release

Which choice uses data from the graph to most directly support the student's claim?

Correct choice: The highest measured concentration at Station A occurred at 2 hours, whereas the highest measured concentration at Station B occurred at 4 hours.

Among the four sampled times, Station A has its highest concentration, 10 milligrams per liter, at 2 hours. Station B has its highest sampled concentration, 9 milligrams per liter, at 4 hours. That later sampled peak at B is consistent with the proposed delay. The wording appropriately limits the conclusion to measured concentrations: sampling at discrete times does not identify the exact moment of a peak between measurements or prove a transport mechanism by itself.

B: Equal initial values do not establish equal peak times; the highest sampled values occur at different later times.

C: Station A ended at 3, not its initial value of 2; the claim about returning to its initial concentration is inaccurate.

Key takeaway: Use measured peaks to support a timing comparison while preserving the distinction between sampled observations and an exact continuous maximum.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 14 of 27

Passenger pigeons once numbered in the billions, yet a genetic study found relatively low diversity in their genomes. One proposed explanation was historical population instability. Another was natural selection: when a favored variant spreads, diversity can decline in neighboring stretches of DNA that remain associated with it. Recombination breaks up those associations. The study found that diversity was especially low in genomic regions where recombination occurred less frequently.

What does the regional pattern of genetic diversity most reasonably suggest?

Correct choice: The association supports a contribution from selection even if population history also influenced the species’ overall level of genetic diversity.

Recombination breaks associations between a favored variant and neighboring DNA. Where it occurs less often, selection can therefore reduce diversity across neighboring regions more persistently. The observed regional pattern supports a contribution from selection. This does not require denying every influence of population history, but neither does it supply independent evidence that instability also acted. The key distinguishes allowing a possible additional cause from demonstrating that cause.

A: The proposed mechanism predicts a stronger neighboring effect of selection where recombination is less frequent, so this choice assigns the regional relationship backward.

C: The described role of recombination yields a specific prediction under selection, giving the regional pattern explanatory value beyond mere compatibility with either hypothesis.

D: Evidence fitting selection does not independently establish population instability; allowing both processes remains possible without making their combined action better supported.

Watch for: When hypotheses predict different spatial patterns, use the observed pattern to compare their fit without expanding the conclusion into universal causation.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 15 of 27

Three translators independently prepared versions of a short poem. All three rendered an unusual phrase as a reference to "borrowed daylight." A scholar initially regarded the agreement as strong evidence that the phrase had that meaning. She then discovered that each translator had consulted the same glossary, whose entry supplied "borrowed daylight" as its only interpretation. The glossary's author had marked the entry as an unresolved conjecture, but that note was omitted from the edition the translators used.

Which conclusion is most strongly supported by the text?

Correct choice: The translators' agreement may reflect a shared unqualified presentation of one conjecture rather than three independent grounds for accepting the interpretation.

The translators worked independently of one another, but their matching phrase may originate in a common source. That source presented a single interpretation without the uncertainty note attached by its author. The agreement therefore need not represent three separately developed reasons for accepting the meaning; it may repeat one conjecture three times. This changes the weight of the apparent corroboration, not necessarily the truth of the interpretation. The passage also does not establish who removed the note or any intent to mislead.

A: An uncertain conjecture can still be correct. The new information reduces the independence of the support without establishing an alternative meaning.

C: The passage does not identify who omitted the note or why. It also supplies no evidence that the author had rejected the conjecture.

D: Working separately does not ensure independent support when all three rely on the same entry. Their lack of mutual consultation does not remove that dependence.

Key takeaway: Independent work is not the same as independent evidence when separate judgments rely on the same underlying source.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 16 of 27

During the international rescue campaign of 1960–1980, organizers protected two celebrated Nubian monument complexes ______ and Philae. Relocating threatened structures preserved monuments that would otherwise have faced rising water.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: from flooding: Abu Simbel

The clause ending “from flooding” is complete: it identifies the organizers, their action, and its objects. A colon can then introduce the specific names that explain “two celebrated Nubian monument complexes.” The names form a phrase rather than an independent clause, so a semicolon or period would leave a fragment. Omitting punctuation also fails to mark how the names elaborate on the preceding complete statement.

B: Without a boundary, the names follow “from flooding” without a grammatical connection that identifies them as the promised two complexes.

C: The period turns “Abu Simbel and Philae” into a sentence fragment because the names lack a verb or complete predicate.

D: A semicolon requires an independent clause after it. “Abu Simbel and Philae” supplies only the names of the complexes.

Watch for: Before selecting a colon, verify that its introductory clause is complete; after it, an explanatory list may be only a phrase.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 17 of 27

The machinists, who had already balanced the rotating platform, ______ and then retested the equipment under a heavier load. Only after the second trial did they approve the platform for use in the gallery.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: tightened its bearings

The sentence uses the subject "The machinists" with two coordinated actions: they "tightened its bearings" and "then retested the equipment." The nonessential clause about balancing the platform is already enclosed in commas. Nothing further is needed inside the first verb-object unit or between these two parts of the compound predicate. Keeping "tightened its bearings" intact allows "and" to connect it directly to the second action performed by the same subject.

B: The semicolon wrongly separates the verb "tightened" from its direct object, "its bearings," and neither resulting segment supplies the needed structure.

C: A comma cannot be placed between the verb "tightened" and its direct object, "its bearings," in this structure.

D: The semicolon separates two verbs sharing the subject "machinists"; the material beginning "and then retested" is not an independent clause.

Key takeaway: Distinguish an interruption already enclosed in commas from the main predicate, and keep each verb with its required object.

In Muskauer Park, the extension of the designed landscape—achieved partly through green passages into the ______ essential to the relationship between the park and its setting. The town itself became part of the larger composition.

The main sentence is “the extension of the designed landscape was considered essential.” The words beginning “achieved partly” interrupt that sentence and open with a dash. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 20 of 27

Before standardized ballots, distinctive papers could make a voter’s political choice visible to observers. The use of uniform ballots printed by the government ______ to make that choice harder to identify from the paper alone.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: provides one way

The subject of the verb is use, a singular noun. The phrase of uniform ballots printed by the government modifies use; its plural noun ballots does not control agreement. Provides is the singular present-tense verb that completes the sentence. The other choices all require a plural subject in this construction, regardless of their differences in tense or aspect.

B: Have provided uses the plural auxiliary have; the singular subject use would require has if that tense were selected.

C: Provide is the present-tense form used with plural subjects; the nearby plural ballots does not control this sentence’s verb.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 21 of 27

Once bark has been harvested from a cork oak, ______ the tree to produce replacement bark. This capacity makes repeated harvesting possible without treating each harvest as the end of the tree's productive life.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: its cork-producing layer enables

The layer belongs to the single cork oak mentioned in the opening clause, so the possessive form “its” is required. “It's” is a contraction of “it is” or “it has,” not a possessive. “Their” does not agree with the singular tree. Finally, the subject of the main clause is the singular “layer,” which takes “enables.” The correct choice satisfies both ownership and grammatical agreement.

A: Although “its” correctly expresses possession, “enable” does not agree with the singular main-clause subject “layer,” which requires “enables.”

C: “Their” points to a plural antecedent, but the opening clause introduces only one cork oak whose layer is being described.

D: “It's” expands to “it is” or “it has.” Neither expansion can express the tree's possession of a cork-producing layer here.

Watch for: Read an apostrophe form as its expanded contraction; if that expansion fails, consider the possessive form and check its antecedent.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 22 of 27

The new field protocol requires observers to record the start of each spoken exchange, note any interruption from machinery, and ______ whether the participants repeat or rephrase words immediately after the interruption.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: assess

The protocol requires observers "to record," "note," and "assess." These are parallel base-form verbs sharing the initial "to." Each names an action the observers must perform. "Assess" therefore completes the series while accepting the following "whether" clause as its object. A noun phrase or an -ing form would change the grammatical structure of the third item and break the parallel sequence established by the first two verbs.

B: "Assessed" is a past-tense or past-participle form, not a base-form verb parallel to "record" and "note" after the shared "to."

C: "An assessment of" is a noun phrase, not a verb parallel to "record" and "note" in the required action sequence.

D: "Assessing" is an -ing form and does not match the base-form verbs sharing "to" at the start of the series.

Key takeaway: In a coordinated action series, identify the shared grammatical structure and preserve it through the final item.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 23 of 27

Abrasion on some woolly rhinoceros horns has been interpreted as evidence that the animals used their horns to brush snow away from vegetation. ______, features of their teeth and downward-directed heads support the view that they often fed close to the ground. These observations concern different structures but fit a broadly compatible account of feeding.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: In addition

The second sentence adds another line of anatomical evidence to the first. Horn wear and the structure of teeth and heads are different observations, but the final sentence explicitly calls their feeding implications compatible. In addition therefore fits the relationship. The passage does not present a contradiction, a concession despite a difficulty, or a sequence in which one finding remained true only until a later event.

A: Until then would establish a time boundary, but no event is supplied that ends the relevance of either line of anatomical evidence.

B: Even so would introduce a concession despite a preceding obstacle; the horn evidence is not presented as an obstacle to the next interpretation.

D: By contrast would emphasize an opposition, but the final sentence explicitly says the observations fit a compatible account of feeding.

Watch for: Use the author’s final assessment of two observations to decide whether their relationship is cumulative, opposing, or merely chronological.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 24 of 27

In Roman marine concrete, seawater reacted with lime and volcanic ash to help cement the mixture together. ______, seawater was not merely an external condition the concrete endured; it participated in the chemical process that bound the structure.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: Thus

The first sentence describes seawater participating in chemical reactions that cement the mixture. The second draws out the implication: seawater was an active participant in forming the structure, not just part of its surroundings. Thus signals this inferential movement. The second sentence is broader than the specific reaction, so it is not a narrower example or particular case of the first.

A: By contrast would oppose the two statements, but the conclusion follows directly from the chemical participation described in the first.

B: For instance introduces an example; the second sentence instead interprets the significance of the example already supplied in the first.

C: In particular usually introduces a more specific case, but the second sentence broadens the reaction into a general conclusion about seawater.

Watch for: Distinguish a concrete process from the conclusion drawn from it; an inference may restate the process at a broader level.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 25 of 27

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • In force mode, a Kibble balance uses electromagnetic force to balance a test weight and records the required current.

Watch for: A phrase beginning “by the time” establishes a reference point; choose the tense that places the other action correctly relative to it.

Little Sun is a portable solar lamp designed for use in places with limited access to electricity. Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: In other words

The final sentence restates the significance of the preceding pricing arrangement: purchases in one market help make the product accessible in another. In other words signals that interpretive restatement. Although the project operates in multiple places, the relationship here is not primarily temporal. Nor does the last sentence oppose the preceding information or announce that the arrangement has never existed before.

B: At the same time emphasizes simultaneity or an additional point; the sentence instead reformulates the purpose of the already described sales.

C: For the first time makes a chronological claim of novelty that the passage neither supplies nor needs to explain the pricing relationship.

D: On the other hand signals a contrasting consideration, but supporting access elsewhere is precisely the function assigned to the wealthier-market purchases.

Correct choice: In short

The paragraph has already given the concrete case, so the final sentence does not introduce an example. Nor does it establish a separate comparison or a previous stage in the decision.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 24 of 27

Strong winds can push water toward one end of an enclosed basin. Once the wind stops, the displaced water can rebound and continue oscillating back and forth, producing a seiche. ______, the continued motion of the water does not establish that the wind that initiated it is still blowing.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: Therefore

The explanation states that oscillation can continue after the initiating wind ceases. The final sentence draws the resulting limitation on inference: observing ongoing water movement does not prove ongoing wind. Therefore is therefore appropriate. The sentence is not a contradiction, a separate simultaneous event, or a particular instance of a seiche; it states a general conclusion about what the observation can establish.

A: The sentence names no particular basin, date, or event; it expresses a general inference rather than a concrete example of the phenomenon.

B: At the same time would suggest an additional concurrent fact, but the sentence derives a logical conclusion from the process already described.

D: The final statement does not oppose the explanation; it draws exactly the limitation on inference that continued oscillation after the wind stops entails.

Watch for: An effect that persists after its trigger ends cannot prove that the trigger is still present; choose a transition marking that inference.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 25 of 27

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Mosi-oa-Tunya is a waterfall on the Zambezi River.
  • The river is over two kilometres wide at the falls.
  • The water plunges into basalt gorges.
  • The falls produce a large plume of spray or mist.
  • The spray can be visible from more than 20 kilometres away.

The student wants to explain the connection between the falling river and the mist around Mosi-oa-Tunya. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct choice: The Zambezi's plunge into basalt gorges produces spray that rises around the falls and can be visible far away.

The goal asks for a causal connection between the falling water and the mist. The correct choice connects the river's plunge with the production of spray and its visibility. Width and rock type are relevant descriptive facts but do not by themselves explain the mist. The notes do not establish downstream channel width or claim that mist carved the gorges, so those proposed causal additions are unsupported.

A: The notes say the falling water produces spray; they do not say that mist formed the basalt gorges by settling on their walls.

B: The width and the presence of basalt gorges describe the site, but the sentence omits how the falling river produces the surrounding mist.

C: Visibility of spray does not establish downstream river width. The notes specify width only at the falls and provide no such inference.

Watch for: A causal synthesis should follow the direction of the relationship stated in the notes; do not invent a cause from neighboring descriptive facts.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 26 of 27

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Thylacines were carnivorous marsupials.
  • Their young initially developed in the mother’s pouch.
  • After leaving the pouch, young remained in a lair while the mother hunted.
  • Only one successful captive breeding event is documented: at Melbourne Zoo in 1899.
  • The last known thylacine died in 1936.

The student wants to emphasize how limited documented success in captive thylacine breeding was. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct choice: The only documented successful captive breeding of thylacines occurred at Melbourne Zoo in 1899, illustrating the rarity of such success.

The goal concerns the limited extent of documented captive breeding success. The notes identify just one successful event, at Melbourne Zoo in 1899, and the correct choice states that directly. Development in the pouch is accurate background but misses the goal. The notes do not compare overall reproductive success in captivity and the wild, nor do they support claiming that every captive attempt failed or that failure alone caused extinction.

A: The documented successful event contradicts every attempt failed, and the notes do not establish captive breeding failure as the sole cause of extinction.

B: A single documented event does not establish higher reproductive success in captivity than in the wild; the notes provide no such comparison.

D: This choice summarizes juvenile development rather than the scarcity of successful captive breeding events, so it does not address the specified emphasis.

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • It makes concentrated tea extract rather than a full pot of ready-to-drink tea.

The student wants to explain the infuser’s small size by relating it to how the object was intended to be used. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

The notes connect small size to a distinct function: the object makes concentrated extract, which is diluted separately in a cup. It therefore need not hold the full volume of a conventional pot of ready-to-drink tea. The correct choice expresses this functional explanation. The other options describe materials, geometry, date, or pouring, but they do not connect the small capacity to the intended preparation method.

A: The date and geometric reinterpretation concern the object’s artistic design, not the preparation method that accounts for its small scale.

B: The statement recognizes the small size and a practical handle, but it never links size to making concentrated extract for later dilution.

C: Materials, geometry, and handle placement describe design features but do not explain why the infuser needs less capacity than a conventional teapot.
