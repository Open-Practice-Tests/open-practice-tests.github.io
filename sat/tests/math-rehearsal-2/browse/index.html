The model V(t) = 360 − 15t gives liters of water remaining after t minutes. What does 15 represent?

Answer: B / The decrease in water volume, in liters per minute. The coefficient of time is −15, so the tank loses 15 liters each minute. The constant 360 is the initial volume.

Answer: B / 6.5 With six values, average the third and fourth: (5 + 8)/2 = 6.5. The mean would use all observations and answer a different question.

Math · Module 1 · Question 14 of 22

A jar contains 8 red, 5 blue, and 7 white beads. One bead is selected at random. What is the probability that it is not blue?

Answer: D / 3/4 There are 20 beads, of which 8 + 7 = 15 are not blue. The probability is 15/20 = 3/4. Equivalently, use 1 − 5/20.

Math · Module 1 · Question 20 of 22

A random sample of 400 shoppers contains 118 who prefer paper bags. What is the best estimate of the number preferring paper bags among 12,000 comparable shoppers?

Answer: A / 3540 The sample fraction is 118/400 = 0.295. Applying it to 12,000 gives 3540. This is a population estimate, not an exact enumeration.

Math · Module 1 · Question 21 of 22

For f (x) = mx + b, f (4) = 17 and f (9) = 32. What is f (0)?

Answer: A / 5 The slope is (32 − 17)/(9 − 4) = 3. Then 17 = 3(4) + b, so b = 5 = f (0).

Answer: 1 Equating outputs gives x2 − 6x + 10 − c = 0. Tangency requires 36 − 4(10 − c) = 0, so 4c − 4 = 0 and c = 1. At this value the intersection equation is (x − 3)2 = 0.

Which point satisfies 2x + 3y ≤ 12 and y > 1?

Answer: C / (0, 4) At (0, 4), the first left side is 12 and the second condition is 4 > 1. Each other choice either makes the first total too large or has y = 1, which fails the strict condition.

Math · Module 2 · Question 17 of 22

Two random samples from the same population are used to estimate a proportion using the same confidence level and sampling method. One has 100 observations; the other has 900. Which is generally expected?

Answer: D / The larger sample has a smaller margin of error. Other features held comparable, a larger random sample generally reduces sampling uncertainty. It does not remove bias automatically or guarantee that the two estimates are identical.

Math · Module 2 · Question 19 of 22

For a positive integer k, the equation x2 − (k + 1)x + 4k = 0 has two distinct real solutions. What is the least possible value of k?

Answer: D / 14 The discriminant must be positive: (k + 1)2 − 16k = (k − 7)2 − 48 > 0. For integers 1 ≤ k ≤ 13, (k − 7)2 ≤ 36, so none work. At k = 14, the discriminant is 49 − 48 = 1 > 0. Thus 14 is the least allowed integer.

Math · Module 2 · Question 21 of 22

The graphs y = x2 − 6x + 11 and y = 2x − 5 intersect. What is the y-coordinate of their intersection?

Answer: A / 3 Equating gives x2 − 8x + 16 = (x − 4)2 = 0. The unique input is 4. Substitute into the line: y = 2(4) − 5 = 3. The question asks for the output, not the repeated root.

Math · Module 2 · Question 22 of 22

A line passes through (2, 7) and (8, 25). Its equation is ax − 2y = c. What is a + c?

Enter an integer, decimal, or fraction. Up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus. No units or mixed numbers.

Answer: 4 The slope is (25 − 7)/(8 − 2) = 3, so the line is y = 3x + 1. Multiply by 2: 2y = 6x + 2, or 6x − 2y = −2. Thus a = 6, c = −2, and a + c = 4.
