A right triangle has legs of lengths 18 and 12. A rectangle lies inside the triangle with two sides along the legs and its opposite vertex on the hypotenuse, as shown. The rectangle's horizontal side is twice its vertical side. What is its area?

Answer: B. 2592/49 Let the vertical side be h and the horizontal side be 2h. The hypotenuse joins (18, 0) and (0, 12), so a point on it satisfies x/18 + y/12 = 1. Thus 2h/18 + h/12 = 1, giving 7h/36 = 1 and h = 36/7. The area is 2h2 = 2592/49.

Math · Module 2 · Question 19 of 22

The linear function g(x) = mx + b has m < 0 and satisfies g(g(x)) = 9x + 8 for every real x. What is g(5)?

Answer: D. −19 Composition gives g(g(x)) = m2x + b(m + 1). Matching coefficients yields m2 = 9 and b(m + 1) = 8. Because m < 0, m = −3, so −2b = 8 and b = −4. Then g(5) = −3(5) − 4 = −19.

The line y = 2x + b is tangent to the circle (x − 2)2 + (y + 1)2 = 20. There are two possible values of b. What is their sum?

Enter an integer, decimal, or fraction. Up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus. No units or mixed numbers.

Answer: −10 Substitute y = 2x + b into the circle: (x − 2)2 + (2x + b + 1)2 = 20. This becomes 5x2 + 4bx + b2 + 2b − 15 = 0. Tangency requires discriminant zero: 16b2 − 20(b2 + 2b − 15) = 0, or b2 + 10b − 75 = 0. The two values are 5 and −15, whose sum is −10.

Watch for: There are two parallel tangent lines; retain both possible intercepts.

Math · Module 2 · Question 22 of 22

A quadratic function f has the following properties. The line y = 3x + 2 meets its graph only at the point with x-coordinate 1, and the line y = −x + 10 meets its graph only at the point with x-coordinate 3. What is f (5)?

Answer: C. 1 Let the leading coefficient be a ≠ 0. A single intersection at x = 1 means f (x) − (3x + 2) = a(x − 1)2, so the linear coefficient of f is 3 − 2a. The other tangency gives f (x) − (−x + 10) = a(x − 3)2, so the same coefficient is −1 − 6a. Equate them to get a = −1. The first identity then gives f (x) = −x2 + 5x + 1, so f (5) = 1.

Watch for: A single intersection with a quadratic means a double root of the difference, not merely a point that lies on both graphs.
