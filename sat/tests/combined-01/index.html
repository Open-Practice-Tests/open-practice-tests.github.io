Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

At Shushtar, a network of diversion canals carried river water to mills and farmland. The system's builders therefore did more than redirect a river: they ______ its flow, using moving water to serve practical purposes.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: harnessed

The phrase “using moving water to serve practical purposes” directly explains the missing word. To harness a natural force is to put it to useful work. Supplying mills and farmland exemplifies that meaning. The builders did redirect water, but the sentence emphasizes what the redirected water accomplished. “Harnessed” captures this purposeful use without adding a claim about concealing, copying, or challenging the river's movement.

A: “Concealed” would mean hid the flow from view. The passage emphasizes its usefulness, not whether it remained visible.

B: “Questioned” describes challenging a claim or expressing doubt, neither of which explains supplying water to mills and fields.

D: “Imitated” would mean copied the flow. The builders used actual river water rather than producing a resemblance to it.

Watch for: An explanation immediately after a blank can define the intended meaning more directly than the passage's broader historical context.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

As used in the text, what does the word “checked” most nearly mean?

The number of cells stopped increasing but later increased again after the nutrient returned. Checked therefore means halted or restrained: growth was stopped, apparently reversibly. The familiar meaning examined is tempting, but nutrient removal changes the growth process rather than inspecting it. Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: ostensibly

Choice C is correct because "ostensibly" describes how something is presented or appears, with the possibility that this appearance does not capture its underlying purpose. Engineering is the tour's advertised subject, and the guide does discuss technical features. Yet the selection of examples serves a political argument, so calling the tour simply technical would overlook that purpose. The correct word preserves the distinction between the tour's outward framing and what that framing is being used to accomplish.

A: "Unequivocally" would deny the qualification the passage develops: technical information is being selected to advance a political argument.

Key takeaway: Distinguish appearance from time and importance. A description can be outwardly accurate while still concealing an additional purpose.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

An automatic shuttle counter records how many times a weaver passes the shuttle across a loom. It does not detect whether a thread has broken or whether the emerging pattern matches the plan. Installing the counter therefore does not ______ the need for inspection; it replaces a repetitive counting task while leaving judgments about the cloth's condition to the weaver.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: obviate

The counter automates counting, but it does not perform the inspections needed to detect broken threads or pattern errors. Thus installing it does not eliminate the need for inspection. "Obviate" means make unnecessary, which fits the contrast between replacing one repetitive task and retaining a different judgment-based task. The passage is not saying that inspection becomes harder, more understandable, or merely postponed; it remains necessary because the counter does not address its purposes.

A: The need for inspection remains, but the text does not say it becomes stronger or more demanding after the counter is installed.

B: The passage specifies what inspection checks, but it does not characterize installation as making that need clearer.

Key takeaway: When a device replaces one task but cannot perform another, distinguish eliminating a need from changing its timing or intensity.

The opening describes trails that look faint from a human viewpoint. The second sentence states that the animals using them can see them readily, and the final sentence makes that difference understandable by inviting a change in eye level and scale. The structure therefore moves from an initial appearance to an explanation of why the same trails appear different to smaller creatures.

TEXT 1

A revival of a century-old satire should replace obscure references to politicians with references its audience can recognize, argues director Hana Lott. The original audience understood whom the jokes targeted; preserving the jokes' function therefore requires changing some of their words. Without that recognition, the play risks becoming a sequence of unexplained names.

TEXT 2

Dramaturg Emre Vale opposes replacing the names in historical satires. A program note can supply the knowledge audiences need to grasp a joke's target while also showing how satire operated in a particular political setting. Replacing the names may preserve immediate intelligibility at the cost of obscuring that setting.

Which statement would both Lott and Vale most likely agree with?

Correct choice: Audiences need some understanding of whom a satire targets to appreciate the point of its jokes.

Choice A is correct because both commentators treat audience recognition of the jokes' targets as necessary to understanding their point. Lott would supply that recognition by replacing unfamiliar names with familiar ones. Vale would supply it through program notes while preserving the historical names. Their disagreement concerns how to make the targets intelligible and which costs to accept, not whether intelligibility matters. The common ground is therefore the audience's need for relevant knowledge, rather than either commentator's preferred method of providing it.

D: Vale endorses program notes, but neither text compares the methods' effectiveness, and Lott advocates changing the dialogue.

Key takeaway: When authors disagree about a method, look for the shared objective that makes their disagreement meaningful rather than choosing either preferred method.

The key combines those facts without ranking positive and negative appearances or treating contrast as more important than subject detail. After a transit agency revised its emergency announcements, listeners recalled the required action more accurately. Which finding, if true, would most directly weaken Iven's explanation relative to the alternative?

Correct choice: In controlled trials, moving the action first improved recall without shortening messages, whereas shortening messages with the action still last produced no improvement.

The trial separates the changes that occurred together in the agency's revision. Moving the action first improves recall even when duration is unchanged, while shortening alone fails to improve recall when the action remains last. This pattern directly favors action placement over reduced duration as the explanation for the observed gain. The other findings describe the revised messages or their length without comparing the two proposed causes independently.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 15 of 27

In a study of petal tissue, researchers permanently tagged every molecule of pigment P present at the start of an experiment. The tag could not separate from a molecule while that molecule remained chemically intact. The isolated tissue received no pigment P from outside, and its color remained unchanged throughout the experiment. At the end, researchers detected some untagged molecules of P, although the total amount of P was the same as at the start.

Which conclusion is most strongly supported by the text?

Correct choice: The tissue produced some pigment P during the experiment despite showing no net increase in its total amount.

All P molecules present at the start were tagged. A molecule cannot simply lose its tag while remaining intact, and no P enters from outside. The later untagged P therefore must have been produced during the experiment. Its appearance despite an unchanged total shows that new production can occur without a net increase in the pigment pool. The result does not establish a rate of production or show that every original molecule was replaced.

A: The tag cannot separate while a molecule remains chemically intact. This proposed source of untagged P contradicts the experiment's stated condition.

B: The observations concern total amount and visible color, not the survival of each original molecule. Untagged molecules indicate that the pigment pool changed.

C: The passage explicitly excludes pigment entering from outside. The explanation must account for new untagged molecules within the isolated tissue.

Key takeaway: An unchanged total can conceal turnover: evidence of newly produced components does not require an increase in the size of the whole pool.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 23 of 27

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The sentence contrasts what one observation can establish with what repeated observations can establish. By contrast marks that difference in informational value.
