The coronagraph designed for the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope uses masks and flexible mirrors to reduce a star's glare. By Underlined: suppressing End underlined. that overwhelming light, the instrument can make nearby, much fainter planets detectable.

As used in the text, what does the word “suppressing” most nearly mean?

Correct choice: reducing

The instrument reduces the glare that makes a planet difficult to detect. Thus, suppressing means reducing the strength of the unwanted light. The surrounding description states the effect directly: less overwhelming starlight makes a faint planet detectable. The task is to identify that local meaning, without supplying a particular optical mechanism or confusing the instrument's action with a scientist's later analysis of its measurements.

A: Explaining light would give an account of its behavior; the passage instead describes physically diminishing glare so planets become visible.

C: Redirecting specifies a change in the light's path, but the stated outcome concerns its reduced strength rather than its direction.

Watch for: Use the stated result to define an unfamiliar verb; avoid replacing a broad effect with an unstated mechanism.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

Before a set of carved wooden instruments arrived at the museum, conservator Elian Voss tested the humidity controls in every display case. He also arranged for spare sensors to be kept nearby. These preparations were intended to forestall damage: a problem discovered after a crack appeared would be a problem discovered too late. Voss regarded the uneventful opening month as a success, although visitors had no reason to notice his work.

As used in the text, what does the word "forestall" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Prevent

Forestall means prevent in this context. Voss checks the cases and stores spare sensors before the instruments arrive, and he considers waiting until a crack appears to be too late. His objective is therefore to keep damage from occurring in the first place. The uneventful month confirms the kind of success he seeks: no visible problem arises. The word describes the purpose of the preparations rather than the technical steps they involve.

A: The text distinguishes these advance preparations from action taken after a crack appears; repairing would address existing damage.

C: Testing the controls is part of the preparation, but the stated purpose is stopping future damage, not measuring damage that exists.

D: Visitors do not notice Voss's work because damage has been avoided, not because he hides damage that has already occurred.

Key takeaway: Use the sequence of events to distinguish preventing a problem from inspecting, concealing, or correcting an existing one.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 4 of 27

The logistics team nevertheless declined to call the design reliable. A single trial conducted during light traffic, they noted, could reveal promise without showing how the system would perform during the busiest weeks.

As used in the text, what does the word "auspicious" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Promising

The trial has favorable results, making the design look promising. The team's caution clarifies the degree of that judgment: "auspicious" does not mean conclusive, because light traffic and a single observation leave reliability unestablished. The passage even distinguishes revealing "promise" from showing performance during busy periods. The target word therefore expresses a favorable beginning or outlook while leaving room for later testing and possible difficulty.

B: The passage does not state what outcome the team expected. A favorable beginning need not be surprising to be auspicious.

C: The team explicitly refuses to treat one favorable trial as proof of reliability. Its outcome suggests promise rather than settles the question.

D: Routine would describe an ordinary or regularly repeated event. The text instead evaluates the favorable implications of a first trial.

Key takeaway: Preserve the strength of a contextual judgment: an encouraging result can be promising without being conclusive or unexpected.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

Early scholarship treated the patterned endpapers in inexpensive books as a ______ feature: attractive packaging with little bearing on how readers interpreted the works. Historian Leena Das argues, however, that repeated visual motifs on those pages prepared readers to recognize themes developed in the main text. For Das, the endpapers belong within an account of the books' meaning, not merely their manufacture.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: peripheral

The early scholars considered the endpapers decorative packaging with "little bearing" on interpretation. A peripheral feature is one considered secondary or outside the central concern. Das challenges that assessment by connecting the visual motifs to themes in the main text. The contrast is therefore between a feature treated as peripheral to meaning and a feature that should be included in an interpretation. The word need not describe the endpapers' physical position at the ends of a book.

A: Persistent would describe the feature's continued presence over time. The relevant contrast concerns interpretive importance, not how long patterned endpapers remained common.

B: A unifying feature would help connect elements of a work. That resembles Das's position, whereas the blank describes the earlier view she challenges.

C: The passage contrasts an earlier judgment of limited importance with Das's account of significance. It does not say the earlier scholars disagreed among themselves.

Key takeaway: Identify whose judgment the blank expresses; a word that fits the later argument may contradict the earlier position being summarized.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

TEXT 1

Music scholar Neri Hall argues that early contour notation left performers considerable freedom. Because the marks showed whether a melody rose or fell without specifying every interval, a singer could realize the same sequence of marks in several distinct melodies. The notation itself did not select just one of them.

TEXT 2

Scholar Davi Sen cautions against inferring actual performance freedom directly from what a notation omits. A singer who had learned a melody orally might use contour marks simply to retrieve it. Several melodies could fit the marks in principle, while only one would fit the musical tradition in which the marks were used.

Which choice best describes how Sen's text relates to Hall's text?

Correct choice: It distinguishes possibilities allowed by the written signs alone from possibilities available to performers within a particular tradition.

Hall moves from what the marks specify to the freedom a performer has. Sen questions that move by introducing information supplied through oral learning. The signs alone may fit several melodies, while the performer's tradition may identify only one as appropriate. Sen can accept the notational omissions without accepting Hall's conclusion about performance freedom. The signs may still help retrieve the known melody; neither the oral knowledge nor the written notation must become useless.

B: Sen accepts the notational omissions but questions the inference of performance freedom; this choice wrongly attributes Hall's conclusion to Sen.

C: Sen gives the contour signs a retrieval function for a melody learned orally. Supplying omitted information through memory does not make the signs unnecessary.

D: Several melodies can fit the signs in principle regardless of what a singer remembers; oral learning constrains practical choices, not those formal possibilities.

Key takeaway: When a second text introduces context, ask which inference from the first text becomes less secure because of that context.

Which finding, if true, would most directly weaken Roe's interpretation?

Choice D is correct because it shows that the grooves would direct water into a closed depression rather than carry it off the working surface. That geometry conflicts with Roe's proposed function of keeping the surfaces dry through drainage. The finding need not prove the wooden-frame explanation to weaken the drainage explanation: demonstrating that the channels lack an outlet directly challenges the mechanism Roe proposes. Evidence that fish processing occurred nearby would not resolve this structural problem.

A: Heavy rainfall could increase the usefulness of drainage. It does not show that these particular grooves could not have served that purpose.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 14 of 27

The following poem is Gerard Manley Hopkins’s “Spring and Fall: to a young child,” first published in Poems (1918).

MARGARET, are you grieving
Over Goldengrove unleaving?
Leaves, like the things of man, you
With your fresh thoughts care for, can you?
Ah! as the heart grows older
It will come to such sights colder
By and by, nor spare a sigh
Though worlds of wanwood leafmeal lie;
And yet you will weep and know why.
Now no matter, child, the name:
Sorrow's springs are the same.
Nor mouth had, no nor mind, expressed
What heart heard of, ghost guessed:
It is the blight man was born for,
It is Margaret you mourn for.

What does the speaker imply about the relationship between Margaret’s present and future sorrow?

Correct choice: Its immediate occasion may change while its underlying source remains the same.

The speaker predicts that Margaret will become less moved by falling leaves but will still weep and eventually understand why. The assertion that sorrow’s “springs are the same” connects her present grief to the human vulnerability identified in the final lines. The occasion of sadness may change as she matures, while its underlying source persists. This is a development in understanding, not the disappearance of sorrow.

A: “And yet you will weep” explicitly rules out sorrow’s disappearance, even though the sight of falling leaves will affect her less.

C: The poem predicts a colder response to the landscape, not a strengthening attachment, and does not describe steadily increasing emotional intensity.

D: The speaker says her mouth and mind have not expressed the deeper source of grief; later she will “know why,” reversing this choice.

Watch for: Track what a poem says will change and what will persist. Emotional understanding can develop even as immediate reactions diminish.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 15 of 27

Pigment granules in bird feathers occur in elongated and rounded forms associated with different colors. In a fossil feather, elongated granules make up a larger share of the surviving mixture near the tip than near the base. Burial can remove granules, but experiments indicate that it cannot convert either form into the other. The researchers assume that, for each granule type, the fraction surviving burial was the same at the tip and base. They do not know, however, whether elongated and rounded granules survived at equal rates.

Given the researchers’ assumption about preservation, which conclusion about the fossil feather is best supported?

Correct choice: The original mixture of granule types probably differed between tip and base, although the size of that difference remains uncertain.

The same preservation bias operates at the tip and base. Consequently, that bias could alter the proportions of granule types but would not produce different proportions from originally identical mixtures. The preserved contrast therefore supports an original regional difference. Unknown survival rates still prevent recovery of its original magnitude. The key separates evidence that a difference existed from knowledge of how large it was or whether preservation amplified it.

B: If identical mixtures underwent the same type-specific survival bias in both regions, unequal survival alone would not create different mixtures between those regions.

C: Preferential loss can change proportions, but the passage does not establish that it necessarily amplifies rather than reduces the observed regional contrast.

D: Precise rates are needed to reconstruct original proportions, but the shared bias already permits an inference that the original regional mixtures differed.

Watch for: Separate evidence for the existence of a difference from evidence for its magnitude; preservation can support one without the other.

The example of advection fog establishes that fog can develop without calm air or clear skies. Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 22 of 27

Kelp obtains energy through photosynthesis, a process that requires light. Sunlight becomes less available with increasing water depth. ______, kelp forests generally occupy relatively shallow coastal waters, where enough light can reach the algae.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: As a result

“As a result” introduces a consequence of the two preceding facts. Kelp needs light, and deeper water receives less of it; its concentration in relatively shallow water follows from that combination. The final sentence therefore continues the causal explanation. It does not introduce an incidental topic, describe a simultaneous event, or contradict the earlier account of kelp’s dependence on light.

A: “On the contrary” introduces a correction or contradiction, whereas shallow habitat is consistent with, and explained by, the need for light.

B: “By the way” marks an incidental aside, but the location of kelp forests is the direct result of the preceding light requirements.

C: “In the meantime” signals events occurring during the same period; the paragraph establishes a causal relationship rather than a sequence of events.

Watch for: When the next sentence describes a consequence of stated conditions, choose a causal transition even if no event happens later.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 23 of 27

In black-figure vase painting, artists applied a slip that fired black to the figures, leaving the surrounding clay exposed. ______, in red-figure painting, the figures retained the clay's color while the slip-covered background became black. The placement of the dark surface was reversed.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: By contrast

The second technique reverses which region receives the black-firing slip: figures in one case, background in the other. By contrast signals that opposition. The final sentence confirms the reversal. The second technique is neither an example of the first, an outcome caused by it, nor a more precise description of it; it arranges the visual components differently.

A: More precisely would introduce a more exact description of the same technique. Red-figure painting instead reverses the treatment of figures and background, so it is a contrasting technique rather than a refinement of the first description.

C: For example would make red-figure painting an instance of black-figure painting, but the techniques place the dark slip in opposite areas.

D: In consequence would claim that the second placement results from the first; the passage describes a contrast rather than a causal sequence.

Watch for: When the same components exchange roles, distinguish a contrast between methods from a clarification of one method.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 24 of 27

A humpback whale’s bubble net concentrates prey in the water before the whale takes a mouthful. ______, its baleen acts on water already taken into the mouth, allowing water to escape while retaining prey. The two structures therefore assist different stages of feeding.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: By comparison

“By comparison” introduces a contrast between two mechanisms serving different feeding stages. The bubble net concentrates prey before intake, whereas baleen separates prey from water after intake. The final sentence makes that distinction explicit. The baleen’s action is neither an example of bubble-net concentration nor a consequence caused by the bubble net, and the sentence adds a second mechanism rather than summarizing the first.

A: “In consequence” would make baleen’s filtering action a result of bubble-net formation, but the paragraph compares separate roles rather than claiming that dependence.

C: “In summary” signals restatement, but the sentence introduces a new structure and function that were not supplied in the preceding sentence.

D: “For instance” would treat baleen as an example of the preceding bubble-net action, even though the two act at different stages.

Watch for: Compare the operations, not just their shared purpose. Two mechanisms can both aid feeding without one exemplifying or causing the other.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 25 of 27

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • The Panama Canal's original locks use gates made of two leaves.
  • Closed leaves meet in a V pointing toward the higher water.
  • Pressure from that water pushes the meeting leaves firmly together.
  • Water levels on both sides must be equalized before the gates open.
  • Lock chambers raise or lower vessels by changing the water level.

The student wants to explain why water pressure helps keep the original Panama Canal lock gates closed. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct choice: The gates' upstream-pointing V allows pressure from higher water to press their leaves together, reinforcing the closure.

The student's goal concerns how pressure contributes to closure, so the answer must connect the gates' shape and orientation to the force exerted by water. The correct choice makes that causal connection. The rule about equalizing levels before opening is relevant to operation but does not explain the seal by itself. Listing chambers and leaves also omits the mechanism, while describing unequal-level opening contradicts the notes.

A: This accurately combines shape and the condition for opening, but it does not explain how pressure acts on the leaves to strengthen closure.

B: The notes require equal water levels before opening. The proposed mechanism incorrectly places the leaves open while unequal levels exert pressure.

D: The number of leaves and the chambers' general purpose do not show why pressure helps keep a particular gate closed.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 26 of 27

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • A team compared two methods for reducing reverberation in a large room.
  • Method P added porous panels to the walls.
  • Method R suspended rigid reflectors near the ceiling.
  • For speech, Method P produced the larger improvement in listener comprehension.
  • For unamplified music, listeners preferred Method R because it preserved more of the room's resonant sound.
  • The team did not identify one method as best for every use of the room.

The student wants to use the comparison to challenge the assumption that the treatment best for spoken communication must also be preferred for music. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct choice: Method P improved speech comprehension more, whereas Method R was preferred for music because it retained more resonance, showing that the preferred treatment depended on the room's use.

The assumption links being best for speech to also being preferred for music. The correct choice supplies the relevant divergence: P yields the larger speech-comprehension gain, while R is preferred for music for its retained resonance. It then draws the supported conclusion that the preferred treatment depends on use. This challenges the assumed necessary connection without claiming that speech improvements always harm music or that one treatment is globally best for a multipurpose room.

B: The notes explicitly identify P as producing the larger speech-comprehension improvement, so this statement contradicts the comparison.

C: A preference for music does not establish overall superiority for a room used for both purposes; the team declined that universal ranking.

D: The study reports different preferences for these methods; it does not establish a necessary tradeoff for every possible improvement in speech comprehension.

Key takeaway: To challenge a necessary connection, provide the relevant counterexample without turning it into a different unsupported universal claim.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 27 of 27

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Engineers developed a machine that removes dry husks from seed pods using two rotating rollers.
  • Smooth rollers allowed some pods to slip through without being opened.
  • Notched rollers gripped the dry pods more firmly, reducing the number that passed through unopened.
  • When pods were wet, material accumulated in the notches and interrupted operation.
  • The engineers recommended the notched design for dry pods but continued testing other surfaces for use with wet pods.

The student wants to explain an advantage of the notched rollers while acknowledging a condition that limits their usefulness. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct choice: Notched rollers open dry pods more reliably than smooth rollers by gripping them more firmly, but wet material can collect in the notches and interrupt operation.

The correct sentence explains the mechanism behind the advantage: notches grip dry pods more firmly, so the machine opens them more reliably. It also states the relevant limit: wet material accumulates in the notches and can interrupt operation. The condition "dry" is essential because the notes do not support recommending the notched design for all pods. A response that gives only the successful result or only the wet-pod problem leaves out one of the goal's two required elements.

A: This describes the limitation and response but does not explain the advantage of notched rollers for dry pods.

B: This explains the advantage accurately but omits the limiting condition involving wet pods, which the goal explicitly requires.

C: The recommendation extends only to dry pods. Including wet pods contradicts the stated problem and the engineers' continued testing.

Key takeaway: When asked for an advantage with a limitation, preserve the condition under which the advantage holds and state the limiting condition explicitly.
