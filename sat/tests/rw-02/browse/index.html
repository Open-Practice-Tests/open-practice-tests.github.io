Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

A conservator cannot close the existing fissures in a weathered limestone relief without replacing much of its original surface. Instead, she injects a binding material beneath vulnerable edges. The treatment is intended to ______ the damage: it will prevent the fissures from widening even though they will remain visible.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: arrest

Choice C is correct because "arrest" can mean stop the development or progress of something. The treatment will not remove the fissures, but it is intended to stop them from widening. That distinction between stopping further damage and reversing existing damage is the passage's central point. "Arrest the damage" expresses the limited but useful effect of the binding material. Neither proving the relief's authenticity nor recording its condition would itself describe the physical effect explained after the colon.

A: Authenticating the damage would concern whether it is genuine, whereas the treatment is designed to change its future progression.

B: The text explicitly says that the fissures will remain visible, so concealing their appearance is not the treatment's intended effect.

D: Documentation records a condition. The passage describes a material intervention that prevents the condition from worsening.

Key takeaway: For a completion question, distinguish preventing a process from reversing its past effects; the explanation after a colon often fixes the meaning.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

Director Nuri Bell follows every stage direction in an early edition of a play, yet she does not subscribe to the view that fidelity requires recreating its first performance. She uses the original directions to generate new effects: a prescribed silence becomes an opportunity for contemporary street sounds to enter the theater.

As used in the text, what does the word "subscribe" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Assent

Choice A is correct because Bell does not agree with, or assent to, a particular view of theatrical fidelity. Her careful use of the written directions might appear to suggest an interest in reproducing the original production. The word "yet" introduces the qualification: she follows those directions while putting them to a new purpose. In the phrase "subscribe to the view," the verb concerns accepting an idea, not making a payment, undertaking an obligation, or identifying a work's creator.

B: The text identifies no dispute about authorship. Bell's relationship to an interpretation of fidelity is the issue.

C: The sentence is about Bell's agreement with a principle, not whether she has made an advance commitment to a production.

D: "Subscribe" can concern contributing to something, but "to the view" identifies a belief that Bell does not accept, not a fund.

Key takeaway: Treat the words immediately following a target word as grammatical evidence for its sense; "to a view" signals an attitude toward an idea.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 7 of 27

The following text is from Robert Browning’s 1855 poem “Respectability.” “Plighted troth” means a pledged commitment between lovers.

Dear, had the world in its caprice
Deigned to proclaim “I know you both,
“Have recognized your plighted troth,
Am sponsor for you: live in peace!”—
How many precious months and years
Of youth had passed, that speed so fast,
Before we found it out at last,
The world, and what it fears?

How much of priceless life were spent
With men that every virtue decks,
And women models of their sex,
Society’s true ornament,—
Ere we dared wander, nights like this,
Thro’ wind and rain, and watch the Seine,
And feel the Boulevart break again
To warmth and light and bliss?

Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?

Correct choice: To suggest that social acceptance might have delayed experiences the speaker now values.

The opening “had the world” introduces a counterfactual: suppose society had approved the couple. The speaker then asks how much precious life would have passed before they dared enjoy nights like the present one. Social approval is thus imagined as a possible delay or constraint, not a missing ingredient in their happiness. The present warmth, light, and bliss contrast with time spent among society’s approved companions.

A: Society’s endorsement belongs to the imagined condition, not to the actual explanation of the freedom the couple presently enjoys.

B: The present walk is already described in intensely positive terms; the counterfactual associates approval with delay rather than greater enjoyment.

D: The speaker calls the lost time priceless and celebrates the present experience, giving no indication of a wish to return to respectable company.

Watch for: Keep a counterfactual condition separate from what actually happened; conditional syntax can reverse the apparent meaning of approving language.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

Text 1

Small motion sensors recorded several migratory birds remaining airborne through the night. During repeated intervals, the sensors detected little bodily movement. Because those quiet intervals occurred during flight rather than on a perch, a researcher concludes that the birds must have been sleeping while airborne.

Text 2

A motion sensor measures activity, not consciousness. A bird that is awake but gliding can produce a low-activity record much like that of a sleeping bird. Establishing sleep during flight therefore requires a measurement that separates those states, even when the evidence for uninterrupted flight itself is persuasive.

How would the author of Text 2 most likely assess Text 1's conclusion about sleeping in flight?

Correct choice: The conclusion requires evidence distinguishing sleep from wakeful gliding, since both could produce the recorded pattern of low movement.

Text 1 moves from low activity during airborne periods to the claim that the birds slept while flying. Text 2 supplies an alternative explanation for the same measurement: an awake bird can glide with little movement. Therefore, motion records alone cannot discriminate between wakefulness and sleep. The appropriate response is to request distinguishing evidence, not deny flight, assume sensor failure, or invent a minimum flight duration for sleep.

A: Text 2 accepts that awake birds can glide without much movement, directly contradicting the assertion that flight requires constant wing motion.

B: Selecting quiet intervals does not resolve the problem: Text 2 explicitly says that wakeful gliding can also produce little recorded movement.

D: Neither text supplies a duration threshold for sleep. A longer recording would still leave low movement compatible with wakeful gliding.

Watch for: When two conditions can generate the same measurement, more of that measurement alone does not establish which condition actually occurred.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 9 of 27

A city evaluating heat-reflecting wall finishes measured both room temperatures and glare on neighboring streets. Some finishes lowered indoor temperatures more than others but also reflected more light into pedestrians' eyes. The planners rejected a ranking based solely on the temperature measurements. Instead, they identified several finishes that met minimum requirements on both measures and allowed neighborhoods to choose among them according to local conditions. Their procedure preserved the environmental objective while recognizing that no single measure captured every consequence relevant to residents.

According to the text, why did the planners allow neighborhoods to choose among several finishes?

Correct choice: The eligible finishes met basic requirements on both measures, while local conditions could affect which tradeoff residents preferred.

The planners first required candidate finishes to satisfy minimum standards for temperature and glare. They then allowed a local choice among the qualifying finishes because relevant consequences and preferences could vary with neighborhood conditions. The decision therefore combines common performance requirements with local judgment about tradeoffs. The passage does not say the finishes perform identically or that residents can disregard the minimum environmental requirement; the environmental objective is explicitly preserved.

B: Minimum requirements were established before the local choices. Residents selected among qualifying finishes rather than deciding whether any minimum was needed.

C: The text says some finishes lowered temperatures more than others. The options were not treated as equivalent on temperature, and glare is not merely appearance.

D: Temperature measurements were already available. The reason for local choice concerns conditions and consequences, not a missing citywide data set.

Key takeaway: Keep the stages of a decision separate: qualification by common standards can precede choice based on local conditions.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 12 of 27

The slime mold Physarum polycephalum has no brain, yet it can avoid areas it has already explored. Researchers proposed that this avoidance supplies a kind of external spatial memory. In their navigation task, an attractive food source lay beyond a U-shaped barrier; following the chemical gradient alone could draw the organism into the barrier’s enclosed region instead of taking it around the obstacle.

Which finding would most directly support the proposed navigational value of avoiding previously explored areas?

Correct choice: Organisms retaining that avoidance escaped the barrier more successfully than organisms deprived of it.

The proposed explanation concerns a particular capacity, avoidance of explored areas, and a particular outcome, successful navigation around an obstacle. Comparing otherwise comparable organisms with and without that capacity directly connects the two. Mere attraction to food, the distribution of a signal, or growth after arrival would not establish that avoidance helps solve the trap. The correct finding tests the mechanism rather than restating the organism’s general abilities.

A: Approaching food without an obstacle demonstrates attraction or movement, but it does not test the navigational advantage supplied by avoiding explored regions.

B: Signal availability helps characterize the task, but it does not compare navigation with and without the proposed external-memory mechanism.

D: Growth after arrival concerns use of the food source, not the process that allowed the organism to escape the barrier and arrive.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: blue image; the remaining soluble chemicals

Two complete clauses meet at the blank: exposure produces an image, and the remaining chemicals are washed away. A semicolon correctly joins those independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction. The second clause’s passive verb, are washed, remains a full finite predicate. Its descriptive words do not make the clause dependent, so a comma alone cannot provide the required boundary.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 22 of 27

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: provides

The paired subjects linked by "neither ... nor" are "the weight" and "the number." Both are singular, so the finite verb must be singular: "provides." The plural noun "threads" belongs to the phrase describing what is counted; it is not the head of the second subject. Supplies gives the main subject the finite predicate it needs. Supplying, to supply, and having supplied are nonfinite forms; each would leave the main sentence without a complete subject-predicate structure.

A: To supply is an infinitive rather than a finite main verb; the embedded verb collect does not complete the main clause.

B: Having supplied is a participial phrase and leaves the project without a finite main predicate, despite the verb within the intervening description.

C: Supplying is a participle, so it cannot independently complete the main clause after the subject and its long explanatory appositive.

Watch for: Do not count a verb inside a relative clause as the main verb of the sentence; identify the principal subject-predicate pair.

  • The conservation team began gently cleaning the screen's surface.
  • Before cleaning was complete, ultraviolet imaging revealed traces of blue pigment on an uncleaned section.
  • The discovery raised concern that cleaning could remove pigment not visible in ordinary light.
  • The team then imaged each remaining section before cleaning it, using the images to guide its work.

The student wants to explain how a discovery changed the team's working procedure, preserving the correct sequence of events. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct choice: After ultraviolet imaging revealed blue pigment on an apparently unpainted carving, the team began imaging each remaining section before cleaning it.

Choice D is correct because it follows the notes' causal and chronological sequence. The team first saw no paint in ordinary light and began cleaning. Ultraviolet imaging then revealed surviving blue pigment on an uncleaned section. Concern about removing unnoticed pigment led the team to image each remaining section before cleaning. The sentence accurately links the discovery to the procedural change without claiming that the team had already cleaned everything, planned to repaint the carving, or needed cleaning for the imaging to work.

A: The pigment was found on an uncleaned section. The team responded by imaging first, not by accelerating cleaning to enable imaging.

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Version P retained an unusual sentence order and included a brief introduction explaining that the narrator often postpones naming people.
  • Version Q used conventional sentence order and had no explanatory introduction.
  • Readers of P identified the narrator's evasiveness more often than readers of Q did.
  • The translator concluded that the tested package of sentence order plus introduction could make the narrator's evasiveness clearer.
  • The comparison did not isolate the contribution of sentence order from that of the introduction.

The student wants to report the finding while preserving the limitation on what caused the difference. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Correct choice: Readers identified evasiveness more often with the unusual-order version and its introduction, but the comparison did not establish how much either feature contributed to that difference.

The observed finding is a higher rate of identifying evasiveness for P, which combines unusual order with an introduction. Because Q differs on both features, the comparison cannot separate their contributions. The correct sentence reports the actual package-level difference while preserving that attribution limit. It neither assigns effects to individual features nor discards the observed result altogether. Both errors are tempting: a limitation on causal explanation does not erase a measured difference, but a measured difference between packages does not identify which component produced it.

B: The design compares packages, not the introduction against sentence order. It cannot establish this feature-by-feature causal ranking.

C: This assigns separate causal contributions to both features, although the comparison did not isolate either contribution.

The key explains the revised interpretation through two observations: the bones would not fit the estimated stomach, and their position places them below the adult rather than within it. One rival reports the revision without explaining its evidentiary basis.
