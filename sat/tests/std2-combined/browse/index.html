Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

Bristlecone pines in the White Mountains of California grow in thin, rocky soil where cold, wind, and drought limit how much wood they can add each year. That slow growth produces wood so dense and resinous that it resists rot and insects, and some living trees have ______ for more than 4,500 years, making them among the oldest known individual organisms on Earth.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: endured

The text explains that the trees’ dense, resinous wood resists rot and insects, which is why some living trees are more than 4,500 years old. Endured, meaning lasted or survived, describes trees that have remained alive for that long despite harsh conditions.

A: Trees are rooted in place; nothing in the text suggests that they have moved.

B: The text stresses that the trees grow slowly, so saying they have accelerated contradicts the passage.

D: The text describes the age of individual trees, not an increase in their number.

Watch for: Use the cause the text gives (wood that resists rot and insects) to predict what the trees have done for 4,500 years.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

The following text is from Joseph Conrad’s 1904 novel Nostromo. In the fictional South American province of Sulaco, a ceremony is being held to mark the start of construction on a railroad financed by foreign investors.

Everybody of note in Sulaco had been invited—the one or two foreign merchants, all the representatives of the old Spanish families then in town, the great owners of estates on the plain, grave, courteous, simple men, caballeros of pure descent, with small hands and feet, conservative, hospitable, and kind. The Occidental Province was their stronghold; their Blanco party had triumphed now; it was their President-Dictator, a Blanco of the Blancos, who sat smiling urbanely between the representatives of two friendly foreign powers. They had come with him from Sta. Marta to countenance by their presence the enterprise in which the capital of their countries was engaged.

As used in the text, what does the word “countenance” most nearly mean?

Correct choice: endorse

The two foreign representatives are described as “friendly,” and they have traveled with the President-Dictator to the ceremony for an enterprise financed by “the capital of their countries.” Their presence is meant to show official approval of that enterprise, so countenance means endorse.

B: In modern usage countenance often means put up with (“would not countenance such behavior”), but the representatives are friendly and have come on purpose to support a venture their own countries are investing in, not merely to accept it reluctantly.

C: The representatives do see the ceremony, and “by their presence” may suggest watching, but their presence is the means; the purpose stated is to lend support to the enterprise, not simply to observe it.

D: Countenance can mean face, but nothing in the text suggests that the representatives oppose or challenge the enterprise; they are described as friendly.

Watch for: When an older text uses a familiar word, ask what the people described are trying to accomplish; here the purpose (backing their countries’ investment) rules out the modern sense “tolerate.”

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

In the 1930s and 1940s the forger Han van Meegeren sold several paintings as newly discovered works by the seventeenth-century Dutch painter Johannes Vermeer. To make his paint look centuries old, van Meegeren mixed it with a synthetic resin and baked the finished canvases until the surface hardened and cracked. Chemical analyses carried out after his arrest found traces of that resin, a substance first manufactured in the twentieth century, in the paint. The very material that van Meegeren relied on to make the works look old thus ______ their recent origin.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: betrays

Van Meegeren added the resin to make the paintings look old, yet chemical analysis of that resin exposed them as modern. Betrays, in its sense of reveals unintentionally or against someone’s purpose, captures this ironic self-exposure.

B: To confide is to reveal something privately to a trusted person, which adds an intention and intimacy that the text does not support.

C: To flaunt is to display something ostentatiously; the resin was hidden in the paint and was detected only by analysis.

D: To advertise is to call public attention to something deliberately; the resin revealed the paintings’ recent origin against the forger’s intention, and only under chemical analysis.

Watch for: When several choices all mean some kind of revealing, decide whether the text treats the revealing as deliberate or as working against someone’s purpose.

Archaeologists long assumed that people began building large monuments only after they had adopted farming, since farming could produce the food surpluses needed to support many workers. At Göbekli Tepe in southeastern Turkey, however, people raised rings of massive carved limestone pillars more than 11,000 years ago, when communities in the region still appear to have lived mainly by hunting and gathering. Some researchers have proposed that the effort of feeding the crowds who gathered to build and use such structures may even have encouraged people to begin cultivating wild grains.

Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?

Correct choice: To present a discovery that challenges an assumed order of two developments, along with a proposal that reverses the assumed relationship between them

The text states an assumption (farming came first and made monuments possible), presents Göbekli Tepe as a site where monuments seem to have come first, and then reports a proposal that reverses the assumed causal link: building monuments may have encouraged farming. Its purpose is to present both the challenging discovery and the reversing proposal.

A: This restates the long-held assumption that the text challenges; the site is presented as evidence that monuments may have come before farming, not after it.

C: The text says nothing about the builders stopping construction; the proposal concerns how monument building may have encouraged cultivation.

Popular accounts often describe forests as communities in which trees use underground fungal networks to pass sugars to struggling neighbors and to send chemical warnings of insect attack. Which choice best describes the function of the underlined sentence in the text as a whole?

Correct choice: It distinguishes a further claim that the evidence has not yet established from a claim that the text treats as established.

The sentence before the underlined one accepts that fungi link neighboring trees. The underlined sentence then separates that established point from the further claim that substantial carbon or signals move through the links rather than by other routes, a claim it calls “far less clear.” The final sentence gives the review’s findings as support for that uncertainty.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 7 of 27

Text 1

Across the grasslands of the Namib Desert, millions of bare, roughly circular patches called fairy circles are ringed by taller grass. In a survey of hundreds of circles, one researcher found sand termites in nearly all of them and proposed that the termites create the circles: by eating the roots of grasses, they clear patches in which rainwater, no longer drawn up by plants, is stored in the soil and sustains their colonies.

Text 2

Some ecologists attribute fairy circles to the grasses themselves. Where water is scarce, grasses at the edge of a bare patch draw water from the surrounding soil, and computer models of this competition reproduce the circles’ remarkably even spacing. These ecologists note that termites could well be attracted to the moist soil that a bare patch provides; in their view, finding termites in a circle does not reveal how the circle began.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to the survey finding described in Text 1?

Correct choice: By granting that termites are common in the circles but denying that their presence shows that they formed them

Text 2 does not dispute that termites occur in the circles; it says termites “could well be attracted” to the moist soil of bare patches and that “finding termites in a circle does not reveal how the circle began.” So Text 2 would accept the survey’s observation but reject the causal conclusion drawn from it.

A: Text 2 does not say that termites keep the circles bare; it suggests only that termites may be attracted to the moist soil of patches that already exist.

B: This accurately reports Text 2’s own evidence for its explanation, but it does not respond to the survey finding, which concerns termites’ presence in the circles.

C: Text 2 does not concede that termites clear the patches, and it agrees that bare patches hold water (“the moist soil that a bare patch provides”).

Watch for: A choice can state something the second text says and still fail to answer the question asked, which here is how Text 2 would respond to one particular finding.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

The following text is from Nathaniel Hawthorne’s 1851 novel The House of the Seven Gables. Hepzibah Pyncheon, an elderly woman from a once-prominent family, has been forced by poverty to open a small shop.

A lady—who had fed herself from childhood with the shadowy food of aristocratic reminiscences, and whose religion it was that a lady’s hand soils itself irremediably by doing aught for bread,—this born lady, after sixty years of narrowing means, is fain to step down from her pedestal of imaginary rank. [...] In this republican country, amid the fluctuating waves of our social life, somebody is always at the drowning-point. The tragedy is enacted with as continual a repetition as that of a popular drama on a holiday, and, nevertheless, is felt as deeply, perhaps, as when an hereditary noble sinks below his order. More deeply; since, with us, rank is the grosser substance of wealth and a splendid establishment, and has no spiritual existence after the death of these, but dies hopelessly along with them.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Correct choice: Hepzibah’s loss of status is a common American misfortune that may be felt even more painfully than a noble’s fall, because in America rank does not survive the wealth that sustains it.

The narrator places Hepzibah’s fall within a general observation: in America “somebody is always at the drowning-point,” and although such falls are common, they are felt as deeply as a noble’s fall, or “more deeply,” because American rank consists only of wealth and “dies hopelessly along with” it.

B: The narrator calls her rank “imaginary,” but the passage treats her fall as a “tragedy” to be felt deeply, not as a deserved punishment.

C: This reverses the text: despite the “continual … repetition,” the fall “is felt as deeply” as a noble’s, and perhaps more deeply.

D: The text says the opposite: in America rank “has no spiritual existence” once wealth is gone, whereas a noble’s rank might outlast it.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 9 of 27

In “Crossing Brooklyn Ferry,” a poem by Walt Whitman included in the 1891–92 edition of Leaves of Grass, the speaker addresses people who will ride the ferry between Brooklyn and Manhattan in future generations. Rather than merely predicting that they will see what he sees, the speaker asserts that the emotions they will feel on the crossing are ones he has already felt himself.

Which quotation from “Crossing Brooklyn Ferry” most effectively illustrates the claim?

Correct choice: “Just as you feel when you look on the river and sky, so I felt, / Just as any of you is one of a living crowd, I was one of a crowd,”

The claim has two parts: the speaker addresses future passengers, and he says their feelings are ones he has already had. In these lines he speaks to “you” and matches their feeling to his own past feeling (“Just as you feel … so I felt”).

B: These lines address the speaker’s present fellow passengers and express his curiosity about them, not a match between future passengers’ feelings and his own.

C: These lines predict what future passengers will do and see, which is exactly what the claim says the speaker goes beyond (“Rather than merely predicting”); they say nothing about his own feelings.

D: “I too” suggests a shared experience, but these lines list things the speaker saw on earlier crossings; they do not speak of anyone’s feelings or equate future passengers’ emotions with his.

Watch for: Test each quotation against every part of the claim; a quotation about the same subject can still fail because it concerns actions or sights rather than feelings.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 10 of 27

The scaly-foot snail has a shell with an outer layer containing iron sulfide, an organic middle layer, and a calcified inner layer. Researchers studying its mechanical properties argue that protection depends on the interaction of layers, not simply on having a hard outer surface. They investigated how the layered structure dissipates the energy of forces resembling a predator’s attack.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support the researchers’ claim about the shell’s protective mechanism?

Correct choice: Stiffening the shell’s middle layer increases transmitted force without changing the outer layer’s resistance to penetration.

The claim attributes protection to the layered system rather than just to a hard exterior. Changing the middle layer’s stiffness while leaving the outer layer’s penetration resistance unchanged isolates a contribution beyond exterior hardness: more force reaches inward when the middle layer is stiffened. The rivals all concern mechanical properties, but each can be established for an individual layer without demonstrating that interaction among layers contributes to the shell’s protection.

A: Ranking the layers by penetration resistance establishes different material properties, but does not show that their interaction improves protection.

C: A fracture threshold characterizes the inner layer under loading; it does not establish how another layer changes the force that reaches it.

D: The isolated-sample comparison concerns thickness within the outer material alone, so it cannot demonstrate the protective contribution of a layered arrangement.

Watch for: Prefer evidence that isolates the proposed interaction; measurements of individual components can be mechanically relevant without testing how the components work together.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

The table shows the approximate size of the world’s population at 25-year intervals from 1950 to 2025. A student claims that the world’s population grew by more people from 2000 to 2025 than from 1950 to 1975 but that its growth over the later period was smaller when expressed as a percentage.

Which choice most effectively uses data from the table to support the student’s claim?

Correct choice: The population grew by about 1.6 billion, or about 64%, from 1950 to 1975 and by about 2.0 billion, or about 32%, from 2000 to 2025.

The claim has two parts: the later increase was larger in number but smaller as a percentage. From 1950 to 1975 the population rose by about 1.6 billion (1.6 ÷ 2.5, about 64%); from 2000 to 2025 it rose by more, about 2.0 billion, but that is only about 32% of the 6.2 billion of 2000.

A: This is true, but the overall increase says nothing about how the two periods compare in number or in percentage.

B: This is true and supports the first part of the claim (the later increase was larger), but it gives no percentages, so it does not show that the later growth was smaller as a percentage.

C: This has the shape the claim needs, but its second percentage divides the increase by the 2025 population (2.0 ÷ 8.2, about 24%) instead of by the 2000 population (2.0 ÷ 6.2, about 32%).

Watch for: A percentage change is measured from the starting value of the period; dividing by the ending value is the most common way to misstate it.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 13 of 27

In the early 1900s a horse named Clever Hans became famous in Berlin for tapping out correct answers to arithmetic questions with his hoof. In 1907 the psychologist Oskar Pfungst tested the horse under varied conditions. Hans usually answered correctly when his questioner knew the answer and stood where Hans could see him, but he rarely did so either when the questioner did not know the answer or when the questioner was hidden from view. These results suggest that ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Correct choice: Hans’s correct answers depended on cues that were available only from a questioner who knew the answer.

Hans succeeded only when two conditions held together: the questioner knew the answer, and Hans could see the questioner. The pattern suggests that Hans was picking up visible cues that only a questioner who knew the answer could supply.

B: The results show that Hans responded to something he saw, but they do not show that the cues were deliberate or meant to deceive.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 14 of 27

The following text is from Katherine Mansfield’s short story “Miss Brill,” collected in her 1922 book The Garden Party and Other Stories. Miss Brill, an aging Englishwoman living in France, spends every Sunday afternoon in a public garden, watching the people there.

Miss Brill put up her hand and touched her fur. Dear little thing! It was nice to feel it again. She had taken it out of its box that afternoon, shaken out the moth-powder, given it a good brush, and rubbed the life back into the dim little eyes. [...] Other people sat on the benches and green chairs, but they were nearly always the same, Sunday after Sunday, and—Miss Brill had often noticed—there was something funny about nearly all of them. They were odd, silent, nearly all old, and from the way they stared they looked as though they’d just come from dark little rooms or even—even cupboards!

Based on the text, what can most reasonably be inferred about Miss Brill?

Correct choice: She does not recognize that details of her own life resemble what she finds odd in the garden’s other regular visitors.

Miss Brill herself comes to the garden every Sunday, and her fur has just come out of a box after being stored with moth-powder. Yet she judges the other regulars, who appear “Sunday after Sunday,” as odd people who look as if they had come “from dark little rooms or even—even cupboards!” The irony is that she does not see how closely her own habits match the oddness she notices in others.

B: The text shows her affection for the fur (“Dear little thing!”), not any wish to be noticed; it never says why she wears it.

C: The text does not say that she avoids the other visitors or that she thinks they judge her; it presents only her judgment of them.

D: She treats the fur tenderly and has brushed it and “rubbed the life back into” it; nothing suggests embarrassment or a plan to replace it.

Watch for: In literary irony, connect details the character reports about herself to what she criticizes in others; the inference often lies in a resemblance she fails to notice.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 16 of 27

Goats, sheep, and many other grazing animals have horizontal, rectangular pupils, which researchers think widen the animals’ view of the ground around them and help them spot approaching predators. These pupils are shaped very differently from ______ of cats and other ambush predators, which are narrow vertical slits.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: those

The blank stands for pupils, the plural noun being compared (“the pupils of cats”). The plural demonstrative pronoun those agrees with it.

A: It is singular and cannot stand for the plural pupils; “it of cats” is also not a standard construction.

B: This is singular, so it does not agree with the plural noun pupils that it replaces.

C: That is singular; it would fit a comparison with a singular noun (“the shape of … that of”), but here the compared noun is the plural pupils.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 17 of 27

The Lycurgus Cup, a Roman glass vessel made in the fourth century CE, looks jade green when light shines on it from the front but glows red when light shines through it from behind. Researchers have traced this effect to tiny particles of gold and silver dispersed throughout the ______ and absorbing light differently depending on the direction from which the light arrives.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: glass, scattering

The phrase “scattering and absorbing light differently …” is a participial phrase describing the particles, not an independent clause. A comma correctly attaches it to the end of the sentence.

A: A semicolon must separate two independent clauses, but the participial phrase has no subject or finite verb.

C: A period would leave “Scattering and absorbing light differently …” as a sentence fragment.

D: A colon introduces an explanation or list after a complete clause; here it would wrongly set the descriptive phrase apart as though it explained what came before.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 21 of 27

The man whose body hikers found in the Ötztal Alps in 1991 lived about 5,300 years ago, and his well-preserved clothing and equipment have revealed much about daily life in his time. He carried a belt pouch holding flint ______ wore shoes stuffed with grass for warmth.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: tools and

The last sentence has one subject, He, with two verbs: carried and wore. Because and wore … has no subject of its own, it is not an independent clause, so no punctuation separates the two parts of this compound predicate.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 22 of 27

In 1847 Ignaz Semmelweis, a physician at Vienna General Hospital, noticed that far more new mothers died of fever in a ward staffed by doctors and medical students than in one staffed by midwives. He concluded that the doctors, who often came directly from performing autopsies, were carrying infectious material on their hands to patients. ______ he required the ward’s doctors to wash their hands in a chlorinated lime solution before examining patients, and deaths in the ward dropped sharply.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: Accordingly,

Semmelweis concluded that doctors were carrying infectious material on their hands; the handwashing rule is the action he took in response to that conclusion. Accordingly, meaning in a way that follows from what was just said, marks this logical consequence.

A: Nevertheless signals an action taken despite what came before, but the handwashing rule follows directly from his conclusion.

B: The handwashing rule is not an example of his conclusion; it is a response to it.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 23 of 27

The explosion that flattened some 80 million trees near the Tunguska River in Siberia in 1908 left no impact crater, and expeditions found no large meteorite fragments. Most researchers therefore conclude that the object responsible, probably a small asteroid or comet, exploded in the air rather than striking the ground. The pattern of fallen trees supports this view: across more than 2,000 square kilometers, trunks lay pointing away from a central area. ______ trees at that central area were left standing but stripped of their branches, as would be expected directly beneath a blast from above.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: Moreover,
