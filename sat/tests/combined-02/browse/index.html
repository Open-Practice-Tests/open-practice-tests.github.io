Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

The city's first redevelopment report devoted entire chapters to road access and property values but mentioned residents' attachment to familiar gathering places only in a brief appendix. Urban historian Leena Marr objected that this arrangement relegated a central concern of the neighborhood to the margins of the discussion, making it appear incidental to the plan rather than fundamental to its evaluation.

As used in the text, what does the word "relegated" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Assigned a less important position

Choice B is correct because the report gives residents' attachment to gathering places only a brief appendix, while other considerations receive full chapters. Marr objects that this arrangement makes a central concern appear incidental. "Relegated" therefore means assigned to a less important position within the discussion. The concern is still included, so the word does not mean eliminated altogether. Its reduced prominence, rather than the literal location of the appendix alone, is what makes the arrangement objectionable to Marr.

A: The brief appendix gives the concern less attention than the full chapters receive; Marr is not objecting to excessive explanatory detail.

C: The report changes the concern's prominence, not who has authority to address it. No transfer between people or institutions is described.

D: The concern appears in the appendix, so it has not been entirely excluded. The issue is its subordinate treatment within the report.

Key takeaway: Distinguish reduced importance from total exclusion; the exact degree of an action often separates a contextual meaning from its closest rival.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

A conservator cannot close the existing fissures in a weathered limestone relief without replacing much of its original surface. Instead, she injects a binding material beneath vulnerable edges. The treatment is intended to ______ the damage: it will prevent the fissures from widening even though they will remain visible.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: arrest

Choice C is correct because "arrest" can mean stop the development or progress of something. The treatment will not remove the fissures, but it is intended to stop them from widening. That distinction between stopping further damage and reversing existing damage is the passage's central point. "Arrest the damage" expresses the limited but useful effect of the binding material. Neither proving the relief's authenticity nor recording its condition would itself describe the physical effect explained after the colon.

A: Authenticating the damage would concern whether it is genuine, whereas the treatment is designed to change its future progression.

B: The text explicitly says that the fissures will remain visible, so concealing their appearance is not the treatment's intended effect.

D: Documentation records a condition. The passage describes a material intervention that prevents the condition from worsening.

Key takeaway: For a completion question, distinguish preventing a process from reversing its past effects; the explanation after a colon often fixes the meaning.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

Director Nuri Bell follows every stage direction in an early edition of a play, yet she does not subscribe to the view that fidelity requires recreating its first performance. She uses the original directions to generate new effects: a prescribed silence becomes an opportunity for contemporary street sounds to enter the theater.

As used in the text, what does the word "subscribe" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Assent

Choice A is correct because Bell does not agree with, or assent to, a particular view of theatrical fidelity. Her careful use of the written directions might appear to suggest an interest in reproducing the original production. The word "yet" introduces the qualification: she follows those directions while putting them to a new purpose. In the phrase "subscribe to the view," the verb concerns accepting an idea, not making a payment, undertaking an obligation, or identifying a work's creator.

B: The text identifies no dispute about authorship. Bell's relationship to an interpretation of fidelity is the issue.

C: The sentence is about Bell's agreement with a principle, not whether she has made an advance commitment to a production.

D: "Subscribe" can concern contributing to something, but "to the view" identifies a belief that Bell does not accept, not a fund.

Key takeaway: Treat the words immediately following a target word as grammatical evidence for its sense; "to a view" signals an attitude toward an idea.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 4 of 27

Histories of Elvar's covered pedestrian passages often credit shopkeepers who wanted customers to browse during rainstorms. Building historian Petra Sol has found that the earliest passages connected two public courtyards and contained no shops. Municipal repair accounts identify them as routes for moving records between offices in wet weather. Shops appeared along these routes only a generation later. Sol does not dispute the passages' eventual commercial importance; she argues that this importance has been projected backward into explanations of their origin.

Which choice best describes the overall structure of the text?

Correct choice: It introduces an accepted explanation, presents evidence for a different origin, and distinguishes that origin from a later use.

Choice A is correct because the text first reports the usual commercial explanation for the covered passages. Sol's evidence then places the earliest passages in an administrative setting without shops, supporting a different account of their origin. Finally, the text acknowledges the later commercial role while warning against treating that later role as the original purpose. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

Archaeologist Imani Vel argues that a shallow basin beside an ancient courtyard collected household refuse over several seasons, rather than remnants of a single public feast. The basin contains many broken serving vessels, a finding compatible with either explanation. Between successive layers of vessel fragments, however, are thin deposits of windblown sand that would have taken weeks to accumulate. The vessels therefore cannot all have entered the basin during one brief gathering.

Which choice best describes the function of the bold sentence in the text as a whole?

Correct choice: It supplies a chronological constraint that helps distinguish between two explanations of the same objects.

Choice C is correct because the sand layers provide information about elapsed time between deposits of pottery. Broken serving vessels alone could result from either repeated disposal or a single feast. Sand that takes weeks to accumulate between successive pottery layers makes a single brief disposal event inadequate. The bold sentence therefore supplies a constraint on the timing of deposition, helping distinguish the rival explanations. It supports the repeated-deposition account without claiming that the sand independently identifies every activity that produced the refuse.

A: The sentence challenges the single-feast explanation and supports Vel's proposal; it does not concede an advantage to the rival account.

B: The sentence concerns intervals between deposits, not the preservation of serving vessels relative to other types of objects.

D: The windblown sand is useful evidence about timing. The text relies on it rather than treating it as a source of irresolvable uncertainty.

Key takeaway: Ask what uncertainty the evidence resolves. A sentence can establish timing without directly identifying the people or activities responsible.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 6 of 27

The following passage is from an original work of fiction. For thirty years, Mara had shortened her name when she introduced herself at the workshop. On the application for a new studio, she wrote the name in full, then reached for the eraser. The form's little boxes, she noticed, had left exactly enough room. She set the eraser down. It was an absurd reason, she thought, to let a name stand, but she carried the form to the desk before she could find a more respectable one.

Which choice best describes the role of the form's boxes in the passage?

Correct choice: They provide an incidental circumstance that helps Mara act on a choice she is reluctant to justify directly.

Choice A is correct because the available space gives Mara a small, almost arbitrary reason to leave her full name on the application. Her movement toward the eraser reveals hesitation, while her decision to submit the form before finding a "more respectable" reason suggests that she wants to retain the name but feels uneasy explaining that desire. The boxes enable the choice without establishing any policy of the studio or explaining why she began shortening her name thirty years earlier.

B: The boxes happen to fit Mara's name, but the passage provides no evidence about who designed them or the studio's intentions.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 7 of 27

TEXT 1

Removing dense reeds from experimental wetland plots increased the number of annual plant species in the following season. Ecologist Dara Pell interprets this result as evidence that reed competition was preventing these species from becoming established. Pell recommends selective reed removal to create opportunities for annual plants.

TEXT 2

Ecologist Owen Fenn notes that pulling reeds out also exposes patches of bare soil. Many annual plants establish more readily on bare soil, regardless of which plant previously occupied it. Fenn proposes comparing cleared plots with plots where reeds are clipped above the ground, leaving the soil surface undisturbed.

Based on the texts, how would Fenn most likely respond to the claim in Text 1?

Correct choice: By arguing that the reported increase does not establish which of two changes caused it

Choice D is correct because Fenn identifies a second consequence of clearing: disturbance exposes bare soil. An increase in annual species after reeds are removed could therefore reflect relief from reed competition, new opportunities created by disturbed soil, or both. Fenn's proposed comparison attempts to distinguish these effects. Text 2 does not deny that clearing increased the observed number of species; it questions whether that observation by itself establishes the specific mechanism claimed in Text 1.

A: Fenn calls for an additional comparison to separate mechanisms. That request is inconsistent with treating the observed species count as sufficient evidence.

B: Neither text limits the argument to dry conditions. The relevant distinction is reed removal versus soil disturbance, not a moisture threshold.

C: Fenn treats exposed soil as an alternative explanation to investigate, not as an explanation already demonstrated to be the only cause.

Key takeaway: When one text proposes an additional control, identify the competing explanations the control would separate; do not turn a possibility into a conclusion.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

TEXT 1

A revival of a century-old satire should replace obscure references to politicians with references its audience can recognize, argues director Hana Lott. The original audience understood whom the jokes targeted; preserving the jokes' function therefore requires changing some of their words. Without that recognition, the play risks becoming a sequence of unexplained names.

TEXT 2

Dramaturg Emre Vale opposes replacing the names in historical satires. A program note can supply the knowledge audiences need to grasp a joke's target while also showing how satire operated in a particular political setting. Replacing the names may preserve immediate intelligibility at the cost of obscuring that setting.

Which statement would both Lott and Vale most likely agree with?

Correct choice: Audiences need some understanding of whom a satire targets to appreciate the point of its jokes.

Choice A is correct because both commentators treat audience recognition of the jokes' targets as necessary to understanding their point. Lott would supply that recognition by replacing unfamiliar names with familiar ones. Vale would supply it through program notes while preserving the historical names. Their disagreement concerns how to make the targets intelligible and which costs to accept, not whether intelligibility matters. The common ground is therefore the audience's need for relevant knowledge, rather than either commentator's preferred method of providing it.

B: Lott explicitly argues that changing names can preserve the jokes' function. This statement therefore contradicts Lott's account of the revision.

C: Vale emphasizes the original political setting; Lott prioritizes preserving the jokes' function for a contemporary audience, so this ranking is not shared.

D: Vale endorses program notes, but neither text compares the methods' effectiveness, and Lott advocates changing the dialogue.

Key takeaway: When authors disagree about a method, look for the shared objective that makes their disagreement meaningful rather than choosing either preferred method.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

In a study of burrowing shore crabs, biologist Teo Venn found that crabs occupied many more burrows beneath low rock ledges than on exposed banks nearby. Venn hypothesizes that the ledges are attractive primarily because they shelter the burrow entrances from strong winds, rather than because they conceal the crabs from predators. The two explanations make different predictions about entrances that are sheltered from wind but remain visible from above.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support Venn's hypothesis?

Correct choice: When transparent windbreaks were added around exposed entrances, occupancy increased even though overhead visibility was unchanged.

Choice A is correct because the transparent windbreaks change the proposed benefit, shelter from wind, while leaving the rival benefit, visual concealment from overhead predators, unchanged. An increase in occupancy under that condition supports the idea that wind protection helps attract the crabs. The result does not prove that predator concealment never matters; it directly supports the hypothesized role of wind shelter by separating that feature from the other feature normally associated with rock ledges.

B: This finding confirms that natural ledges provide both possible benefits, leaving the competing explanations entangled rather than distinguishing them.

C: Occupancy during calm weather does not show why the crabs selected the sites; existing burrows may remain useful between windy periods.

D: Retreat from a predator shows sensitivity to predation risk, but it does not support wind shelter as the primary explanation for ledge preference.

Key takeaway: Choose evidence that changes the proposed cause while holding the strongest rival explanation steady; a finding compatible with both is less diagnostic.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 12 of 27

Grooves cut into stone platforms at an ancient shoreline settlement have been interpreted in two ways: as channels that drained water from working surfaces or as sockets that held wooden frames in place. Archaeologist Lina Roe favors drainage, pointing out that the platforms stand near areas used for processing fish. Roe argues that the grooves would have kept those working surfaces dry.

Which finding, if true, would most directly weaken Roe's interpretation?

Correct choice: Every groove slopes toward a closed central depression, and none connects that depression to an edge from which water could escape.

Choice D is correct because it shows that the grooves would direct water into a closed depression rather than carry it off the working surface. That geometry conflicts with Roe's proposed function of keeping the surfaces dry through drainage. The finding need not prove the wooden-frame explanation to weaken the drainage explanation: demonstrating that the channels lack an outlet directly challenges the mechanism Roe proposes. Evidence that fish processing occurred nearby would not resolve this structural problem.

A: Heavy rainfall could increase the usefulness of drainage. It does not show that these particular grooves could not have served that purpose.

B: A common stone source concerns construction materials. It does not show whether the grooves could drain the working surfaces.

C: Fish scales support the processing context, and wood may fail to survive. Neither fact directly undermines the proposed drainage function.

Key takeaway: Test whether the proposed structure can perform the claimed function. Evidence against one explanation need not conclusively establish its rival.

Which statement about the graph most directly supports the researcher's observation about the gap?

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 15 of 27

Seeds of a dune plant remained dormant when watered in a laboratory. Researchers then removed the outer seed coats from one group and left another group intact. Both groups received equal amounts of water under identical conditions, and only the coatless seeds germinated. In a second trial, intact seeds germinated after their coats were nicked, although tests showed that nicking did not change how much water the seeds absorbed. The researchers therefore rejected inadequate water absorption as the sole explanation for dormancy.

Which conclusion is most strongly supported by the text?

Correct choice: The outer coat can prevent germination through an effect other than limiting the amount of water absorbed.

Choice D is correct because nicking permits germination without changing the amount of water absorbed. The coat must therefore be capable of preventing germination in a way not fully explained by reduced total water uptake. The text does not identify that additional effect; it might involve some other condition altered by nicking. The warranted conclusion preserves that limit. It rules out inadequate absorption as the sole account without claiming that water is unimportant or that a particular replacement mechanism has been demonstrated.

A: The experiments used equal water amounts and did not determine minimum water requirements for either group. Germination alone does not establish those thresholds.

B: Water absorption after complete coat removal is not reported. The passage supports no comparison between removal and nicking on that measure.

C: No measurement of water distribution within seeds is described. This identifies a specific mechanism that the evidence does not establish.

Key takeaway: When an experiment rules out one complete explanation, infer only that something more is needed; do not invent the unmeasured replacement mechanism.

A factory makes 60% of its units on line A and the rest on line B. Exactly 5% of line A units and 8% of line B units are rejected. Among rejected units, what fraction were made on line B?

Answer: C. 16/31 Imagine 1,000 units: 600 from A and 400 from B. Rejected counts are 0.05(600) = 30 and 0.08(400) = 32. Among the 62 rejected units, the fraction from B is 32/62 = 16/31.

A circle has equation x2 + y2 + Dx + Ey + 45 = 0. Its center is in the first quadrant, and it is tangent to both coordinate axes. What is D + E?

Answer: A. −125 A circle tangent to both axes with first-quadrant center has center (r, r) for radius r > 0. Its equation expands to x2 + y2 − 2rx − 2ry + r2 = 0. Therefore r2 = 45, so r = 35, and D + E = −4r = −125.

Watch for: The first-quadrant condition determines the signs of the center coordinates and hence the linear coefficients.

Math · Module 2 · Question 21 of 22

For exactly two real values of a, the system (a + 2)x + 4y = 6 and 6x + (a − 2)y = 9 has no solution. What is the sum of the squares of those two values of a?

Answer: B. 56 For the variable coefficients to be proportional, (a + 2)(a − 2) = 4(6), giving a2 = 28. The candidates are a = ±28. Coincident lines would also need the right-side ratio 9/6 = 3/2, which requires 6/(a + 2) = 3/2, hence a = 2. Neither candidate is 2, so both give distinct parallel lines and no solution. Their squared values sum to 28 + 28 = 56.

Watch for: A zero determinant is only the candidate condition; rule out coincident lines before counting no-solution cases.

Math · Module 2 · Question 22 of 22

What is the least integer a for which x2 + (a − 6)x + 2a = 0 has two distinct negative real roots?

Answer: C. 19 The root sum is 6 − a, so two negative roots require a > 6. Their product 2a is then positive. Distinct real roots require (a − 6)2 − 8a > 0, which factors as (a − 2)(a − 18) > 0. Combining this with a > 6 gives a > 18. Thus the least integer is 19. Negative sum and positive product ensure that both real roots are negative.

Watch for: The sign conditions alone do not ensure real roots; the discriminant condition is also necessary.
