Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

At the exchange hall, traders habitually lowered their voices whenever negotiations began at the central table. No posted regulation required this, and newcomers were never formally instructed to do it. Instead, they noticed the practice and followed it. The hall therefore depended on a ______ understanding that important negotiations should not be interrupted by nearby conversation.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: tacit

Tacit means understood without being explicitly stated. The passage repeatedly distinguishes the traders' behavior from a formal instruction: there is no posted rule, and newcomers learn by observation rather than explanation. The blank therefore describes an understanding communicated implicitly through practice. Its duration and the degree of individual agreement are not the central issues. A norm can be tacit because of how it is conveyed, even when many people follow it.

A: The text shows a widely followed practice but does not establish that every trader has explicitly or internally endorsed the understanding.

B: The practice is habitual, and nothing identifies an expiration or brief duration; the missing quality concerns how the understanding is communicated.

C: Traders conduct negotiations, but the understanding concerns their shared conduct around those negotiations, not rivalry between the traders.

Key takeaway: Identify whether the context describes how an agreement is communicated, how long it lasts, or how widely it is accepted.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

Before a set of carved wooden instruments arrived at the museum, conservator Elian Voss tested the humidity controls in every display case. He also arranged for spare sensors to be kept nearby. These preparations were intended to forestall damage: a problem discovered after a crack appeared would be a problem discovered too late. Voss regarded the uneventful opening month as a success, although visitors had no reason to notice his work.

As used in the text, what does the word "forestall" most nearly mean?

Correct choice: Prevent

Forestall means prevent in this context. Voss checks the cases and stores spare sensors before the instruments arrive, and he considers waiting until a crack appears to be too late. His objective is therefore to keep damage from occurring in the first place. The uneventful month confirms the kind of success he seeks: no visible problem arises. The word describes the purpose of the preparations rather than the technical steps they involve.

A: The text distinguishes these advance preparations from action taken after a crack appears; repairing would address existing damage.

C: Testing the controls is part of the preparation, but the stated purpose is stopping future damage, not measuring damage that exists.

D: Visitors do not notice Voss's work because damage has been avoided, not because he hides damage that has already occurred.

Key takeaway: Use the sequence of events to distinguish preventing a problem from inspecting, concealing, or correcting an existing one.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

In a cooperative market, members who transport goods farther receive larger credits, but distance alone does not determine the award. Loads that are especially fragile also earn additional credits. The arrangement is intended to make each member's compensation ______ the effort and care that the delivery requires, rather than simply identical to what every other member receives.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: commensurate with

Commensurate with means appropriately proportioned to or corresponding to. The cooperative adjusts credits for both distance and fragility, two aspects of the work required. The text contrasts this approach with giving everyone the same amount. It therefore calls for a relationship in which differences in effort and care are reflected in differences in compensation. It does not suggest that the credits and the work are literally the same thing.

A: The credits explicitly depend on distance and fragility, so compensation is connected to rather than independent of the work.

B: Compensation is the arrangement's intended response to effort and care, not a minor consequence that matters only indirectly.

D: Effort and compensation are different kinds of things. The text seeks a proportionate relationship, not identity between them.

Key takeaway: When a blank links two quantities or concepts, identify the intended relationship: proportion, independence, similarity, or identity.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

Researchers sliding two rock surfaces against each other in a model fault observed that friction dropped at high speeds. They proposed that heat from the sliding melted the contact surface and allowed it to move more freely. To test whether melting was necessary, they repeated the experiment while cooling the surfaces enough to prevent it. Friction still dropped. The result did not rule out melting in natural faults; it showed that the laboratory effect could occur without melting.

Which choice best describes the overall structure of the text?

Correct choice: It presents an observation and a proposed explanation, describes a test of that explanation, and draws a limited conclusion from the result.

The passage first reports a friction decrease and then gives a possible explanation involving melting. Cooling the surfaces tests whether the effect depends on that proposed mechanism. Because the effect persists without melting, the researchers can conclude that melting is not necessary for their laboratory result. The final sentence limits the scope: it does not exclude melting as a process in natural faults. The correct structural description preserves that sequence and qualification.

A: The cooling test challenges melting as a necessary explanation of the laboratory result; it does not confirm every part of a theory.

C: The passage describes a controlled model experiment rather than a comparison of natural faults, and it offers no classification system.

D: The experiment confirms the friction drop under a new condition; it does not reveal that the initial observation was erroneous.

Key takeaway: When a mechanism is experimentally removed, distinguish evidence that it is unnecessary in that experiment from a claim that it never operates.

Geologist Tomas Iri agrees that a regional sea-level change could explain the shared timing. Based on the texts, how would Iri most likely respond to Pell's account?

Iri accepts the feature of Pell's explanation that concerns timing: a regional sea-level change could account for the shorelines forming in the same interval. Iri then identifies another feature, their different present heights, that calls for local island movement as well. The disagreement is therefore about explanatory completeness, not whether the regional process occurred at all. The correct response preserves the accepted component and adds the process needed for the remaining observation.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

TEXT 1

Music scholar Neri Hall argues that early contour notation left performers considerable freedom. Because the marks showed whether a melody rose or fell without specifying every interval, a singer could realize the same sequence of marks in several distinct melodies. The notation itself did not select just one of them.

TEXT 2

Scholar Davi Sen cautions against inferring actual performance freedom directly from what a notation omits. A singer who had learned a melody orally might use contour marks simply to retrieve it. Several melodies could fit the marks in principle, while only one would fit the musical tradition in which the marks were used.

Which choice best describes how Sen's text relates to Hall's text?

Correct choice: It distinguishes possibilities allowed by the written signs alone from possibilities available to performers within a particular tradition.

Hall moves from what the marks specify to the freedom a performer has. Sen questions that move by introducing information supplied through oral learning. The signs alone may fit several melodies, while the performer's tradition may identify only one as appropriate. Sen can accept the notational omissions without accepting Hall's conclusion about performance freedom. The signs may still help retrieve the known melody; neither the oral knowledge nor the written notation must become useless.

B: Sen accepts the notational omissions but questions the inference of performance freedom; this choice wrongly attributes Hall's conclusion to Sen.

D: Several melodies can fit the signs in principle regardless of what a singer remembers; oral learning constrains practical choices, not those formal possibilities.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

In a study of a fictional trading community, economist Mina Taal proposes that transferable market vouchers helped small producers withstand brief shortages of coins. Unlike vouchers redeemable only by their original recipients, transferable vouchers could pass through several purchases before being exchanged for coins. Taal argues that this feature mattered most when producers had willing customers but those customers temporarily lacked coins. She is seeking evidence that transferability, rather than merely the existence of vouchers, protected trade during such shortages.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support Taal's argument?

Correct choice: During comparable coin shortages, sales fell less among producers using transferable vouchers than among producers using recipient-only vouchers.

Taal's specific claim concerns a benefit of transferability during shortages, not simply a benefit of issuing vouchers. Comparing sales declines under comparable shortages across the two voucher systems targets both parts of that claim. A smaller decline with transferable vouchers is the predicted protective effect. The comparison does not prove that no other factor matters, but among the choices it provides the most direct evidence connecting the distinctive feature to the proposed outcome.

A: The association between voucher issuance and annual trade does not isolate transferability or the effect of brief coin shortages.

B: Durability might facilitate repeated use, but this finding does not establish a difference in sales protection during coin shortages.

D: Preference when coins are plentiful does not test whether transferability specifically protects sales during shortages of coins.

Key takeaway: For support questions, identify the claim's distinctive feature and outcome, then look for evidence comparing the relevant alternatives.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 12 of 27

Scholar Arel Noy examined two editions of a fictional piano suite. The later edition added precise metronome markings where the earlier edition used only words such as "briskly." Noy claims that these numerical markings substantially narrowed performers' choices of tempo. To evaluate the claim, another researcher collected recordings by musicians who used the earlier edition and recordings by musicians who used the later one, all made under comparable performance conditions.

Which finding, if true, would most directly weaken Noy's claim?

Correct choice: The range of tempos in recordings using the later edition was approximately as wide as the range in recordings using the earlier edition.

Noy claims that numerical markings substantially narrowed tempo choices. A similar spread of actual tempos before and after the markings were added runs against that predicted reduction in variation. The comparison concerns the range rather than merely the average: performers could cluster around a new average while retaining a wide range of choices. The other findings concern average alignment, edition use, or musicians' descriptions, none of which directly contradicts the claimed narrowing.

A: Calling the markings useful does not determine whether they narrowed final tempo choices, which is the specific claim under examination.

C: A shift in the average toward the marking does not show that performers' tempos remained broadly varied; the range could still narrow.

D: The number of recordings using each edition does not by itself reveal how much the performers' tempo choices varied.

Key takeaway: Match evidence to the quantity a claim concerns: a change in the average is different from a change in variation.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: surface; nevertheless,

The material before the blank is an independent clause: "The research vessel encountered no visible waves at the surface." The material after the blank also has a subject and finite verb: "instruments ... detected a sharp pressure change." A semicolon can connect these clauses. The conjunctive adverb "nevertheless" then introduces the contrast in the second clause and is followed by a comma. It cannot supply the joining function of a coordinating conjunction by itself.

Key takeaway: A conjunctive adverb can express a relationship between clauses, but a semicolon or period must supply the appropriate clause boundary.

Answer: D. 18 The smaller-to-larger area ratio is 96/216 = 4/9, so the height ratio is 2/3. Write the heights as 2u and 3u. Their difference is u = 6, giving the greater height 3u = 18.

The graphs of f (x) = x2 + ax + 12 and g(x) = 2x2 − 4x + b intersect at inputs x = 1 and x = 5. What is a + b?

Enter an integer, decimal, or fraction. Up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus. No units or mixed numbers.

Answer: 19 The difference g(x) − f (x) = x2 − (a + 4)x + (b − 12) has roots 1 and 5 and leading coefficient 1. It must equal (x − 1)(x − 5) = x2 − 6x + 5. Therefore a + 4 = 6 and b − 12 = 5, giving a = 2, b = 17, and sum 19.

Watch for: An intersection makes the difference of the functions zero, not either individual function zero.

Math · Module 2 · Question 20 of 22

A rectangle has area 84 and diagonal length 193. What is its perimeter?

Answer: A. 38 Let the side lengths be l, w > 0. The area gives lw = 84, and the diagonal gives l2 + w2 = 193. Then (l + w)2 = 193 + 2(84) = 361, so l + w = 19. The perimeter is 2(19) = 38. One consistent rectangle has sides 7 and 12.

Watch for: The diagonal gives the sum of the squares of the sides, not the square of their sum.

For a linear function f and a fixed real number t, f (t + 2) = 3 f (t) and f (t + 8) = f (t) + 48. What is f (t + 5)?

Enter an integer, decimal, or fraction. Up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus. No units or mixed numbers.

Answer: 36 The second condition gives slope 48/8 = 6. Therefore f (t + 2) = f (t) + 12. Compare this with 3 f (t) to get f (t) = 6. The output five input units later is f (t + 5) = 6 + 5(6) = 36.

A quadratic function f with leading coefficient 1 satisfies f (1) = f (7) and has minimum value −4. What is the sum of the squares of all real solutions of | f (x)| = 3?

Answer: B. 80 Equal outputs put the axis at x = 4, so f (x) = (x − 4)2 − 4. The equation | f (x)| = 3 gives (x − 4)2 = 7 or (x − 4)2 = 1. For a pair 4 ± d, the squared values sum to 2(16 + d2). Thus the total is 2(16 + 7) + 2(16 + 1) = 46 + 34 = 80. All four roots are real and distinct.

Watch for: Include both f (x) = 3 and f (x) = −3 before computing the requested sum.
