Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

The following text is from E. M. Forster’s 1910 novel Howards End. Margaret Schlegel is thinking about the Wilcoxes, a family of brisk, practical businesspeople whose way of life differs from that of Margaret and her younger siblings, Helen and Tibby.

To Margaret this life was to remain a real force. She could not despise it, as Helen and Tibby Underlined: affected End underlined. to do. It fostered such virtues as neatness, decision, and obedience, virtues of the second rank, no doubt, but they have formed our civilisation. They form character, too; Margaret could not doubt it; they keep the soul from becoming sloppy.

As used in the text, what does the word “affected” most nearly mean?

Correct choice: feigned

In an older sense, to affect to do something is to make a show of doing it. Helen and Tibby profess to despise the Wilcoxes’ way of life, and the word implies that their contempt is partly a pose, whereas Margaret cannot share it at all. Feigned captures this meaning.

A: Resolved would mean that Helen and Tibby had firmly decided to despise the Wilcoxes’ life; affect does not carry this meaning, and the word suggests a show of contempt rather than a determination.

C: Influenced is the most familiar meaning of affect, but it does not fit the sentence: Helen and Tibby are not influencing anyone; they are the ones who display contempt.

D: Declined would mean that Helen and Tibby refused to despise the Wilcoxes’ life, which erases the contrast the sentence draws between them and Margaret.

Watch for: When a familiar word is followed by an infinitive (affected to do), test older senses: affect can mean to pretend or to make a show of.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

The story of six-year-old George Washington confessing that he had damaged his father’s cherry tree first appeared in 1806, in the fifth edition of a biography by Mason Locke Weems, more than six years after Washington’s death. Weems attributed the anecdote to an unnamed elderly woman who had known the family when she was a girl, and no earlier account of the episode has ever been found. For these reasons, most historians regard the story as ______, even though it remains one of the best-known episodes of Washington’s childhood.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

Correct choice: apocryphal

The reasons given all concern where the story came from: it surfaced years after Washington’s death, rests on an unnamed source, and has no earlier record, yet it is widely retold. A story of doubtful authenticity that nonetheless circulates widely is apocryphal.

A: Specious describes something, usually an argument, that seems plausible but is actually false or misleading; the text’s evidence concerns the story’s uncertain origin, not a deceptive plausibility.

B: Anachronistic means placed in the wrong period, but nothing suggests the story is set in the wrong time; the problem is that it first appeared long after the events it describes.

C: Tendentious means written to promote a particular point of view; the text says nothing about Weems’s purpose, only about the lack of any reliable source for the story.

Watch for: Match the word to the reason the text gives: evidence about a story’s source points to doubtful authenticity, not to bias or misleading reasoning.

The following text is from Sinclair Lewis’s 1922 novel Babbitt. The narrator describes Zenith, a fictional American city, early in the morning.

The towers of Zenith aspired above the morning mist; austere towers of steel and cement and limestone, sturdy as cliffs and delicate as silver rods. They were neither citadels nor churches, but frankly and beautifully office-buildings. The city was full of such grotesqueries, but the clean towers were thrusting them from the business center, and on the farther hills were shining new houses, homes—Underlined: they seemed End underlined.—for laughter and tranquillity.

Which choice best describes the function of the underlined portion in the text as a whole?

Correct choice: It suggests that the new houses’ promise of laughter and tranquillity may be only an appearance, introducing doubt into a description that has so far admired the new city.

Up to this point the narrator describes the new city in glowing terms. The brief interruption “they seemed” turns the final claim about “laughter and tranquillity” into a matter of appearance, introducing a note of doubt about whether the houses’ promise will be fulfilled.

B: “Seemed” does mark an appearance, but nothing in the text attributes that impression to the residents; the aside qualifies the narrator’s own description.

C: The aside casts doubt on one claim about the new houses; it does not show that every earlier statement, such as the description of the towers, was ironic.

D: The aside is attached to the new houses on the hills, not to the older buildings, which the narrator plainly calls “grotesqueries.”

The following text is from William Makepeace Thackeray’s 1848 novel Vanity Fair. Miss Crawley is the wealthy, unmarried half-sister of Sir Pitt Crawley, a baronet.

Miss Crawley was, in consequence, an object of great respect when she came to Queen’s Crawley, for she had a balance at her banker’s which would have made her beloved anywhere. Underlined: How tenderly we look at her faults if she is a relative (and may every reader have a score of such), what a kind good-natured old creature we find her! End underlined. How the junior partner of Hobbs and Dobbs leads her smiling to the carriage with the lozenge upon it, and the fat wheezy coachman!

Which choice best describes the function of the underlined sentence in the text as a whole?

Correct choice: It widens the narrator’s ironic target from Miss Crawley’s relatives to readers themselves, implying that anyone would overlook a rich relative’s faults.

The narrator has just said that Miss Crawley’s bank balance “would have made her beloved anywhere.” The underlined sentence shifts to “we” and wishes “every reader” such relatives, suggesting that we all look “tenderly” on a rich relative’s faults and find her “kind” because of her money. The satire now includes the reader.

B: Read literally, the sentence praises Miss Crawley, but its point is that her money makes people find her kind; the praise is ironic, and nothing suggests her relatives failed to appreciate her.

C: The sentence mentions her faults only to say that we overlook them; its target is the people who flatter her, not Miss Crawley herself.

Watch for: Notice when a narrator switches to “we” or addresses the reader: in satire, that switch often turns the criticism on the audience.

New Zealand’s moa, flightless birds that included species taller than a person, disappeared within about two centuries after Polynesians settled the islands around 1300 CE. Some researchers have suggested that hunting merely finished off moa populations that were already shrinking before people arrived, perhaps because of a changing climate or volcanic eruptions.

Geneticist Morten Allentoft and colleagues analyzed ancient DNA from hundreds of moa bones dated to the four thousand years before the birds’ extinction. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 7 of 27

Text 1

The Hawaiian–Emperor seamount chain, a line of volcanic islands and undersea mountains on the Pacific floor, bends sharply at a point that formed about 47 million years ago. The chain is thought to have grown as the Pacific plate moved over a hot spot, a plume of hot rock rising from deep in the mantle. Treating that hot spot as fixed in place, geologists long read the bend as a record of an abrupt change in the direction of the plate’s motion.

Text 2

In 2003 geophysicist John Tarduno and colleagues analyzed the magnetism preserved in lava drilled from several of the older Emperor seamounts. Because this magnetism records the latitude at which the lava cooled, it showed that the seamounts had formed well north of Hawaii’s present latitude—and farther north the older they were.

Based on the texts, how would Tarduno’s team (Text 2) most likely respond to the reading of the bend described in Text 1?

Correct choice: They would question it, because it assumes a stationary hot spot, whereas their data indicate that the hot spot moved while the Emperor seamounts were forming.

If the hot spot had stayed in place, every seamount would have formed at the hot spot’s latitude, near Hawaii’s. Text 2 finds that the older Emperor seamounts formed progressively farther north, which implies that the hot spot drifted south over time. That undercuts the fixed-hot-spot assumption on which Text 1’s reading depends.

A: Text 2 shows that the hot spot moved, which weakens Text 1’s reading, but it does not show a change in the hot spot’s direction and does not rule out a change in the plate’s; this goes beyond what the data show.

B: The magnetism records where the lava cooled, not where the seamounts sit now. A plate carrying seamounts away from a fixed hot spot would still produce seamounts that all formed at the same latitude.

D: A single hot spot that moved would also produce seamounts that formed at different latitudes, so the data do not require a second hot spot.

Watch for: Distinguish where a seamount formed (what the magnetism records) from where it is now; only the first bears on whether the hot spot moved.

The following text is from Henry Adams’s 1918 autobiography The Education of Henry Adams. Adams attended Harvard College in the 1850s.

Harvard College, as far as it educated at all, was a mild and liberal school, which sent young men into the world with all they needed to make respectable citizens, and something of what they wanted to make useful ones. Leaders of men it never tried to make. [...] In effect, the school created a type but not a will. Four years of Harvard College, if successful, resulted in an autobiographical blank, a mind on which only a water-mark had been stamped. The stamp, as such things went, was a good one. [...] It taught little, and that little ill, but it left the mind open, free from bias, ignorant of facts, but docile. The graduate had few strong prejudices. He knew little, but his mind remained supple, ready to receive knowledge.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Correct choice: Adams judges that Harvard gave its students a respectable character and receptive minds but little knowledge and no strong sense of purpose.

Adams says Harvard made “respectable citizens” of a recognizable “type” but “not a will,” and that it “taught little, and that little ill”; yet it left minds “open,” “free from bias,” and “ready to receive knowledge,” and its stamp “was a good one.” His judgment is mixed: a sound but thin education.

A: Adams does credit Harvard with open minds, but he says “Leaders of men it never tried to make,” so he does not claim it prepared graduates to lead.

C: Adams does credit Harvard with forming character, but he says the graduate’s mind “remained supple, ready to receive knowledge,” so the teaching did not reduce students’ ability to learn.

D: Adams presents the lack of strong prejudices as a merit (“free from bias”), and he calls Harvard’s stamp “a good one,” so he does not treat its moderation as its chief failing.

Watch for: Adams’s irony mixes criticism with praise; a choice that keeps one half of his judgment and reverses the other misstates the main idea.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 10 of 27

When touched or jolted, the leaves of the sensitive plant Mimosa pudica fold shut, a response thought to discourage plant-eating animals. In a 2014 study, ecologist Monica Gagliano and colleagues repeatedly dropped potted Mimosa plants a short distance onto a cushioned surface. At first the leaves folded after each drop, but after several drops many plants stopped folding in response. The researchers concluded that the plants had learned to ignore a harmless disturbance rather than simply having exhausted the energy needed to keep folding.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support the researchers’ conclusion?

Correct choice: Plants that had stopped folding after repeated drops folded their leaves fully when they were then shaken, a disturbance they had not experienced before.

The rival explanation is exhaustion: a plant that had used up the energy needed to fold could not fold in response to anything. If plants that ignored drops still folded fully when shaken, they clearly still had that energy; they had stopped responding specifically to the harmless drops, as the researchers concluded.

B: Folding again after a long rest is what exhaustion would predict, since an exhausted plant would recover its energy over time; this finding does not favor learning.

C: Plants with less energy giving up sooner fits the exhaustion explanation at least as well as the learning explanation, so it cannot show that the plants learned rather than tired.

D: How quickly plants fold after drops of different heights says nothing about why the plants eventually stopped responding to repeated drops.

Watch for: To support one explanation over a rival, look for a finding the rival cannot explain; a new disturbance that still triggers folding shows that the plants were still able to fold.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 13 of 27

Most planetary scientists think that the Moon formed from debris thrown out when a body about the size of Mars struck the young Earth. Computer simulations of such a collision typically predict that most of the Moon’s material came from the impacting body rather than from Earth. Yet rocks brought back by the Apollo missions have almost exactly the same proportions of oxygen isotopes as rocks on Earth, whereas meteorites from Mars and most meteorites from the asteroid belt have proportions measurably different from Earth’s. If the impact explanation is correct, the Apollo results suggest that ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Correct choice: the impacting body resembled Earth in composition, or the collision mixed the two bodies’ material more thoroughly than simulations suggest.

Because other bodies in the solar system differ from Earth in their isotope proportions, a Moon made mostly of impactor material should differ from Earth too. It does not. Keeping the impact explanation therefore requires either an impactor that happened to resemble Earth or far more mixing of material than the typical simulations assume.

A: Nothing in the text suggests that the Apollo samples came from an unusual region; this proposes an untested sampling problem instead of drawing a conclusion from the evidence and the impact explanation.

B: The Apollo results are surprising precisely because a Moon made mostly of impactor material would be expected to differ from Earth; the results do not suggest that the simulations are right.

C: Nothing in the text suggests the samples were contaminated, and such a claim would dismiss the results rather than draw a conclusion from them.

Watch for: When a completion begins “If the explanation is correct,” keep the explanation and ask what else must be true for it to fit the new evidence.

The Dead Sea Scrolls, found in caves near Qumran between 1947 and 1956, are the remains of roughly 900 manuscripts, ______ survive only as fragments that scholars have spent decades piecing together.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

The thousands of life-size clay soldiers buried near the tomb of Qin Shi Huang, China’s first emperor, were originally painted in bright colors, but the paint often begins to curl and flake within minutes of exposure to air. Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct choice: is

When every introduces each part of a compound subject joined by and, the subject is treated as singular: every fragment and every flake … is recorded. The present tense also matches attempt.

D: Comparing nonconsenting children with vaccinated children mixes the effect of consent with the effect of the vaccine, so it cannot show that consent itself marked higher risk.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 14 of 27

In 1958 Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl grew bacteria for many generations on a nutrient containing a heavy form of nitrogen, making their DNA dense, and then moved them to a nutrient containing only ordinary, lighter nitrogen. After one round of DNA replication, all of the DNA had a single, intermediate density. This ruled out a model in which each original DNA molecule stays intact and the copy is built entirely from new material, but it fit two others: one in which each new molecule has one original strand and one new strand, and one in which old and new material are mixed throughout both strands. After a second round, the DNA formed two bands, one of intermediate density and one light. This second result ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Correct choice: favored the model with one original and one new strand, since the mixed model predicts a single band lying between intermediate and light.

Under the mixed model, every molecule after two rounds would contain the same small share of original material, giving one band between intermediate and light. Under the one-original-strand model, half the molecules would still carry an original strand (intermediate) and half would be entirely new (light). The two observed bands match the second model only.

B: The mixed model predicts a single band, not two, so the result counts against it rather than for it.

C: The intact-original model predicts a band of fully dense DNA and no intermediate band; the observed intermediate band still rules it out.

D: Only the one-original-strand model predicts a fully light band; the mixed model does not, so the two bands do distinguish the models.

This process suggests that the increase in leg length at the front ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Correct choice: could occur even if long-legged toads at the front survived and reproduced no better than their short-legged neighbors.

Nothing in it requires long legs to improve survival or reproduction, so leg length at the front could rise even without such an advantage.

A: This is the intuitive explanation, but the process the researchers describe works through who breeds with whom at the front and does not require any survival advantage.

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 16 of 27

Like other E. coli, the bacteria could consume glucose but could not use citrate when oxygen was present. After more than 30,000 generations, bacteria in one population evolved the ability to use citrate; samples taken earlier contained no bacteria that could do so. To learn why, researchers restarted that population’s evolution many times from single cells isolated from frozen samples taken at different generations, growing every restarted culture under the same conditions for the same number of generations. Citrate users evolved again only in cultures started from samples taken after roughly generation 20,000. This result suggests that ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Correct choice: mutations that had arisen in that population by about generation 20,000, though they did not themselves allow citrate use, made that ability possible later.

Every restarted culture had the same conditions and time, yet only those begun from later samples produced citrate users. Something in the population’s history by about generation 20,000 must have made the ability possible—earlier mutations that set the stage without themselves allowing citrate use, since no earlier sample could use citrate.

A: The result concerns the history of one population; nothing suggests that every population reaches the same point after 20,000 generations, and the other eleven had not evolved the ability.

B: The restarted cultures began from single cells, and the text says samples taken before generation 30,000 contained no bacteria that could use citrate, so citrate use had not already arisen and gone undetected; it evolved again in the new cultures.

C: A chance event unrelated to the population’s earlier history could have happened in cultures started from any generation, yet citrate users arose only from cells taken after about generation 20,000.

Correct choice: will have rung

The sentence looks ahead to a point in the future (2040) and describes ringing that will be complete by then. After a real condition in the present tense (If it keeps ringing), the future perfect, will have rung, expresses an action completed by a future time.

B: Has rung describes ringing up to the present, but the two full centuries will be reached only in 2040.

Correct choice: a

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: On the other hand,

The last sentence describes the other group in the comparison—people exposed early rather than late—and their different outcome. On the other hand introduces this contrasting case.

B: Nevertheless signals that something holds despite what came before, but the early group’s outcomes are not a surprising exception to the late group’s; they are a second, different case.

C: Instead signals that one thing takes the place of another, but the early group’s outcomes do not replace the late group’s; both are findings of the same comparison.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct choice: In turn,

The ancient seed produced a tree, and that tree then supplied the pollen that let another ancient palm bear fruit. In turn marks this next step, in which the result of one step becomes the means of the next.

A: Meanwhile signals that two things happened at the same time, but the pollination happened “later,” after Methuselah had grown into a tree.
