Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

Historian Elina Moss offered a qualified endorsement of a new atlas. She praised its careful mapping of trade routes but noted that its discussion of seasonal travel relied on only two surviving journals. The atlas, she concluded, was a useful starting point rather than a definitive account.

As used in the text, what does the word "qualified" most nearly mean?

Words in Context | Review lesson 3.3

Correct choice: Limited by reservations

Moss praises part of the atlas while identifying a limitation and refusing to call it definitive. Her endorsement is therefore limited by reservations.

A: Qualified can describe someone with credentials, but here it describes the nature of an endorsement.

C: The praise and reservation are stated directly.

D: The explicit limitation rules out an entirely enthusiastic endorsement.

If you missed it: Substitute the proposed meaning into the sentence and compare it with the rest of the passage.

Strategy in use: The contrast between praise and limitation gives the needed meaning; professional status does not.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

The first tests of a ceramic sensor produced equivocal results. At low temperatures, its readings closely matched those of a reference instrument; at higher temperatures, the two instruments sometimes agreed and sometimes diverged sharply. The team therefore postponed its judgment about the new sensor's reliability.

As used in the text, what does the word "equivocal" most nearly mean?

Words in Context | Review lesson 3.3

Correct choice: Inconclusive

The mixed pattern leaves the team unable to reach a firm judgment. Inconclusive captures that uncertainty without assigning intent to the results.

A: Nothing suggests anyone deliberately misled the team.

B: Some readings agree, but others diverge; the overall results do not establish precision.

C: The text describes the results' uncertainty, not an unusually large amount of testing.

If you missed it: Identify what the surrounding sentences explain about the tested word.

Strategy in use: Use the consequence, postponing judgment, to confirm the meaning rather than relying on a familiar-looking root.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 3 of 27

To record the faint vibrations of a bridge cable, engineer Pavel Imani placed a flexible layer between the recording unit and its metal support. The layer was intended to attenuate vibrations traveling through the support, allowing the unit to register the cable's movement without those stronger disturbances overwhelming it.

As used in the text, what does the word "attenuate" most nearly mean?

Words in Context | Review lesson 3.3

Correct choice: Reduce

The unwanted support vibrations would overwhelm the desired signal. The flexible layer is intended to reduce those disturbances.

B: Generating additional support vibrations would worsen the stated problem.

C: Cataloging means recording or listing them; the layer is meant to change their strength.

D: The passage does not describe aligning the timing of different vibrations.

If you missed it: Describe the physical job in ordinary language before choosing the vocabulary answer.

Strategy in use: Technical names can remain unfamiliar when the functional relationship is clear.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 4 of 27

Early catalogues often described the woven panels of the fictional Laren workshop simply as decorative wall coverings. Recent examination, however, has revealed regularly spaced loops along their upper edges and wear concentrated at their lower corners. These features suggest that at least some panels were repeatedly raised and lowered, perhaps to divide rooms. Their visual appeal may thus have accompanied a practical use that the catalogues overlooked.

Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?

Text Structure and Purpose | Review lesson 3.3

Correct choice: To present evidence for a possible use of some panels beyond decoration

The text contrasts a decorative classification with physical evidence supporting a possible room-dividing use. Its tentative language and the phrase at least some limit the claim.

A: The mechanism is not established, and the claim is not about all panels.

B: The final sentence allows visual appeal and practical use to coexist.

D: An omission is identified, but no deliberate concealment is alleged.

If you missed it: Summarize what the author does with the evidence, preserving its level of certainty.

Strategy in use: A purpose answer must capture the passage's central move, not magnify a tentative explanation.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 5 of 27

Biologist Ren Osei suspected that a pale shell coating protected a snail species from heat. In one trial, coated shells remained cooler than uncoated shells under a lamp. When the lamp was removed, however, the two groups cooled at nearly the same rate. Osei concluded that the coating's main effect in this setup was to limit heat gain rather than to accelerate heat loss.

Which choice best describes the function of the bold sentence in the text as a whole?

Text Structure and Purpose | Review lesson 3.3

Correct choice: It helps distinguish two possible explanations of the coating's thermal effect.

Similar cooling rates after the lamp is removed help separate limiting heat gain from speeding heat loss. That distinction supports the conclusion that follows.

B: The sentence gives no information about species selection.

C: The preceding sentence reports a temperature difference under the lamp.

D: The sentence supplies experimental evidence, not a separate application.

If you missed it: Connect the tested sentence both to what precedes it and to the conclusion that follows.

Strategy in use: Function questions ask what a sentence contributes to the argument, not merely what it mentions.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 6 of 27

One account credits a single editor with making the Orlan journal accessible to a broad readership. The editor did shorten technical articles and add explanatory diagrams. Yet letters from the journal's printers show that workshop readers had requested such changes for years, and several of the new diagrams were adapted from their sketches. The editor's contribution was substantial, but the journal's transformation was also shaped by its audience.

Which choice best describes the overall structure of the text?

Text Structure and Purpose | Review lesson 3.3

Correct choice: It acknowledges evidence for one account, then adds evidence that qualifies that account's emphasis.

The text first gives an editor-centered account, acknowledges the editor's actions, and then presents readers' contributions. The final sentence retains the editor's importance while qualifying the single-person emphasis.

A: The editor's contribution is explicitly retained, and audience participation is directly related to the change.

B: Both contributions have supporting evidence; the author does evaluate how they should be understood.

D: The passage is not a failed-process chronology.

If you missed it: Track claim, concession, counterevidence, and revised conclusion as separate roles.

Strategy in use: A qualification does not automatically amount to a rejection.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 7 of 27

TEXT 1

Literary scholar Ada Sen argues that private notebooks are unreliable guides to the order in which poet Tomas Vale composed his poems. A notebook may contain an early draft of one poem beside a much later revision of another. Sen therefore cautions against treating page order as a direct record of composition.

TEXT 2

Scholar Ivo Neri agrees that notebook page order can mislead. He nevertheless uses Vale's notebooks alongside dated letters: when a letter quotes a draft passage, it can help establish when that version existed. For Neri, the notebooks become chronologically useful when supported by independent evidence.

Based on the texts, how would Neri most likely respond to Sen's caution?

Cross-Text Connections | Review lesson 3.4

Correct choice: By accepting it while explaining that corroborating evidence can make the notebooks useful for chronology

Neri accepts Sen's caution against treating page order as a direct chronology. He adds that dated letters can corroborate when particular draft versions existed, making the notebooks useful alongside that independent evidence.

A: Neri agrees that page order can mislead.

C: Neri uses letters as supporting evidence; he does not rank them as universally less reliable.

D: The discussion concerns draft versions, not only final poems.

If you missed it: Write a brief position for each text, then identify the exact agreement and difference.

Strategy in use: Do not force a paired-text question into total agreement or total disagreement.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 8 of 27

TEXT 1

A study found that a desert plant continued transporting water after several days of drought, even though the number of open pores in its leaves had declined. Researcher Mara Pell proposes that the plant temporarily changes the permeability of internal membranes. She views the continued transport as evidence consistent with that mechanism.

TEXT 2

Researcher Theo Rao notes that the study measured whole-plant transport and pore counts, but not membrane permeability. He suggests that changes in the geometry of internal water channels could also explain the transport. Direct measurements of membranes and channels would help distinguish these possibilities.

Based on the texts, which statement would Rao most likely make about Pell's proposed mechanism?

Cross-Text Connections | Review lesson 3.4

Correct choice: It remains possible, but the reported measurements do not distinguish it from another explanation.

Rao offers an alternative mechanism and identifies missing measurements that could distinguish the explanations. He does not rule Pell's mechanism out; he argues that the current evidence is insufficiently discriminating.

A: Rao does not claim fewer pores make the proposed membrane change impossible.

B: His request for discriminating measurements shows that confirmation has not been established.

C: He explicitly proposes direct measurements as the next useful evidence.

If you missed it: Separate evidence consistent with a hypothesis from evidence that rules out competing hypotheses.

Strategy in use: The decisive difference lies in what the measurement can establish, not in the shared technical topic.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 9 of 27

A town archive began transcribing a collection of shipping records with software designed to recognize handwriting. The software quickly located repeated dates and cargo labels, but it frequently misread abbreviations used by only one clerk. Volunteers familiar with those abbreviations corrected the errors. Together, the automated search and the volunteers' knowledge made the records easier to use than either approach had alone.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Central Ideas and Details | Review lesson 3.1

Correct choice: Combining automated processing with specialized human knowledge improved access to the records.

The passage describes complementary strengths: software efficiently finds repeated features, while informed volunteers correct unfamiliar abbreviations. Their combination improves access.

A: The errors discussed are recognition errors, and the records become more usable.

B: The problem specifically involves abbreviations associated with one clerk.

D: The software recognized repeated dates and continued to contribute.

If you missed it: Include both the main activity and the passage's conclusion in your summary.

Strategy in use: A detail about errors must not replace the larger point about complementary methods.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 10 of 27

Composer Nila Arden initially wrote her harbor suite for a large ensemble. When a smaller group commissioned a performance, she reassigned several wind melodies to strings and omitted two percussion parts. She retained the suite's sequence of movements, however, explaining that the order was essential to its gradual shift from stillness to activity. The revised version therefore changed the sound of individual passages while preserving the work's larger progression.

According to the text, why did Arden retain the original sequence of movements?

Central Ideas and Details | Review lesson 3.1

Correct choice: She considered that sequence necessary to the suite's overall development.

Arden explains that the order is essential to the gradual shift from stillness to activity. That is a statement about the work's overall development.

B: The text discusses reassigned wind melodies, not a performance restriction on their order.

C: Two percussion parts were omitted, and no such prohibition appears.

D: Individual passages sound different in the revised version.

If you missed it: Locate the sentence that directly supplies the requested reason, then check for distortions in the paraphrase.

Strategy in use: A detail question may require less inference than its distractors invite.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

At three excavation sites, researchers found similar clay vessels. Only vessels from Site H contained traces of a particular dye. Because the dye breaks down rapidly in damp soil, the researchers compared burial conditions: Site H was unusually dry, while the other two sites had remained damp for centuries. The researchers caution that the absence of detectable dye at those two sites ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Inferences | Review lesson 3.1

Correct choice: does not by itself establish that the vessels there were never used with the dye.

Damp conditions could have destroyed traces of dye. Therefore, failure to detect dye at the damp sites does not establish that dye was never present.

A: No manufacturing dates are supplied.

B: Preservation conditions do not locate the dye's production.

C: The evidence does not establish different uses, much less a universal rule.

If you missed it: Ask whether absence of observed evidence could result from how evidence is preserved or measured.

Strategy in use: The supported inference is a limit on a conclusion, not a new historical claim.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 12 of 27

A team used a computer model to estimate the spread of a shrub across a valley. The model assumed that seeds traveled only with the prevailing wind. It predicted no seedlings west of the existing plants. A later field survey found several clusters of young shrubs to the west. The team concluded that, for this valley, ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Inferences | Review lesson 5.1

Correct choice: the model did not fully account for the observed distribution of young shrubs.

The model predicts no western seedlings, but the field survey finds western clusters. The model therefore does not fully account for the observations. The mismatch does not identify which model feature needs revision or establish a particular alternative mechanism.

A: A mismatch between prediction and observation does not show that wind played no role.

C: Deliberate planting is an invented explanation; other possibilities have not been excluded.

D: Disagreement is a reason to examine the model and evidence, not automatically reject observations.

If you missed it: Choose the conclusion established by the mismatch before inventing its cause.

Strategy in use: A tempting answer may diagnose a real problem and then add an unsupported explanation.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 13 of 27

A literary critic argues that, in an original novel about an apprentice mapmaker, the apprentice's growing confidence is shown through increasing willingness to question a teacher's decisions. The critic wants evidence of a change in the apprentice's behavior rather than evidence that the teacher's maps were inaccurate.

Which hypothetical quotation from the novel would most effectively support the critic's argument?

Command of Evidence: Textual | Review lesson 3.2

Correct choice: "In spring, she had copied his lines without a word; by autumn, she was asking him to explain every boundary she found doubtful."

The quotation contrasts earlier unquestioning copying with later requests for explanations. That change directly supplies the behavior the critic identifies as a sign of growing confidence.

A: An inaccurate map does not show a change in the apprentice's willingness to question the teacher.

B: Preparation habits do not establish the relevant behavioral change.

C: The visual setting does not support the argument about confidence.

If you missed it: Turn the claim into two required pieces: a change over time and increased questioning.

Strategy in use: Relevant subject matter is weaker than evidence that matches the exact claim.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 14 of 27

Researchers propose that visitors spend longer at a museum display when its label poses a question about the object than when the label states the same background facts without a question. They compare two labels of equal length for the same object, assigning visitors at random to view one label or the other. They measure each visitor's viewing time.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support the researchers' proposal?

Command of Evidence: Textual | Review lesson 3.2

Correct choice: Visitors assigned to the question label spent more time at the display on average than visitors assigned to the factual label.

The proposal predicts a difference in viewing time associated with the question label. A higher average viewing time in that group directly matches the prediction in the described comparison.

A: Recall one week later is a different outcome from time spent at the display.

C: Staff preference is not the measured visitor behavior.

D: The presence of both visitor types does not establish the predicted difference in viewing time.

If you missed it: Match the evidence to the exact outcome and comparison named in the proposal.

Strategy in use: Do not exchange the measured variable for a related but different benefit.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 16 of 27

A narrow channel once connected the harbor to an inland lake. By 1930, deposited sand had blocked the ______ however, small boats could still reach the lake through a second, shallower passage to the east.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Boundaries | Review lesson 4.1

Correct choice: channel;

The statements on both sides of the blank are independent clauses. A semicolon joins them correctly, with however followed by its existing comma. However is a transition, not a coordinating conjunction.

B: A comma alone cannot join these two independent clauses; however does not repair the comma splice.

C: Without punctuation, the clauses form a run-on.

D: And would need appropriate boundary punctuation before it; as printed, channel and however does not supply the required clause boundary.

If you missed it: Locate the two subject-verb cores before choosing punctuation.

Strategy in use: The transition's meaning does not determine whether it can join clauses grammatically.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 17 of 27

At the annual exhibit, Mira Lan, the director of the restoration ______ the newly repaired tapestry to visitors. Her presentation focused on the small areas of original fabric that the team had preserved.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Boundaries | Review lesson 4.1

Correct choice: program, unveiled

The phrase the director of the restoration program is a nonessential appositive identifying Mira Lan. The comma after her name opens that phrase, and the comma in B closes it before the main verb unveiled.

A: It leaves the appositive's opening comma unmatched.

C: A semicolon cannot serve as the closing partner of the appositive's opening comma.

D: A colon cannot close this inserted appositive before the main verb.

If you missed it: Temporarily remove the inserted description: Mira Lan unveiled the tapestry.

Strategy in use: Track punctuation already printed outside the blank; the answer must complete that structure.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 18 of 27

To make the archive easier to search, the team sorted its documents into three ______ letters, technical reports, and application forms. Each group received a separate index before the documents were scanned.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Boundaries | Review lesson 4.1

Correct choice: categories: personal

The words before the colon form a complete clause, and the words after it name the three categories. A colon correctly introduces that explanatory list.

A: A semicolon requires an independent clause after it, but the list has no subject-verb structure.

B: The period leaves the list as a sentence fragment.

C: The list lacks the punctuation needed to connect it to the preceding complete clause.

If you missed it: Read the material before a proposed colon alone, then verify what follows explains or specifies it.

Strategy in use: A colon can introduce a list because of the structure of the sentence, not merely because several nouns follow.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 19 of 27

Throughout the winter, the sensors that record changes in the tunnel's air ______ their measurements in a local memory unit. Technicians retrieve those measurements during scheduled visits because the tunnel has no reliable wireless connection.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Boundaries | Review lesson 4.1

Correct choice: temperature store

The main subject is the sensors, modified by that record changes in the tunnel's air temperature. Store is the main verb. No punctuation should separate this complete subject from its verb.

B: The comma wrongly divides the subject from its main verb.

C: The words before the semicolon are not an independent clause; the main verb has not yet appeared.

D: The material before the colon is likewise not a complete clause.

If you missed it: Identify the main verb separately from a verb inside a relative clause.

Strategy in use: A long subject is still a subject; its length does not create a punctuation boundary.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 20 of 27

Although the museum has digitized most of its photographs, each of the three albums donated by the local historical society ______ available for in-person study today. Visitors may request an album at the reference desk.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Form, Structure, and Sense | Review lesson 4.2

Correct choice: remains

Each is the singular head of the subject. The present-tense singular verb remains agrees with it and fits the statement about availability today.

A: Remain does not agree with singular each.

B: Have remained uses a plural auxiliary with the singular subject.

D: Were remaining also uses a plural form and does not fit the stated present description.

If you missed it: Find the head of the subject rather than matching the verb to a nearby plural noun.

Strategy in use: The phrase of the three albums does not make each plural.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 21 of 27

The conservators finished cataloging the collection in 2009. By the time the new gallery opened in 2011, they ______ that work and had begun designing an exhibit about the collection's history.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Form, Structure, and Sense | Review lesson 5.3

Correct choice: had completed

The cataloging was complete before another past event, the gallery's opening. Had completed expresses that earlier completion and is consistent with had begun.

A: Will complete incorrectly places the work in the future.

B: Complete is a present-tense form that conflicts with the dated past sequence.

D: Have completed is present perfect; the sentence locates the completion before a specified past event.

If you missed it: Draw the order mentally: completion in 2009, then gallery opening in 2011.

Strategy in use: If you first chose D because it sounded natural, the dates and the past reference point give a specific reason to change.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 22 of 27

Using photographs taken before the flood, ______ The reconstruction helped the town identify stretches of riverbank that had shifted and plan a more detailed survey of the affected area.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Form, Structure, and Sense | Review lesson 4.2

Correct choice: researchers reconstructed the former shoreline.

The opening modifier describes the people using photographs. B places researchers immediately after it, so the modifier attaches logically to the subject who performs that action.

A: The grammatical subject is the shoreline, which is not using photographs; adding by researchers later does not fix the dangling modifier.

C: A reconstruction is not the actor using photographs.

D: The subject is the shoreline's position, not the researchers who use photographs.

If you missed it: After an introductory action phrase, identify who or what performs that action.

Strategy in use: A familiar passive construction can conceal a modifier error; check attachment rather than fluency.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 23 of 27

The first folding screen was made from a sheet so thin that its designers expected it to tear after repeated bending. ______, the sheet remained intact through five hundred test cycles, prompting the team to investigate what gave the material its durability.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Transitions | Review lesson 4.3

Correct choice: Nevertheless

The observed durability holds despite the expectation of tearing. Nevertheless expresses that concession and reversal of expectation.

A: The sheet's durability was not a consequence of the designers expecting it to tear.

B: Remaining intact does not illustrate the prediction of tearing.

C: The outcome does not repeat the expected pattern.

If you missed it: Paraphrase the two propositions: expected failure; observed survival.

Strategy in use: Read the actual direction of the claims rather than treating all material-testing sentences as related in the same way.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 24 of 27

The restoration team changed the recipe for the mortar in one carefully defined way. ______, it replaced one quarter of the original sand with crushed brick while keeping the amounts of lime and water unchanged.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Transitions | Review lesson 4.3

Correct choice: Specifically

The second sentence specifies the single change announced in the first. It identifies the substituted material and confirms that the other stated amounts stayed the same.

B: The second sentence explains the announced change; it does not replace that announcement with an alternative action.

C: The sentence describes the change just introduced, not a separate earlier event.

D: The substitution is the change itself, not a consequence produced by making a different change.

If you missed it: Distinguish a precise description of an action from an outcome caused by that action.

Strategy in use: A shared event across two sentences often signals explanation or specification, but confirm the exact relationship.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 25 of 27

A landslide blocked the only road to the mountain observatory, leaving a helicopter as the sole available means of delivering replacement equipment. ______, the next shipment of instruments had to be transported by air.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Transitions | Review lesson 4.3

Correct choice: Consequently

The loss of the road and the stated sole alternative necessitate air transport. Consequently introduces that practical result.

A: There is no second parallel case; the shipment responds to the stated access problem.

C: Air transport follows from the stated access constraint; it does not happen despite a reason to expect otherwise.

D: The next shipment follows the blockage; the text does not place it earlier.

If you missed it: Identify which proposition supplies the reason and which states what follows.

Strategy in use: The words sole available and had to make the cause-and-result connection explicit.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 26 of 27

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. Designer Irena Sol made two prototypes of a portable display stand, Model K and Model L.

2. Both prototypes used the same type and amount of aluminum.

3. Model K required a wrench to connect its separate frame pieces.

4. Model L used hinges, allowing the frame to unfold without tools.

5. The prototypes were shown at the same regional design exhibition.

The student wants to emphasize a difference in how the two prototypes are assembled. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Rhetorical Synthesis | Review lesson 4.4

Correct choice: Whereas Model K's separate frame pieces required a wrench for connection, Model L's hinged frame could be unfolded without tools.

C directly compares the assembly methods: connecting separate pieces with a wrench versus unfolding a hinged frame without tools. It gives the relevant feature of both prototypes.

A: The shared material and exhibition are accurate but do not explain the assembly difference.

B: This states shared material quantity and function, not different assembly methods.

D: It explains Model L's method but supplies no corresponding assembly fact about Model K.

If you missed it: Underline the requested comparison dimension and locate one matching fact for each subject.

Strategy in use: Two subjects appearing in a sentence do not guarantee that the sentence actually compares them on the requested feature.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 27 of 27

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. A team studied visitor behavior in a gallery before and after replacing text-only labels with labels that combined text and diagrams.

2. Average viewing time rose from four minutes to six minutes during the study.

3. The team proposed that the diagrams contributed to the increase.

4. The mix of visiting groups also changed between the two observation periods.

5. The study did not isolate the diagrams' effect from changes in the visiting groups.

The student wants to report the observed change in viewing time while explaining why it cannot be attributed conclusively to the diagrams. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Rhetorical Synthesis | Review lesson 5.2

Correct choice: Average viewing time rose from four to six minutes, but changes in visiting groups prevented the study from isolating the diagrams' contribution.

A reports the measured change and the specific attribution limit. The study could not separate a diagram effect from the changed visitor mix; that uncertainty does not prove the diagrams had no effect.

B: This accurately attributes a proposed contribution to the team, but omits the requested explanation of why that attribution is inconclusive.

C: Failure to isolate an effect does not establish that no effect occurred.

D: The two changes are accurately named, but the answer does not report the viewing-time result or explain the attribution limit.

If you missed it: Check the two required jobs separately: report the result, then explain the limit on its interpretation.

Strategy in use: The closest rival may preserve attribution but still leave part of a compound goal unfinished.
