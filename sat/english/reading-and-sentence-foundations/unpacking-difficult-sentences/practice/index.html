Question 5

  • Guide question 2.05
  • Foundation

The catalog that the volunteers assembled from photographs of objects stored in the basement contains several entries absent from the museum's earlier inventory.

Which choice states the sentence's main claim?

Why this answer

Find the main claim

Correct choice: The new catalog includes entries missing from the earlier inventory.

The main structure is 'The catalog contains several entries absent from the earlier inventory.' Intervening material describes its assembly.

A: Neither the photographers nor comprehensive coverage are established.

C: The objects, not the earlier inventory, were in the basement.

D: No move is explained.

If you missed it: Find the subject and main verb before unpacking descriptions.

Question 6

  • Guide question 2.06
  • Foundation

The apartments that overlook the courtyard retain their original windows; the apartments facing the street have replacement windows.

Which statement accurately preserves the distinction?

Why this answer

Preserve a restrictive description

Correct choice: The courtyard-facing apartments retain original windows, unlike the street-facing apartments.

The two descriptions identify different groups with different window conditions.

A: This incorrectly extends a restricted claim to every apartment.

B: This assigns original windows to the wrong group.

C: This reverses the courtyard apartments' stated window condition.

If you missed it: Pair each apartment group with its window type.

Question 7

  • Guide question 2.07
  • Transfer

The ceramic panels arrived in wooden crates. After the crates were opened, they were removed one by one and mounted side by side to form a continuous ceramic mural.

What does 'they' refer to?

Why this answer

Resolve a pronoun

Correct choice: The ceramic panels

The ceramic panels are the objects removed from the opened crates and assembled into a ceramic mural. The nearer plural noun 'crates' fits the pronoun's number but not the described action and material.

B: The wooden crates are packaging; they do not form the ceramic mural.

C: Opening the crates does not make their wooden lids parts of a ceramic mural.

D: The mural is assembled from ceramic panels, not from panels together with wooden packaging.

If you missed it: Substitute each candidate and check both the action and the material.

Review lesson: 2.3 Resolve references by meaning, not distance

Question 8

  • Guide question 2.08
  • Transfer

The town published the proposed bus timetable before adopting it and invited riders to identify difficult connections. This led planners to revise two departure times.

What does 'This' most directly refer to?

Why this answer

Identify an event reference

Correct choice: Making the proposal available and seeking riders' input

'This' refers backward to the consultation process, which led to revisions.

A: Publication preceded adoption, and changes followed.

B: This later detail does not identify the consultation process.

D: Revision is the consequence, not the preceding event.

If you missed it: Distinguish the preceding action from its consequence.

Question 9

  • Guide question 2.09
  • Transfer

The unusually neat handwriting may indicate that the page was copied for presentation, but neatness alone cannot establish its intended audience.

Which paraphrase is most accurate?

Why this answer

Retain uncertainty

Correct choice: The handwriting offers a possible clue about the page's purpose but does not identify its audience.

'May indicate' introduces a possible interpretation, and 'cannot establish' limits what that observation can show.

A: 'Proves' changes a tentative interpretation into certainty.

C: Copying is presented as possible, not impossible.

D: No handwriting change or such cause is stated.

If you missed it: Circle possibility words and limits.

Review lesson: 2.4 Preserve contrast, limits, and uncertainty

Question 10

  • Guide question 2.10
  • Transfer

Unlike the sketches preserved in the artist's notebook, the drawings donated by her former assistant depict completed buildings rather than proposed designs.

What distinction does the sentence make?

Why this answer

Track contrasting groups

Correct choice: The donated drawings show completed buildings, while the notebook sketches show proposed designs.

'Unlike' contrasts the collections; 'rather than' identifies completed buildings versus proposed designs as the distinction.

B: This erases the stated contrast.

C: Donating drawings does not establish who designed the buildings.

D: No creation chronology is supplied.

If you missed it: Pair each collection with its subject matter.

Question 11

  • Guide question 2.11
  • Transfer

Little did Ravi expect applause when he entered the hall; he had come merely to retrieve the coat he had forgotten.

Which choice best restates Ravi's expectation and purpose?

Why this answer

Interpret literary inversion

Correct choice: Ravi scarcely expected applause; his purpose was to collect his coat.

'Little did Ravi expect' means he hardly expected applause. 'Merely' limits his purpose to retrieving the coat.

A: 'Little' concerns expectation, not the amount of applause.

B: His stated purpose is collecting the coat.

C: Neither this cause nor anticipation of applause is supported.

If you missed it: Restore ordinary subject-verb order.

Question 12

  • Guide question 2.12
  • Transfer

By the time the caretaker reached the gallery, the damaged frame had been removed, though the painting itself remained on the wall.

Which statement is supported by the sentence?

Why this answer

Respect an unstated agent

Correct choice: The frame had already been removed when the caretaker arrived, but the painting remained.

The earlier action is expressed by 'had been removed.' The contrast specifies that the painting stayed in place.

A: This changes timing and invents an agent.

B: The painting explicitly remained on the wall.

D: The damage's cause and timing are unstated.

If you missed it: Separate the event sequence from unnamed participants.

Question 13

  • Guide question 2.13
  • Challenge

The explanation favored by critics who regard the novel's abrupt ending as accidental depends on a letter whose authenticity is accepted but whose date remains disputed.

Which choice accurately reconstructs the sentence?

Why this answer

Rebuild nested qualifications

Correct choice: Some critics favor an explanation that relies on a letter accepted as authentic but of disputed date.

The critics favor an explanation relying on a letter. They regard the ending as accidental; the letter is accepted as authentic, but its date remains disputed.

B: This reverses what is disputed and invents a cause.

C: A view becomes proven fact, and the uncertainty is misassigned.

D: The critics favor the explanation; the date remains disputed.

If you missed it: Pair each noun with its own details.

Question 14

  • Guide question 2.14
  • Challenge

The reviewers did not object to the model's use of simplified assumptions. Rather, they objected to treating predictions derived from those assumptions as though the assumptions had already been verified. This, they argued, gave the predictions an appearance of certainty they had not earned.

What does 'This' refer to?

Why this answer

Trace a qualified event reference

Correct choice: Treating the predictions as if their underlying assumptions were already verified

'Rather' identifies the objection: treating unverified assumptions as verified when presenting predictions, thereby inflating apparent certainty.

A: The first sentence explicitly excludes simplification itself as the objection.

B: Actual verification differs from treating assumptions as verified.

C: Acknowledging uncertainty opposes the criticized appearance of certainty.

If you missed it: Separate the excluded objection from the actual objection.

Question 15

  • Guide question 2.15
  • Challenge

Although the essays that mention the strike were written after the factory closed, their failure to mention the closure is difficult to interpret: the writers may have assumed that readers already knew of it.

Which paraphrase best preserves the sentence's reasoning?

Why this answer

Combine restriction and a limited conclusion

Correct choice: The relevant essays postdate the closure, but omitting it does not establish ignorance because the writers may have presumed readers knew.

The strike-related essays postdate the closure. Their silence remains ambiguous; presumed reader knowledge is one possible explanation, not a verified motive.

A: This reverses chronology and extends the claim to every essay.

B: Ambiguous silence does not prove ignorance.

D: This turns a possible assumption into a certain motive and fact.

If you missed it: Separate group, timeline, omission, and possible explanation.

Review lesson: 2.6 Paraphrase, then audit your paraphrase

Question 16

  • Guide question 2.16
  • Challenge

The chair remained beside the window, as it had throughout her childhood. It was not its emptiness that unsettled Mira, who had expected to find the room unoccupied, but the new cushion, bright and unfamiliar, resting where her grandfather's worn shawl had always lain.

Which choice best explains what unsettles Mira?

Why this answer

Separate literary implication from invented motive

Correct choice: An unfamiliar cushion has replaced a familiar object in an otherwise familiar setting.

'Not ... but' identifies the unfamiliar cushion, not emptiness, as the source of discomfort. The cushion contrasts with the familiar shawl.

A: Mira explicitly expected an unoccupied room.

C: The chair remains in its familiar position.

D: Her reaction does not establish anyone's hostile intention.

If you missed it: Identify the rejected cause and the stated cause.
