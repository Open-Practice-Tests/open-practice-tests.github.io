Question 5

  • Guide question 1.05
  • Foundation

The apparent similarity between the two artifacts has misled several observers.

Which word is the simple subject of the main clause?

Why this answer

Simple subject

Correct choice: similarity

'Similarity' names what has misled the observers. The complete subject is 'the apparent similarity between the two artifacts.'

A: 'Artifacts' belongs to the prepositional phrase describing the similarity.

C: 'Observers' is the central noun in the object: the people who were misled.

D: 'Apparent' is an adjective modifying 'similarity,' not a noun serving as the subject.

If you missed it: Ask 'What has misled the observers?' rather than choosing the noun nearest the verb.

Question 6

  • Guide question 1.06
  • Foundation

Several of the notes copied into the revised edition appear to contradict the author's earlier account.

Which word is the finite verb of the main clause?

Why this answer

Main finite verb

Correct choice: appear

'Appear' makes the main statement about 'several of the notes.' The infinitive 'to contradict' completes its meaning.

A: 'Copied' is a participle describing which notes are meant.

B: 'Contradict' is part of the nonfinite infinitive 'to contradict.'

C: 'Account' is a noun here: the author's earlier description of events.

If you missed it: Reduce the sentence to 'Several appear to contradict the account.'

Question 7

  • Guide question 1.07
  • Foundation

A collection of maps annotated by nineteenth-century travelers reveals how the port's boundaries changed over time.

Which statement best captures the sentence's grammatical core and meaning?

Why this answer

Main statement

Correct choice: A collection reveals changes in the port's boundaries.

The main subject is 'a collection,' and the main verb is 'reveals.' The final clause identifies what the collection reveals.

B: The travelers annotated maps; the sentence does not say they changed boundaries.

C: This reverses the relationship and invents information about arrival dates.

D: The sentence explicitly refers to boundaries changing over time, not remaining unchanged.

If you missed it: Match the main subject and verb before interpreting the modifiers.

Question 8

  • Guide question 1.08
  • Foundation

The museum displayed a sketch by an architect who later redesigned the town hall.

Which noun does 'who later redesigned the town hall' modify?

Why this answer

Relative clause attachment

Correct choice: architect

The clause describes the architect and supplies additional information about that person.

A: The museum performs the displaying; it is not the person introduced by 'who.'

B: The sketch was created by the architect; it did not redesign the hall.

D: The hall is the object of 'redesigned' inside the relative clause.

If you missed it: Ask who performed the action inside the relative clause.

Question 9

  • Guide question 1.09
  • Transfer

Which option contains a subject and finite verb but cannot stand alone as a complete sentence in its current form?

Why this answer

Dependent clause recognition

Correct choice: While researchers surveyed abandoned orchards

'Researchers' is the subject and 'surveyed' is finite. 'While' makes the clause dependent on another statement.

A: This is a prepositional phrase; it has no finite verb.

B: 'Surveying' is nonfinite here, so the group lacks a finite main predicate.

D: This infinitive phrase has no finite verb or expressed subject.

If you missed it: Check for both a finite verb and a word creating dependence.

Question 10

  • Guide question 1.10
  • Transfer

The researchers measured soil moisture and recorded root growth, but the gardeners supplied the seedlings.

Which description correctly explains the sentence's structure?

Why this answer

Coordination

Correct choice: The researchers perform two coordinated actions; the gardeners perform an action in a separate coordinated clause.

'Measured' and 'recorded' share the subject 'the researchers.' After 'but,' a new subject, 'the gardeners,' begins another independent clause.

B: 'Recorded' still belongs to the researchers' predicate.

C: The last action has a different explicitly stated subject.

D: 'But' marks a contrast; it does not explicitly state a causal relationship.

If you missed it: Draw a boundary where the new subject begins.

Question 11

  • Guide question 1.11
  • Transfer

What the experiment failed to establish was whether the coating would remain transparent after prolonged exposure to sunlight.

What is the complete grammatical subject of 'was'?

Why this answer

Noun clause as subject

Correct choice: What the experiment failed to establish

The opening clause functions as one noun-like unit: the thing the experiment did not establish. 'Was' links that unit to the unresolved issue.

A: 'The experiment' is the subject of 'failed' within the opening clause.

B: The 'whether' clause follows 'was' and identifies the unresolved issue; it is not the subject.

C: 'The coating' is the subject of 'would remain' in the embedded 'whether' clause.

If you missed it: Replace the opening clause with 'the unresolved issue' to see the larger structure.

Question 12

  • Guide question 1.12
  • Transfer

Which option is a complete sentence in standard written English?

Why this answer

Sentence completeness

Correct choice: The survey that the residents requested has finally begun.

The main subject 'the survey' has the finite predicate 'has finally begun.' The relative clause describes the survey.

A: 'Although' makes the only clause dependent; a main clause is missing.

C: 'Requested' is a nonfinite participle here; the noun phrase has no main predicate.

D: 'Began' belongs to a relative clause. The larger noun phrase has no main predicate.

If you missed it: Set aside dependent material and check whether the subject still has a finite predicate.

Question 13

  • Guide question 1.13
  • Challenge

The assumption that a pigment's present appearance faithfully reflects its original color ignores the possibility that chemical reactions, rather than deliberate choices by the artist, account for the contrast between the painted figures.

Which statement best expresses the sentence's main claim?

Why this answer

Main claim beneath embedded content

Correct choice: An assumption about original color overlooks a possible chemical explanation for the contrast.

The main structure is 'the assumption ignores the possibility.' The two embedded clauses explain the assumption and the overlooked alternative.

B: This treats the criticized assumption as a conclusion the sentence endorses.

C: The sentence presents chemical reactions as a possibility, not a proven or universal cause.

D: No intention to create later chemical changes is stated.

If you missed it: Separate what the assumption says from what the sentence says about that assumption.

Question 14

  • Guide question 1.14
  • Challenge

The engineer who inspected the beams that support the balcony concluded that replacing the railing would not address the weakness in the structure.

Which verb anchors the main clause?

Why this answer

Nested relative clauses

Correct choice: concluded

The main subject-verb pair is 'the engineer concluded.' Everything between those words describes the engineer and the beams; the final clause states the conclusion.

A: 'Inspected' belongs to the relative clause modifying 'engineer.'

B: 'Support' belongs to a second relative clause modifying 'beams.'

D: 'Address' occurs in the embedded conclusion and follows the modal 'would.'

If you missed it: Read the nested description outward: beams support; engineer inspected; engineer concluded.

Question 15

  • Guide question 1.15
  • Challenge

That the archive contains no letters written during the composer's final winter does not demonstrate that the composer stopped corresponding; documents from that period may simply have been lost.

Which idea serves as the subject of 'does not demonstrate'?

Why this answer

Clause-sized subject and negation

Correct choice: The archive contains no letters written during the composer's final winter.

The opening 'that' clause packages the archive's lack of letters as the subject. The sentence says this absence does not prove correspondence stopped.

A: This belongs to the second independent clause and offers a possible explanation.

C: This is the conclusion that the lack of letters fails to demonstrate.

D: The sentence does not assert that such letters certainly existed; it presents possible loss.

If you missed it: Replace the entire opening clause with 'this absence,' keeping 'does not demonstrate.'

Question 16

  • Guide question 1.16
  • Challenge

Although the new index captures changes in rental prices more promptly than the annual survey does, the committee that commissioned it cautions that movements in the index should not be treated as evidence of changes in the cost of all housing.

Which version preserves the main clause's message while removing the opening concession and the description of the committee?

Why this answer

Main clause versus concession

Correct choice: The committee cautions that movements in the index should not be treated as evidence of changes in the cost of all housing.

The main subject is 'the committee,' and its main verb is 'cautions.' The final content clause gives the caution, including its essential negation and distinction between rentals and all housing.

A: This preserves the opening concession but omits the main caution.

B: This reverses the opening statement and substitutes a different conclusion.

C: This drops the caution and expands the index's coverage beyond what the sentence supports.

If you missed it: Preserve the main subject, main verb, and content needed to understand the stated warning.
