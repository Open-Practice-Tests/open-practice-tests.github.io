Question 5

  • Guide question 4.05
  • Foundation

Fourteen of the 20 surveyed volunteers supported extending the community garden's opening hours. The survey did not include other garden users.

Which conclusion follows?

Why this answer

Quantity and population

Correct choice: Most surveyed volunteers supported the extension.

Fourteen exceeds half of 20, so a majority of the surveyed volunteers supported the change.

A: The stated count is 14, not 20.

C: The unsurveyed users' views are unknown.

D: Other users might also support the extension.

If you missed it: Keep the denominator and the named population together.

Question 6

  • Guide question 4.06
  • Foundation

Wear on the stone's edge is consistent with its having been used to scrape hides. Similar wear can also result from working plant fibers, so the object's precise use remains uncertain.

Which statement is supported?

Why this answer

Degree of certainty

Correct choice: Scraping hides is one possible explanation for the wear.

The passage identifies hide scraping as compatible with the evidence while retaining an alternative.

A: Two possible uses do not establish two actual uses.

B: The passage does not identify a primary use.

C: It gives no probability ranking between the explanations.

If you missed it: Distinguish a possible explanation from an established history.

Review lesson: 4.2 How strong is the claim?

Question 7

  • Guide question 4.07
  • Foundation

Not all of the translated poems preserve the original poems' rhyme schemes.

Which statement must be true?

Why this answer

Not all

Correct choice: At least one translated poem does not preserve its original rhyme scheme.

'Not all' requires at least one exception to preservation.

B: Zero preserving translations is possible, but not required.

C: The statement supplies no majority.

D: All translations could fail to preserve the rhyme scheme.

If you missed it: Translate 'not all' into 'at least one does not.'

Review lesson: 4.3 Negation and missing evidence

Question 8

  • Guide question 4.08
  • Transfer

Under the museum's rules, a painting may enter the loan program only after passing a condition inspection. Several paintings that passed inspection remain outside the program because their owners declined to participate.

Which statement follows?

Why this answer

Only and requirements

Correct choice: A painting entering the program in accordance with the rules has passed inspection.

Passing inspection is required for compliant entry, even though it does not guarantee entry.

A: Some passing paintings remain outside the program.

B: The passage explicitly provides passing paintings outside the program.

D: The inspection requirement has no stated owner-based exception.

If you missed it: Read 'only after' as a requirement, not a promise.

Review lesson: 4.4 Only, if, and required conditions

Question 9

  • Guide question 4.09
  • Transfer

At the first survey, 42% of residents in East District and 68% in West District supported a proposal. At the second survey, support was 57% in East and 73% in West.

Which comparison is accurate?

Why this answer

Change versus final value

Correct choice: East's percentage-point increase was larger, although West's final support remained higher.

East gained 15 percentage points; West gained five. West still finished higher: 73% versus 57%.

B: A larger gain does not imply a higher final value.

C: Five is smaller than 15.

D: The reported final percentages differ.

If you missed it: Calculate changes and compare final values as separate tasks.

Question 10

  • Guide question 4.10
  • Transfer

Among surveyed office workers, those who took walking breaks reported less afternoon fatigue. Workers chose whether to take breaks, and the study did not measure sleep duration.

Which conclusion is best supported?

Why this answer

Association and causation

Correct choice: Walking breaks were associated with less reported fatigue, but their causal effect was not established.

The survey reports an association. Self-selection and unmeasured sleep leave causal explanations unresolved.

A: It converts an association into prevention.

B: It asserts an unsupported reverse direction.

C: Sleep is a possible factor, not a demonstrated complete explanation.

If you missed it: Do not replace one unproven cause with another.

Review lesson: 4.6 Causal direction and limits of a finding

Question 11

  • Guide question 4.11
  • Transfer

No surviving account mentions a performance of the play before 1710. Many theater records from earlier decades have been lost.

Which conclusion is warranted?

Why this answer

Evidence and absence

Correct choice: A performance before 1710 is not documented in the surviving accounts.

The passage establishes the limits of surviving documentation, not the date of the first performance.

A: The passage does not establish when the first performance occurred, or whether a performance occurred in 1710.

C: It invents both an earlier performance and the content of missing evidence.

D: The contents of the lost records are unknown.

If you missed it: Keep an observation about records distinct from a claim about events.

Review lesson: 4.3 Negation and missing evidence

Question 12

  • Guide question 4.12
  • Challenge

Most of the 40 restored maps display coastlines. None of the maps displaying coastlines is printed on cloth.

Which statement must be true?

Why this answer

Combining quantifiers

Correct choice: More than 20 restored maps are not printed on cloth.

More than 20 maps display coastlines, and every one of those maps is not on cloth. Therefore more than 20 are not on cloth.

A: Not being on cloth does not establish paper, and other maps remain unrestricted.

B: The material of maps without coastlines is unspecified.

D: Maps without coastlines could be printed on cloth.

If you missed it: Apply the second rule only to the group covered by it.

Review lesson: 4.1 Who, how many, and under what conditions?

Question 13

  • Guide question 4.13
  • Challenge

In this experiment, every seed exposed to both moisture and warmth germinated. Some seeds exposed to moisture without warmth also germinated.

Which interpretation follows?

Why this answer

Sufficient versus necessary

Correct choice: Moisture together with warmth was sufficient for germination among the tested seeds, but warmth was not necessary.

All tested seeds with both conditions germinated. Germination without warmth provides a direct counterexample to warmth being necessary. The sufficiency claim concerns this tested setup, not a universal guarantee for seeds in other conditions.

A: Some seeds germinated without warmth.

B: Some successes with moisture alone do not establish success for every seed.

C: No result for seeds without moisture is given.

If you missed it: Use a counterexample to reject necessity; preserve the experiment's limits.

Review lesson: 4.4 Only, if, and required conditions

Question 14

  • Guide question 4.14
  • Challenge

A collection grew from 400 items, including 120 photographs, to 600 items, including 150 photographs.

Which statement accurately describes the change?

Why this answer

Counts versus shares

Correct choice: The number of photographs increased, but their share of the collection decreased.

The count rose from 120 to 150. The original share was 120/400 = 30%. The later share was 150/600 = 25%. The share fell.

B: The collection grew proportionally faster than its photograph count.

C: It confuses a falling proportion with a falling count.

D: Thirty percent and 25% are different.

If you missed it: Write the count and the denominator separately at each time point.

Question 15

  • Guide question 4.15
  • Challenge

Researchers randomly assigned 100 adult volunteers to practice remembering faces or to complete unrelated puzzles for two weeks. Immediately afterward, both groups took the same new-face recognition test; the face-practice group averaged a higher score. No later follow-up test was administered.

Which conclusion is best supported?

Why this answer

Direction, strength, and scope

Correct choice: The findings support better average performance from face practice than from puzzles on the immediate recognition test, without establishing a lasting benefit.

Random assignment supports attributing the average test-score difference to the assigned programs. The comparison concerns performance immediately after training; it does not measure lasting benefit or establish how much each person improved from a baseline.

A: Permanence requires evidence beyond the study period.

B: Participants were assigned rather than choosing groups.

D: A difference in group averages does not establish every individual's result.

If you missed it: Retain the causal evidence without expanding its outcome, duration, or individual scope.

Review lesson: 4.6 Causal direction and limits of a finding

Question 16

  • Guide question 4.16
  • Challenge

For the sampled water, a test reliably detects compound Z whenever its concentration is at least 5 milligrams per liter. The properly performed test did not detect Z. It cannot reliably detect concentrations below that threshold.

Which conclusion is best supported?

Why this answer

Interpreting a negative result

Correct choice: The result supports a concentration below 5 milligrams per liter, while leaving open whether any Z is present.

Given the stated reliable threshold, non-detection supports a concentration below it. Zero and lower positive concentrations remain possible.

A: A low positive concentration could escape detection.

C: The test does not establish an exact below-threshold value.

D: The stated detection threshold makes the negative result informative.

If you missed it: Use the method's stated sensitivity instead of treating all missing evidence alike.
