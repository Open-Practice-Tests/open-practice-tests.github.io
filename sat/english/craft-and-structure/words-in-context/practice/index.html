Question 7

  • Guide question 1.07
  • Foundation

The theater's borrowed lighting board lacked several features of the newer model, and its controls were awkwardly arranged. Even so, it reliably performed every lighting change required by the current production. The director therefore considered it ______ and postponed buying a replacement until the show closed.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Predicting status from use and limitations | Review lesson 1.1

Correct choice: serviceable

A is correct. "Serviceable" means adequate for use or capable of doing the required job. The board performs every needed change despite its inconvenient controls and missing features. That supports adequacy for this production, which explains delaying replacement.

B: "Versatile" means useful for many different purposes. The passage establishes suitability for one production, not a broad range of uses.

C: "Sophisticated" means advanced or highly developed. Missing features and awkward controls do not establish advanced design; the director values adequate function.

D: "Indispensable" means impossible to do without. This particular board is usable, but the passage contemplates replacing it and does not establish that no substitute could work.

If you missed it: Predict the minimum supported claim: "does the needed job." Do not turn adequate performance into technical excellence, wide applicability, or necessity.

Question 8

  • Guide question 1.08
  • Transfer

One critic connected the poem's traveler to a sailor mentioned in an old letter. The connection remained Underlined: tenuous End underlined., however. The letter and poem shared only a common surname, and neither supplied a date, place, or event that could independently link the two figures. The critic acknowledged that firmer evidence was needed.

As used in the text, what does the word "tenuous" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Interpreting a meaning from the full explanation | Review lesson 1.1

Correct choice: Weakly supported

C is correct. The passage explains that the connection rests on one nonunique detail and lacks independent linking evidence. "Tenuous" therefore means weak or insubstantial in support, not unclear in expression or unimportant as a topic.

A: The connection is easy to state: the traveler may be the sailor. The difficulty is establishing it, not expressing it clearly.

B: Nothing indicates when the critic first proposed the connection. Age is unrelated to the stated evidential weakness.

D: A claim can be important yet poorly supported. The passage assesses the strength of the evidence, not the significance of identifying the traveler.

If you missed it: Ask what the explanatory sentences evaluate. Here they evaluate evidence, so your paraphrase must concern support rather than age, clarity, or importance.

Question 9

  • Guide question 1.09
  • Foundation

Unlike the earlier settlement, whose houses clustered around one square, the later community was Underlined: diffuse End underlined.. Small groups of homes appeared beside several distant streams, with substantial stretches of unoccupied land between them. Archaeologists found no single area containing most of the community's buildings.

As used in the text, what does the word "diffuse" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Using examples to define distribution | Review lesson 1.2

Correct choice: Spread across separate locations

D is correct. The distant groups of homes, intervening empty land, and absence of a dominant cluster all describe a dispersed spatial arrangement. The examples define how the buildings are distributed.

A: The passage identifies where homes stood, not differences in their design. Spatial separation does not establish stylistic variation.

B: A community can occupy scattered sites with either a small or a large population. No total population is given.

C: The archaeologists can identify several groups of buildings. The marked word describes the community's arrangement, not how poorly its remains survive.

If you missed it: Extract the property common to every example: separation across space. Avoid substituting a related but unmeasured feature such as population size.

Question 10

  • Guide question 1.10
  • Transfer

The station recorded a short burst of interference on one evening, nothing for eleven days, and then two bursts within an hour. Similar gaps and clusters continued throughout the month, with no stable interval between events. The interference was ______ rather than steadily present or regularly recurring.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Distinguishing irregular occurrence from regular cycles | Review lesson 1.2

Correct choice: sporadic

B is correct. "Sporadic" means occurring occasionally or irregularly. The uneven gaps and clusters, together with the explicit absence of a stable interval, describe irregular occurrence.

A: "Periodic" means recurring at intervals, often as part of a regular pattern. The text explicitly rejects a stable recurrence pattern.

C: "Fleeting" means lasting a very short time. Individual bursts are short, but the blank summarizes the irregular pattern across the month rather than each burst's duration.

D: "Diminishing" means becoming smaller or weaker. The passage reports gaps and clusters but no steady decrease in frequency or strength.

If you missed it: Separate duration from frequency and regularity. A short event can recur regularly or irregularly; the surrounding sentence identifies which feature matters.

Question 11

  • Guide question 1.11
  • Transfer

The reviewer clearly admired the pianist's control, discussing several passages in detail and recommending the recording. Her praise was nonetheless ______. Instead of calling the performance flawless or unprecedented, she chose measured descriptions of its strengths and identified two passages where the interpretation was less convincing.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Reading concession without inventing the opposite attitude | Review lesson 1.3

Correct choice: restrained

D is correct. "Restrained" means controlled or held within limits. The reviewer gives clear, detailed praise while avoiding extravagant claims and acknowledging weaknesses. The concession limits the intensity of approval without canceling it.

A: "Detached" means emotionally uninvolved or removed. The reviewer's clear admiration is compatible with measured praise; the passage does not establish emotional distance.

B: "Equivocal" means unclear or open to conflicting interpretations. The recommendation and stated admiration make the favorable judgment clear despite its limits.

C: "Cursory" means hurried and lacking thoroughness. The reviewer discusses several passages in detail, directly contradicting a superficial treatment.

If you missed it: State both sides of the concession: genuine approval plus controlled expression. Do not translate limited enthusiasm into ambiguity, indifference, or lack of attention.

Question 12

  • Guide question 1.12
  • Transfer

At first, the two costume designers seemed poorly matched: one generated bold shapes rapidly, while the other concentrated on how small seams affected movement. During rehearsals, their approaches proved ______. The first designer supplied inventive forms, and the second made those forms practical for the dancers without sacrificing their visual impact.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Recognizing a productive relationship between differences | Review lesson 1.3

Correct choice: complementary

A is correct. "Complementary" means combining in a way that supplies what the other part lacks or enhances the overall result. The designers retain different strengths, and the practical revisions allow the inventive forms to work in performance.

B: "Convergent" means moving toward a common point or becoming more alike. The approaches remain distinct; their interaction is useful because of that difference.

C: "Interchangeable" means capable of replacing one another. Each designer supplies a different contribution, so the text does not support substituting one approach for the other.

D: "Comparable" means similar enough to be compared or of similar quality. The passage emphasizes how the approaches work together, not a judgment that they are equivalent in quality.

If you missed it: When differences produce a good result, ask whether they disappear or contribute distinct strengths. Here the strengths combine; they do not become identical.

Question 13

  • Guide question 1.13
  • Transfer

The first telescope images seemed to show a second ring around the planet. Later images revealed that a faint reflection inside the instrument could create a similar shape. This possibility did not disprove the second ring's existence, but it led the astronomers to Underlined: temper End underlined. their initial confidence until they could obtain independent observations.

As used in the text, what does the word "temper" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Selecting a familiar verb's contextual sense | Review lesson 1.4

Correct choice: Moderate

B is correct. "Temper" here means moderate or reduce the intensity of something. A possible alternative explanation makes the astronomers less confident without requiring them to reject the ring altogether.

A: "Express" means communicate or show. The passage describes a change in confidence, not merely a decision to state it.

C: "Strengthen" means make firmer or more intense. A possible instrumental reflection weakens the basis for confidence rather than adding support.

D: "Test" means examine to determine validity. Independent observations will help test the interpretation later; the current change is a reduction in confidence.

If you missed it: Keep the present response separate from the planned investigation. The astronomers moderate confidence now and seek additional evidence later.

Question 14

  • Guide question 1.14
  • Transfer

The elevated footbridge Underlined: afforded End underlined. pedestrians an unobstructed view of the marsh. From the lower path, tall reeds concealed most of the water, but from the bridge people could see the entire channel and the islands beyond it. The bridge's height, rather than any change to the vegetation, created the difference.

As used in the text, what does the word "afforded" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Using a verb's object to select its sense | Review lesson 1.4

Correct choice: Provided

C is correct. "Afforded" means provided or made available in this context. The bridge's height gives pedestrians a view that the lower path does not offer.

A: "Financed" means paid for or supplied money for. The sentence concerns a visual opportunity, not the cost of building or visiting the bridge.

B: "Reserved" means kept or set aside for a particular use or recipient. The bridge makes the view available; nothing indicates that it holds the view back or sets it aside exclusively for particular visitors.

D: "Spared" means saved someone from a burden or refrained from harming something. The verb's object is a view being supplied, not a hardship being avoided.

If you missed it: Read the complete pattern: "the bridge afforded pedestrians a view." The recipient and object identify a providing relationship.

Question 15

  • Guide question 1.15
  • Challenge

Measurements taken over six months consistently showed high mineral concentrations beside one riverbank near a natural spring. Samples from the opposite bank and from sites farther downstream remained low throughout the study. Although the spring continued to supply mineral-rich water, the researchers described the elevated concentrations in the river as ______.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Distinguishing spatial limits from duration and degree | Review lesson 1.5

Correct choice: localized

A is correct. "Localized" means confined to a particular area. The high concentrations persist beside one bank while the other sampled locations remain low. Their distribution supports a spatial description even though the mineral source continues to operate.

B: "Transient" means short-lived. The pattern consistently appears over six months, and the conclusion concerns location rather than a brief duration.

C: "Marginal" can mean slight or of limited significance. The concentrations are described as high; being restricted to one location does not make them small or unimportant.

D: "Residual" means remaining after something else has ended or been removed. The text provides no earlier event or removal process that would make the minerals a remainder.

If you missed it: When several choices imply a limit, name the dimension of the limit: location, time, magnitude, or what remains. Only location is established here.

Question 16

  • Guide question 1.16
  • Challenge

A researcher proposed that a change in the pottery's color resulted from altered firing conditions rather than a new clay source. The surviving kiln remains were consistent with that explanation, but the same color could arise in more than one way. The team called the proposal Underlined: plausible End underlined. and recommended testing it alongside two accounts involving other causes.

As used in the text, what does the word "plausible" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Distinguishing credibility from proof and comparative likelihood | Review lesson 1.5

Correct choice: Reasonably believable

D is correct. "Plausible" means credible or reasonable enough to believe possible. The kiln remains fit the explanation, but the team retains other possible causes and recommends further testing. The stated support establishes credibility without settling which explanation is best.

A: "Conclusive" means decisively establishing a result. The passage expressly allows other causes and withholds a final decision.

B: "Comparatively likely" means more probable relative to other possibilities. The team recommends testing this account alongside others; the text does not rank its probability above theirs.

C: "Generally accepted" means widely endorsed. The passage reports this team's assessment, not agreement across a broader community.

If you missed it: Separate "fits some evidence" from "proved," "best among alternatives," and "widely accepted." These are different claims about an explanation.

Question 17

  • Guide question 1.17
  • Challenge

A report showed that the neighborhood's average household income had risen, although several households earned less than before. In summarizing the report, a columnist wrote that every household's income had increased. By treating an increase in the average as identical to improvement for each member, the columnist ______ aggregate gains with universal individual gains.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Identifying the exact relationship expressed by a verb | Review lesson 1.6

Correct choice: conflates

C is correct. "Conflates" means combines or confuses distinct things as though they were the same. The columnist treats a higher average and improvement for every household as identical, despite the report's explicit counterexamples.

A: "Compares" means examines similarities or differences. The columnist does more than set two ideas alongside each other: the summary wrongly treats them as equivalent.

B: "Correlates" means establishes or identifies an association between variables. The passage describes an invalid substitution of one claim for another, not an analysis of how two measured variables vary together.

D: "Reconciles" means makes apparent conflicts consistent or explains their discrepancy. The columnist ignores the contrary household results rather than resolving the conflict.

If you missed it: Distinguish comparison, association, confusion, and resolution. State the writer's actual move: two different claims are being treated as one.

Question 18

  • Guide question 1.18
  • Challenge

In the experimental garden, cooler flowers attracted fewer pollinating insects, and fewer insect visits led to lower seed production. When researchers supplied equal numbers of insect visits at both temperatures, the seed-production difference disappeared. The researchers concluded that changes in insect activity helped ______ the effect of temperature on seed production.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Interpreting an action within a causal chain | Review lesson 1.6

Correct choice: mediate

B is correct. "Mediate" means serve as an intervening process through which an effect occurs. The passage supplies the chain: temperature changes insect activity, which changes seed production. Equalizing visits removes the observed difference.

A: "Moderate" means reduce or restrain the strength of an effect. Insect activity explains the path through which the temperature-related difference occurs; the text does not describe insect activity reducing an otherwise stronger temperature effect.

C: "Replicate" means repeat or reproduce. Insect activity is an intermediate mechanism, not a separate repetition of the temperature effect.

D: "Quantify" means measure or express in numerical terms. Insect activity is part of the process being explained, not an act of measuring the temperature effect.

If you missed it: Draw the relationship in words: temperature changes visits; visits change seed production. Select the verb describing the middle link's role.

Question 19

  • Guide question 1.19
  • Challenge

The grant fixed the restoration team's total budget and required the footpath to reopen in June. The manager could shift money from surfacing to drainage and change the order of repairs, but adding funds to one task required a cut elsewhere. She welcomed this Underlined: room to maneuver End underlined. when the storm damage proved unevenly distributed.

As used in the text, what does the phrase "room to maneuver" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Preserving the limits of a figurative expression | Review lesson 1.7

Correct choice: Discretion over how to meet the requirements

B is correct. "Room to maneuver" figuratively means flexibility to adjust one's actions within a situation. The manager can change allocations and repair order to respond to uneven damage, while the total budget and reopening date remain fixed.

A: Reconsidering the requirements would permit changes to the mandated budget or reopening date. The grant fixes those terms; the manager has discretion over implementation.

C: Increasing available resources would add money or other resources overall. Shifting money between tasks preserves the fixed total.

D: Protection from the costs of changes would remove their consequences or trade-offs. The text explicitly says that adding money to one task requires a cut elsewhere.

If you missed it: List the decisions the person can and cannot change. A figurative expression of freedom does not necessarily imply unrestricted authority.

Question 20

  • Guide question 1.20
  • Challenge

Lena had an offer to join her aunt's bakery and an invitation to interview for an apprenticeship in the city. She asked her aunt for another week and arranged the interview without promising to accept a place if one was offered. She wanted to keep both futures Underlined: in play End underlined. while she learned more about the city position.

As used in the text, what does the phrase "in play" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Separating open possibilities from settled arrangements | Review lesson 1.7

Correct choice: Possibilities not yet ruled out

D is correct. Here "in play" means remaining active possibilities. Lena delays committing to the bakery and pursues the interview, preserving both paths without deciding. One is an existing offer and the other only a possible offer, but neither has been eliminated from consideration.

A: Equal security would make the opportunities comparably assured. Lena already has the bakery offer but has only an interview for the apprenticeship. Keeping both under consideration does not give them equal certainty.

B: Plans ready to carry out would be sufficiently settled for implementation. Lena has not accepted the bakery offer, and an apprenticeship offer has not yet been made.

C: Renegotiating an arrangement means revising its agreed terms. Lena requests more decision time and seeks information; she is not revising the terms of two established work arrangements.

If you missed it: Separate an option's continued consideration from its certainty or readiness. A possibility can remain open even before an offer or agreement exists.

Question 21

  • Guide question 1.21
  • Challenge

When Tomas arrived with a sprained wrist, his host was quietly ______. She moved a heavy pitcher within reach of his uninjured hand and loosened the lid of the jam before passing it to him. She asked once whether he needed anything else, then let the conversation continue without drawing attention to the injury.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Distinguishing care from deference, appeasement, or flattery | Review lesson 1.8

Correct choice: solicitous

A is correct. "Solicitous" means attentively concerned for someone's comfort or welfare. The host anticipates practical difficulties and discreetly adjusts the setting to meet Tomas's needs.

B: "Deferential" means showing respect by yielding to another's judgment or status. No disagreement, authority, or decision-making hierarchy appears; the actions address a physical need.

C: "Conciliatory" means intended to ease hostility or restore goodwill. The passage supplies no quarrel or resentment that the host is trying to resolve.

D: "Ingratiating" means seeking favor or approval, often through calculated charm. The passage establishes considerate assistance, not a motive to gain Tomas's favor.

If you missed it: Describe the observable action before naming the attitude. Practical care supports concern; it does not automatically establish submission, peacemaking, or a desire for approval.

Question 22

  • Guide question 1.22
  • Transfer

I expected a famous illustrator's studio to overflow with curiosities, but the room was Underlined: austere End underlined.: one plain table, two chairs, and white walls without a single picture. Its owner greeted me warmly and talked with easy enthusiasm. The spareness belonged to the furnishings, not to her manner.

As used in the text, what does the word "austere" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Separating a setting's style from its occupant's attitude | Review lesson 1.8

Correct choice: Plain and unadorned

C is correct. The plain furniture, bare white walls, and explicit description of spareness define an unadorned setting. The passage separately characterizes the illustrator's manner as warm and enthusiastic.

A: An imposing scale would concern large or impressive dimensions. The room's size is not stated; the contrast concerns the absence of decoration.

B: Wear would concern deterioration from use. Plain furniture and bare walls can be new or old; their condition is not described as damaged or aged.

D: An unwelcoming social atmosphere would concern coldness toward visitors. The owner's warm greeting contradicts that interpretation, and the text limits the description to furnishings.

If you missed it: Identify exactly what the adjective modifies. Do not transfer a room's visual simplicity into an unsupported judgment about its owner's personality.

Question 23

  • Guide question 1.23
  • Challenge

The editor's standard did not require a translation to reproduce every original rhyme. It did, however, require the translation to preserve the passage's shifts in rhythm; a version that missed those shifts could not meet the standard, however accurate its individual word choices. Preserving those shifts was thus ______, though doing so alone did not guarantee success.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word?

Why this answer

Distinguishing a requirement from a guarantee | Review lesson 1.9

Correct choice: indispensable

D is correct. "Indispensable" means necessary or impossible to do without. The editor's stated rule makes preserved rhythmic shifts a requirement, and the final clause clarifies that meeting this one requirement is not enough by itself.

A: "Sufficient" means enough to produce the required result. The passage expressly says that preserving the shifts alone does not guarantee success.

B: "Beneficial" means helpful or advantageous. That is weaker than the stated rule: preserved shifts are required, not simply useful. "Indispensable" captures the necessary status more precisely.

C: "Distinctive" means characteristic in a way that sets something apart. The passage defines a requirement but does not claim that this feature distinguishes these translations from all others.

If you missed it: Translate "could not succeed without it" into "necessary." Keep necessity separate from sufficiency and from a merely helpful contribution.

Question 24

  • Guide question 1.24
  • Transfer

At the rehearsal, the conductor had objected to both the balance of the instruments and the pacing of the finale. By the public performance, neither concern remained: the quiet lines were audible, and the closing section moved at the intended tempo. Her approval afterward was Underlined: unqualified End underlined.; she attached no remaining reservations to her judgment.

As used in the text, what does the word "unqualified" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Reading the scope of a qualified-looking word | Review lesson 1.9

Correct choice: Without reservations

B is correct. In the phrase "unqualified approval," the word means approval without limiting conditions or reservations. Both earlier concerns have been resolved, and the final clause explicitly restates the relevant sense.

A: "Unqualified" can describe someone lacking necessary credentials, but the target modifies "approval," not the conductor. Nothing questions her expertise.

C: A judgment without reservations can belong to one person. The passage reports the conductor's response and makes no claim about every listener.

D: Postponement concerns when a judgment is given. The conductor expresses approval after the performance; the marked word describes its unrestricted character, not a further delay.

If you missed it: Track both the prefix and the noun being modified. Here the absent "qualifications" are reservations attached to approval, not credentials required of a person.

Question 25

  • Guide question 1.25
  • Transfer

During the move, a single-page inventory proved Underlined: invaluable End underlined.. It directed the volunteers to the correct boxes whenever a request arrived, preventing repeated searches through the storeroom. Identical copies were easy to print, and the sheet had no sale value; its importance lay in how much time and confusion it saved.

As used in the text, what does the word "invaluable" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Testing a familiar-looking word against explicit context | Review lesson 1.10

Correct choice: Extremely useful

C is correct. "Invaluable" here means extremely useful or valuable in a practical sense. The inventory repeatedly saves time and confusion, while the passage explicitly separates its usefulness from sale value or the difficulty of making a replacement.

A: An irreplaceable object cannot be substituted or reproduced. The passage says identical copies are easy to print, so uniqueness is not the source of value.

B: This would make the inventory minimally helpful. The passage repeatedly shows its practical usefulness; the beginning of "invaluable" does not turn the word into a denial of value.

D: Difficulty assigning a price is not the stated issue. The sheet has no sale value, and the text expressly locates its value in practical assistance.

If you missed it: Do not let a prefix-like beginning override the established word and its contextual explanation. Distinguish practical value, market price, and replaceability.

Question 26

  • Guide question 1.26
  • Challenge

Ordinary cleaning solutions softened the newer finish within minutes, but the older coating was Underlined: refractory End underlined.: repeated applications of those solutions left it unchanged. A carefully controlled heating process eventually loosened the old coating, allowing it to be removed. The conservator therefore used different methods for the two layers.

As used in the text, what does the word "refractory" most nearly mean?

Why this answer

Inferring an unfamiliar word while preserving its scope | Review lesson 1.10

Correct choice: Resistant to standard removal methods

A is correct. "Refractory" here means resistant to treatment or control. The normal cleaning solutions repeatedly fail to alter the coating, although a different treatment eventually works. The scope is resistance to the usual treatments, not immunity to every possible condition.

B: Complete immunity to all removal methods is too broad. Controlled heating eventually loosens the coating, so its resistance is limited rather than universal.

C: Recovery would require a change followed by restoration of the earlier condition. The cleaning solutions leave the coating unchanged; no softening-and-recovery sequence appears.

D: Successive weakening would require the repeated applications to alter the coating progressively. They leave it unchanged; a different method eventually produces the change.

If you missed it: Define the unknown word from the explained behavior, then check any exception. Resistance to one method does not mean invulnerability to all methods.
