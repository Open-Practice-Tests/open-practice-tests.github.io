Words in Context

Does this sense fit the clues, degree, and local construction?

A familiar alternate meaning; a near-synonym that changes the relation or intensity.

Main purpose

Do the action verb and its object describe what the whole text does?

A true topic or detail that misses the writer's central action.

Structure or function

Can I match each claimed move to the actual text, in the correct order?

A reversed sequence, invented rebuttal, or summary presented as a function.

Cross-Text Connections

What in each text supports this exact relationship and response direction?

Wrong speaker, overstated disagreement, or a claim supported by only one text.

Rhetorical verbs carry commitments

Verb

What the text must actually do

Illustrate

Provide an example that makes a general point concrete.

Qualify

Limit or refine a claim, without necessarily rejecting it.

Challenge

Raise a relevant objection to a claim or its support.

Concede

Acknowledge a point, often before limiting its force.

Propose

Put forward an explanation, interpretation, or course of action.

Contrast

Set out a meaningful difference; this alone need not establish a disagreement.

A verb can be plausible while the rest of the answer is wrong. Verify what is being illustrated, qualified, or challenged, who makes the claim, and how far the text goes.

Three retest prompts

• Vocabulary: Which contextual clue or combination of clues rules out the nearest synonym? Write a new sentence in which that wrong word would work.

• Purpose and structure: What does the passage say, and what does saying it accomplish? Name the connection between the marked part and the rest.

• Paired texts: Which proposition do both authors accept? Where exactly do they differ, if they differ at all? Identify the evidence for both sides.

Move from accuracy to speed

Reduce unnecessary rereading after your reasoning is reliable. If you miss the same distinction repeatedly, return to that lesson, explain one worked example without its solution, and then try a fresh question. Keep using official practice to check how the skills transfer to the full test. [1]

Coverage in this guide

Words in Context

Chapter 1: both meaning and completion; logical clues, multiple senses, precision, phrases, connotation, and vocabulary learning.

Text Structure and Purpose

Chapter 2: main purpose, overall organization, local function, argument, attribution, and scientific and literary texts.

Cross-Text Connections

Chapter 3: agreement, disagreement, responses, qualifications, methods, competing explanations, application, and synthesis.

The guide contains 30 worked examples and 114 independent practice items: 18 short drills and 96 multiple-choice questions. Practice comprises a 12-question diagnostic, 26 items in each of three chapters, and a 24-question cumulative review. Every multiple-choice example and practice item explains the correct answer and all three alternatives. The separate vocabulary reference contains 60 entries.

Craft and Structure is one domain within Reading and Writing. Information and Ideas, Expression of Ideas, and Standard English Conventions require separate preparation.
