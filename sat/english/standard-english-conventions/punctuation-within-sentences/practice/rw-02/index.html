Question 1

  • Guide question RW-02.01

The long footbridge—a route used by walkers throughout the ______ the two neighborhoods.

Which completion preserves the simple insertion and main clause?

Why this answer

A: The comma does not close the single dash-marked insertion.

B — correct: The dash closes the supplement, leaving The long footbridge connects the two neighborhoods.

C: The extra comma separates the subject from connects after the supplement closes.

D: A semicolon cannot close this insertion or follow the incomplete subject phrase.

Question 2

  • Guide question RW-02.02

The ______ into three areas—printing, binding, and packing—provides paid training.

Which completion preserves the underlying main clause?

Why this answer

A: Is divided and provides would be unconnected finite predicates.

B: Has been divided creates the same unconnected-predicate problem.

C: Is makes the description finite; the comma after is also splits the verb phrase.

D — correct: Read as The workshop, divided into three areas—printing, binding, and packing—provides paid training. Divided introduces the outer supplement; the dash introduces its inner list. The core is The workshop provides paid training.

Question 3

  • Guide question RW-02.03

The program, designed by two volunteers (a nurse and an engineer), ______ residents find available appointments.

Which completion supplies only the main predicate this sentence needs?

Why this answer

A — correct: The singular subject program takes helps. Designed is a participial supplement, and the parenthetical identifies the volunteers.

B: Is and helps cannot form one verb phrase; there is no connector between them.

C: Helping alone is nonfinite and leaves the main clause without a finite verb.

D: Has helped and assists are two unconnected finite predicates.

Review lesson: Nested punctuation and the main clause

Question 4

  • Guide question RW-02.04

The schedule includes a morning session, which is held outdoors; an afternoon session, which uses the studio; and an evening session, which is recorded. ______

Which analysis correctly explains the punctuation?

Why this answer

A: The first semicolon is followed by an afternoon session, a noun phrase rather than an independent clause.

B: Each which-clause modifies its own session; there is no single spanning supplement.

C — correct: The semicolons mark the three large list items; the commas introduce smaller relative-clause supplements within them.

D: Each finite verb in a which-clause belongs to that subordinate clause and is licensed. The main verb is includes.
