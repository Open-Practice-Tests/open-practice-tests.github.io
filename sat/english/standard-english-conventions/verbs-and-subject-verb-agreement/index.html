3.1 Find the finite verb phrase

A long sentence can contain several verb-shaped words but still lack the verb its main clause needs. First find the subject and ask what the sentence actually says that subject does or is. A finite verb phrase can serve as the predicate of a complete declarative clause: the artist sketches, the artist is sketching, the artist has sketched, or the artist may sketch. The whole phrase counts, including its auxiliaries.

An infinitive, such as to sketch, or an unaided participle, such as sketching, cannot ordinarily do that job alone. In The artist sketching the bridge, the phrase sketching the bridge describes the artist; the main clause still needs a finite verb. But The artist is sketching the bridge is complete. Never eliminate every answer containing an -ing word. An -ing form can also head a subject, traditionally called a gerund use: in Sketching bridges requires patience, Sketching bridges acts as the subject, while requires supplies the finite main verb.

Check which clause owns each finite verb. In The artist who sketches bridges lives nearby, sketches belongs to the relative clause beginning with who; lives is the main verb. A verb buried inside a description cannot also finish the surrounding clause. Conversely, when a finite main verb is already present, a descriptive participle may be exactly what a blank needs.

W3.1: A relative clause does not finish the main clause

The courtyard is designed to be explored without a map. Its carefully arranged paving stones, which form a spiral around the central fountain, ______ visitors toward a narrow passage leading to the sculpture garden.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. guiding

B. to guide

C. guide

D. having guided

Practice this lesson: 3.05, 3.06.

Show worked solutionHide worked solution for W3.1

Answer: C

Completed text: The courtyard is designed to be explored without a map. Its carefully arranged paving stones, which form a spiral around the central fountain, guide visitors toward a narrow passage leading to the sculpture garden.

  1. Locate the main subject: paving stones. The sentence must say what those stones do.
  2. Assign the existing verb form to its own clause: which form a spiral around the central fountain. That description does not supply the main clause with a verb.
  3. Restore the core: Its paving stones guide visitors. The finite present-tense verb guide completes that clause and agrees with plural stones.

Why A fails: Guiding is a participle without a finite auxiliary here. It adds another description but leaves the main clause unfinished.

Why B fails: To guide is an infinitive. It can express a purpose, but it cannot serve as this clause's finite predicate.

Why D fails: Having guided is a nonfinite participial phrase. The word having does not make the main clause complete.

Key takeaway: When several verb forms compete, label the clauses before judging endings. A finite verb in a relative clause cannot replace the main verb.

3.2 Make the verb agree with the subject head

The subject is often an entire noun phrase, but one noun or pronoun controls its number. In The arrangement of the panels allows more light inside, the subject head is singular arrangement. The nearby plural noun panels belongs to an of phrase. Temporarily bracket intervening descriptions, then read the subject head with its verb: arrangement allows.

Do not mechanically choose the nearest noun. A subject can be separated from its verb by prepositional phrases, appositives, participial phrases, and relative clauses. Each embedded clause has its own agreement: The arrangement of panels that reflect light allows.... Plural panels controls reflect; singular arrangement controls allows. Removing the description for analysis does not mean it is unimportant to the meaning.

In the simple present, most third-person singular subjects take the verb with -s: a design works; plural subjects take the base form: designs work. With auxiliaries, check the first finite element: the design has worked, the designs have worked. Most simple-past verbs do not change for number, though was/were does. Therefore, diagnose agreement from the actual choices instead of treating every verb question as an agreement question.

W3.2: The closest noun is a decoy

The theater uses movable panels to change the size of its rehearsal rooms. The arrangement of these panels, each of which rests on a separate track, ______ staff members to create two smaller rooms within minutes.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. allow

B. allows

C. are allowing

D. have allowed

Practice this lesson: 3.07, 3.08.

Show worked solutionHide worked solution for W3.2

Answer: B

Completed text: The theater uses movable panels to change the size of its rehearsal rooms. The arrangement of these panels, each of which rests on a separate track, allows staff members to create two smaller rooms within minutes.

  1. Find the noun that names what enables the change: singular arrangement. The phrase of these panels identifies the arrangement.
  2. Bracket each of which rests on a separate track. Its verb rests belongs to the inserted relative construction, not to the main clause.
  3. Test the core The arrangement allows staff members.... Choice B supplies the singular present-tense verb required by the subject.

Why A fails: Allow is the plural present-tense form here. The main subject is arrangement, not panels or staff members.

Why C fails: The finite auxiliary are requires a plural subject in this construction. Singular arrangement would require is.

Why D fails: The finite auxiliary have does not agree with third-person singular arrangement. A singular perfect phrase would begin with has.

Key takeaway: Bracket the interruption, name the subject head, and check the first finite word in the answer. Agreement follows grammar, not proximity.

3.3 Find subjects that come after the verb

English often puts the subject before the verb, but descriptive openings can reverse that order: Beyond the gate stand two statues. The subject is two statues, even though it follows stand. Mentally restore ordinary order: Two statues stand beyond the gate. Choose agreement only after this rearrangement.

Existential there introduces something's presence: There are two entrances; There is one entrance. In these formal written constructions, use the number of the noun phrase after the verb rather than treating there as a singular noun. Here can also introduce inverted order: Here are the revised plans. A location phrase before the verb is not automatically the subject.

Read the entire noun phrase after the blank. Near the door sits a box of samples has singular box; Near the door sit samples from a box has plural samples. A plural noun inside a location phrase can be another distraction. This procedure is an analysis tool, not an instruction to rewrite the passage on the test. The original inverted sentence can be entirely grammatical.

Core order for checking

Under the prints stands a cabinet.

W3.3: The subject is still ahead

The exhibition places large works outdoors so visitors can walk around them. Along a path bordered by low hedges ______ three metal sculptures whose curved surfaces reflect the changing colors of the surrounding garden.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. stands

B. has stood

C. is standing

D. stand

Practice this lesson: 3.09, 3.10.

Show worked solutionHide worked solution for W3.3

Answer: D

Completed text: The exhibition places large works outdoors so visitors can walk around them. Along a path bordered by low hedges stand three metal sculptures whose curved surfaces reflect the changing colors of the surrounding garden.

  1. Recognize Along a path bordered by low hedges as a location phrase. Neither path nor hedges is the subject of the blank.
  2. Continue past the blank to three metal sculptures. The subject head sculptures is plural.
  3. Rearrange mentally: Three metal sculptures stand along a path. Choice D gives the corresponding plural verb without changing the original order.

Why A fails: Stands is singular. The singular noun path belongs to the opening location phrase and does not control this verb.

Why B fails: Has is singular. The plural subject sculptures would require have in a present-perfect phrase.

Why C fails: Is is singular. A progressive phrase with plural sculptures would begin with are.

Key takeaway: If the opening words name a location, check whether the true subject appears after the blank. Then restore subject-verb order to verify agreement.

3.4 Separate joined subjects from added descriptions

Two distinct subjects joined by and normally take a plural verb: A map and a compass are provided. Count the subjects before checking the verb. Do not let a singular noun inside a later description hide the compound subject. There are special constructions that name one person or one unified item, so understand what the phrase refers to rather than applying a word-count rule blindly.

Phrases introduced by along with, together with, as well as, and in addition to do not ordinarily create a compound subject. Compare The curator and the assistants are attending with The curator, together with the assistants, is attending. In the second sentence, the subject head remains singular curator. The commas mark the addition; the phrase's grammatical role determines the agreement.

With two singular alternatives joined by or or nor, use a singular verb: Neither the map nor the diagram shows the entrance. With two plural alternatives, use a plural verb. In standard edited constructions with mixed numbers, the nearer alternative generally governs agreement. Such sentences can sound strained; when freely editing, recasting them often improves clarity. On the SAT, examine the exact construction and avoid mistaking an additive phrase for an and subject.

W3.4: Two subjects share a predicate

The restoration team has identified two separate features that need attention. A crack above the doorway and a loose section of plaster near the windows ______ immediate repairs before the room can reopen to visitors.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. require

B. requires

C. has required

D. is requiring

Practice this lesson: 3.11, 3.12.

Show worked solutionHide worked solution for W3.4

Answer: A

Completed text: The restoration team has identified two separate features that need attention. A crack above the doorway and a loose section of plaster near the windows require immediate repairs before the room can reopen to visitors.

  1. Identify both subject heads: crack and section. They name two distinct features and are joined by and.
  2. Set aside the location phrases above the doorway and near the windows, along with of plaster. They do not remove either subject.
  3. Read the compound core: A crack and a section require repairs. The two subjects together require plural agreement.

Why B fails: Requires would agree with one singular subject. Here crack and section form a plural compound subject.

Why C fails: The singular auxiliary has cannot agree with these two distinct subjects joined by and.

Why D fails: The singular auxiliary is has the same agreement problem. Changing the aspect does not make the compound subject singular.

Key takeaway: An and connection between distinct subjects changes their combined number; an additive description such as along with usually does not.

3.5 Handle indefinite and quantity subjects

Some subjects name individuals indirectly. Each, either, neither, everyone, everybody, and everything take singular agreement in their ordinary uses as subjects: Each of the panels is numbered. The plural noun after of does not change each. Likewise, every panel is numbered. But in The panels each have a number, the subject is plural panels; the later word each does not make it singular.

Pay attention to the difference between a number of and the number of. A number of visitors have arrived means several visitors; plural agreement follows. The number of visitors has increased refers to one count; singular agreement follows. Replacing the article changes the grammatical structure and meaning.

With fractions, percentages, and words such as some, the noun being quantified matters: Half of the pages are missing, but half of the paper is missing. Do not apply the ordinary head-noun shortcut without checking this quantity construction. A measured amount treated as one total can also be singular: Ten minutes is enough. Avoid guessing from the visible numeral alone; determine whether the sentence refers to plural items or one amount.

The designers have prepared several alternative covers for the exhibition catalog. Each of the proposals, including the two that use photographs rather than drawings, ______ displayed beside a sample of the paper on which it would be printed.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. are

B. have been

C. were

D. is

Practice this lesson: 3.13, 3.14.

Show worked solutionHide worked solution for W3.5

Answer: D

Completed text: The designers have prepared several alternative covers for the exhibition catalog. Each of the proposals, including the two that use photographs rather than drawings, is displayed beside a sample of the paper on which it would be printed.

  1. Locate the main subject Each of the proposals. The word each considers the proposals individually and takes singular agreement.
  2. Bracket the addition beginning with including. The plural words two, photographs, and drawings do not change the main subject.
  3. The completed passive phrase is Each ... is displayed. Choice D supplies a singular finite auxiliary; the existing participle displayed completes the passive phrase.

Why A fails: Are is plural, but each requires singular agreement here.

Why B fails: The finite auxiliary have is plural in this construction. A singular present-perfect passive phrase would begin has been.

Why C fails: Were is plural in this ordinary statement. The main subject is singular each, not plural proposals.

Key takeaway: Quantity expressions require a meaning check. Each of stays singular, while a fraction of plural items generally takes plural agreement.

3.6 Choose tense from the actual timeline

Verb tense locates an event relative to a time frame. Look for explicit anchors such as in 1998, today, next year, and since 2017, then ask which event each anchor modifies. A passage can move from a finished historical event to a present condition: The museum opened in 1982 and now houses a photography collection. That shift is necessary, not an error.

For a finished event located at a specific past time, the simple past is often appropriate: In 1982, the museum opened. The present perfect normally does not combine with a time expression locating that event wholly within a finished past period: has opened in 1982 does not work in that sentence. The present perfect instead connects a past situation to the present, as in The museum has expanded since 1982.

Do not force every verb into the tense of its nearest neighbor. Identify the time of the blank's clause. Also distinguish tense from agreement and completeness: the museum have opened fails agreement, and the museum opening lacks a finite predicate. Eliminate those failures first when useful, but explain the decisive rule accurately. Present-tense narration can be legitimate; a test item must provide enough context to establish the intended time frame.

Time relationship

Example

Finished event at a stated past time

The archive moved in 2006.

Present condition after a past event

It moved in 2006 and now occupies two floors.

Past-to-present span

It has occupied this building since 2006.

W3.6: A finished date rules out the present perfect

The town hall originally relied on oil lamps. During a renovation completed in 1934, workers ______ electric fixtures in the meeting room and replaced the damaged floorboards. The building reopened the following spring.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. have installed

B. installed

C. will install

D. installing

Practice this lesson: 3.15, 3.16.

Show worked solutionHide worked solution for W3.6

Answer: B

Completed text: The town hall originally relied on oil lamps. During a renovation completed in 1934, workers installed electric fixtures in the meeting room and replaced the damaged floorboards. The building reopened the following spring.

  1. Build the timeline: the renovation was completed in 1934, and the building reopened the following spring. Both are finished historical events.
  2. The workers' installation belongs to that renovation. Simple past installed places it in the stated past period and coordinates naturally with replaced.
  3. Check the full clause: workers installed electric fixtures ... and replaced the damaged floorboards. One subject controls two complete past-tense predicates.

Why A fails: Have installed is present perfect. The text locates this installation within a specific renovation completed in 1934, not within an unfinished period extending to the present.

Why C fails: Will install locates the installation in the future relative to the present. That conflicts with its placement in the completed 1934 renovation.

Why D fails: Installing supplies no finite verb for the first predicate. It also cannot coordinate as a matching predicate with the finite verb replaced in this construction.

Key takeaway: Use the time of the particular event, supported by its clause and surrounding sentences. Tense consistency means a coherent timeline, not identical verb forms.

3.7 Read perfect and progressive verb phrases

Aspect shows how an event relates to a reference time or whether it is viewed as ongoing. The perfect uses a form of have plus a past participle: has written, had written, will have written. Present perfect connects a past event or interval to the present. Past perfect places an event before a past reference point. Future perfect places completion before a future reference point.

The progressive uses a form of be plus an -ing participle: is writing, was writing. The two aspects can combine: has been writing. Read the entire chain; been follows have, and the progressive main verb takes -ing. An auxiliary is not optional merely because the sentence already contains a different verb elsewhere.

Use these meanings without inventing rigid rules. After the courier arrived, the curator opened the crate and After the courier had arrived, the curator opened the crate can both be grammatical. The word after already supplies the order; the past perfect emphasizes the earlier event. Likewise, simple and progressive forms can sometimes describe the same situation from different viewpoints. On a multiple-choice question, inspect the actual alternatives, their formation, and the explicit reference times; do not eliminate a valid form solely because another sounds more precise.

Pattern

Reference relationship

has/have + past participle

past event or interval connected to now

had + past participle

event before a past reference point

will have + past participle

completion before a future reference point

is/was + -ing participle

action viewed as ongoing at its reference time

has/had been + -ing participle

W3.7: Keep the entire auxiliary chain

Mira began keeping detailed records of rainfall at the field station in 2021. Since then, she ______ rainfall each morning, and she continues to add the measurements to a notebook kept beside the station's rain gauge.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. has recording

B. having been recording

C. has been recording

D. is been recording

Practice this lesson: 3.17, 3.18.

Show worked solutionHide worked solution for W3.7

Answer: C

Completed text: Mira began keeping detailed records of rainfall at the field station in 2021. Since then, she has been recording rainfall each morning, and she continues to add the measurements to a notebook kept beside the station's rain gauge.

  1. Locate the interval: since then begins in 2021, and continues connects the activity to the present.
  2. Identify the complete present-perfect progressive chain: has + been + recording. Singular she agrees with has.
  3. Insert the entire phrase: she has been recording rainfall each morning. It is finite, correctly formed, and consistent with the ongoing past-to-present activity.

Why A fails: After perfect has, this progressive construction needs been before recording. The chain has recording is not grammatical.

Why B fails: Having been recording is nonfinite. It leaves the clause beginning she without a finite predicate before the comma and coordinating conjunction.

Why D fails: Been cannot follow is in this chain. The perfect construction requires has been, while a present progressive would use is recording.

Key takeaway: Check the first finite auxiliary, each following form, and the reference time. A long verb phrase is one grammatical unit, not a collection of interchangeable words.

3.8 Check voice, auxiliaries, and irregular participles

In the active version of an action, the subject is the actor: The illustrator drew the map. In a passive clause, the subject receives the action: The map was drawn by the illustrator. Passive voice is fully grammatical and often useful when the recipient matters most. The ordinary passive pattern uses a form of be plus a past participle. A by phrase may name the actor, but it is not required.

Do not confuse the simple past with the past participle. Regular verbs often use the same form for both, but irregular verbs can differ: wrote/written, drew/drawn, chose/chosen, and took/taken. Perfect have and passive be select participles: has written, was written. Neither has wrote nor was wrote works.

On a difficult item, check several constraints in order: Does the clause have a finite verb? Does its first auxiliary agree with the subject? Does the chain contain the forms its auxiliaries require? Does the subject perform or receive the stated action? Finally, verify the timeline. A passive perfect phrase such as has been written can satisfy all five checks. Never remove an auxiliary or reject passive voice merely to make an answer shorter.

W3.8: The passive needs a past participle

The exhibition includes the planning materials for an unbuilt concert hall. The original blueprints were ______ by the architect in 1962, but the scale model beside them was constructed much later by students studying the proposed design.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A. drawn

B. drew

C. drawing

D. draw

Practice this lesson: 3.19, 3.20.

Show worked solutionHide worked solution for W3.8

Answer: A

Completed text: The exhibition includes the planning materials for an unbuilt concert hall. The original blueprints were drawn by the architect in 1962, but the scale model beside them was constructed much later by students studying the proposed design.

  1. Identify the passive structure: the blueprints received the action, and by the architect names the person who performed it.
  2. The existing finite auxiliary were agrees with plural blueprints. To complete this passive phrase, supply the past participle of draw.
  3. Distinguish the irregular forms: drew is simple past; drawn is the participle. The completed phrase is were drawn.

Why B fails: Drew is the simple-past form. Passive were requires the participle drawn, not drew.

Why C fails: Were drawing would be an active progressive phrase, making the blueprints perform the drawing. It does not form the passive action attributed to the architect by the by phrase.

Why D fails: The base form draw cannot follow were to form this passive predicate. The needed form is drawn.

Key takeaway: When an auxiliary is already printed before the blank, let it constrain the next form. Be plus a past participle can form a passive phrase.

Independent practice

Keep these checks in mind

• Assign every finite verb to its own clause. A relative-clause verb cannot finish the surrounding main clause.

• Find the subject head before choosing agreement; if the subject follows the verb, mentally restore ordinary order.

• Check the exact construction: distinct subjects joined by and, an additive phrase, alternatives with or/nor, or a quantity expression.

• Use the blank's time frame. Past and present can coexist when the passage changes time, and an earlier past event does not automatically require past perfect.

• Read the full auxiliary chain. Perfect have selects a past participle; progressive be selects an -ing participle; passive be selects a past participle.

Answer: Both versions are grammatical. The delivery happened first, and the opening happened afterward. Had delivered explicitly presents the delivery as earlier than the past opening, but after already makes that order clear.
