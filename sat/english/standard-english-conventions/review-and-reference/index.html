Clause and punctuation checks

IC means independent clause; DC means dependent clause. Identify each clause's subject and finite predicate. A verb inside a relative clause may not complete the main sentence. Check which grammatical units the punctuation actually separates.

Structure

A valid pattern

Check before choosing

Two complete statements

IC. IC. / IC; IC.

Both sides must stand independently. Capitalize the next sentence after a period. A semicolon is not the only possible valid separator.

Coordinated clauses

IC, but IC. / IC, and IC.

The conjunction joins two independent clauses. A shared subject with two predicates is a different structure.

Subordination

DC, IC. / Although the test ended, analysis continued.

An introductory adverbial dependent clause takes a comma before the main clause. A dependent clause cannot stand as a complete sentence by itself.

Conjunctive adverb

IC; however, IC. / IC. However, IC.

However and therefore do not do the same grammatical job as and or but. Their position and use matter.

Colon introduction

Complete introduction: explanation.

The material before the colon must stand as a complete clause. The explanation may be a phrase, list, or clause.

Single explanatory dash

Complete statement—explanation.

A dash can introduce an emphatic expansion. Do not assume that a colon is always the uniquely valid alternative.

Inserted supplement

Core, supplement, core.

A medial supplement needs a complete punctuation pair. Paired dashes or parentheses can also enclose supplementary material.

Essential material

Core with identifying material.

Do not set off information that restricts the intended referent. Identification depends on context.

Uninterrupted core

Subject + verb + complement.

No separator belongs between a subject and its verb, an auxiliary and main verb, a verb and its object, or a preposition and its object merely because the unit is long.

Agreement and form checks

Decision

Reliable check

Model

Subject-verb agreement

Locate the subject head; ignore the number of nouns inside its modifiers.

The box of samples is sealed. The samples in the box are sealed.

Inversion

Find the subject even when it follows the verb.

Behind the curtain stand two statues. There is one entrance.

Additive phrases

Together with and as well as do not create an and-compound subject.

The curator, together with two assistants, is preparing the exhibit.

Pronoun agreement

Identify the antecedent and its intended number. Do not impose gender absent from the text.

The notebooks retained their labels. A participant may revise their response. Singular they is standard usage.

Verb finiteness

Determine whether the clause has a complete finite predicate.

Walking is nonfinite by itself; is walking is a finite verb phrase. To walk cannot replace walks as the main predicate.

Time and aspect

Anchor each action to the stated reference time. Use context, not a rule that all tenses must match.

The team measured the sample yesterday and is analyzing it now.

Perfect / progressive / passive

Check the auxiliary and the required next form.

has written; is writing; was written. The auxiliaries establish different relationships.

Ownership

Determine how many owners there are and whether ownership is shared. Then apply the possessive form.

one artist: the artist's studio; several artists: the artists' studios; irregular plural: the children's books.

Modifier attachment

Ask who or what actually performs the opening action or has the stated property.

After comparing the drafts, the editor chose the shorter version. The editor performed the comparison.

Common forms and close calls

Form

Use

Expansion test

its / it's

The instrument retained its label. It's fragile.

it's = it is or it has; possessive its has no apostrophe.

their / they're / there

Their notes are clear. They're ready. Put it there.

they're = they are; their shows possession; there indicates a location or introduces a construction such as there is.

your / you're

Your notes are ready. You're prepared.

you're = you are; your shows possession.

whose / who's

Whose notes are these? Who's arrived?

who's = who is or who has; whose shows possession.

Do not infer an error from passive voice, a justified change of tense, a long subject, or the use of singular they. Identify the actual structural or meaning problem.

Punctuation pair check

The maps—some of which were incomplete—were scanned. Removing the supplement leaves The maps were scanned. The second dash closes the interruption; it does not separate two independent clauses. In The maps were scanned—a slow process, the single dash introduces an explanation at the end. Count the marks and identify their jobs before applying a rule.

A valid repair is not always unique

An editing drill can admit several correct revisions. A carefully constructed multiple-choice item has only one choice that works with the exact surrounding text. Never reject a grammatically valid sentence merely because you would have written it differently. When two choices appear valid, check capitalization, conjunctions, paired marks, sentence completeness, and meaning again.

Retest by reconstructing, not remembering

  • For a boundary item, underline each subject and finite verb, then label the units on both sides of the blank. Explain why the closest wrong punctuation fails.
  • For an agreement or tense item, name the controlling subject or reference time. Change that feature and explain how the answer would change.
  • For a possessive or modifier item, restate the intended ownership or attachment in plain language before choosing a form.

Coverage in this guide

Boundaries

Chapter 1: sentence completeness, coordination, subordination, clause separators, conjunctive adverbs, colons, and explanatory dashes. Chapter 2: supplementary material, punctuation pairs, introductory elements, series, and punctuation that must be omitted.

Chapter 3: subject-verb agreement, verb finiteness, tense, and aspect. Chapter 4: pronoun-antecedent agreement, genitives and plurals, possessives and contractions, and subject-modifier placement.

Every multiple-choice worked example and practice item explains the key and all three alternatives.
