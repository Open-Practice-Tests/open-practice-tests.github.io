Question 1

  • Guide question C01
  • Transfer

Compact notation can represent a mathematical instruction that would require many words to express in ordinary language. ______, a symbol defined to mean "add every number in the indicated list" can replace that entire verbal command wherever the instruction recurs. Readers who know the definition can then recognize the instruction immediately.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Identify a general claim and the specific proposition that develops it | Review lesson 1.1

Correct choice: For example

The first sentence states a general benefit of compact notation. The defined symbol is one instance of notation replacing a lengthy instruction, so "For example" links the propositions correctly. No earlier symbol or historical stage is described.

A: The symbol implements the benefit described in the first sentence; it does not replace or correct that claim.

C: The specific symbol supports the claim that notation can save words rather than opposing it.

D: The passage supplies an illustrative case, not a chronology in which this symbol came before another practice.

If you missed it: Paraphrase the general statement and ask whether the following case belongs within it.

Review lesson: 1.1 Find the claims before naming the connection

Correct choice: Merrow's mapping project aimed to identify routes to the public library that wheelchair users could travel without encountering steps along the way.

Note 2 explicitly names the practical aim, and note 1 supplies the routes' destination. D states that aim directly. The other choices report accurate survey activities, preparation, or geographic scope without stating the accessibility objective.

A: This accurately combines notes 3 and 4, but it describes what volunteers did without stating why step-free routes were being identified.

B: This accurately reports preparation from note 5, but distributing materials is not the project's practical aim.

C: This accurately describes the area and landmarks, but it omits the goal of identifying usable routes for wheelchair users.

Question 6

  • Guide question C06
  • Challenge

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. A small experimental greenhouse uses a controller to operate a roof vent in response to air temperature.

2. When the temperature reaches 28 degrees Celsius, the controller opens the vent, allowing warm air to escape.

3. After the vent opens, the controller leaves it open until the temperature falls to 25 degrees Celsius.

4. The vent remains closed between 25 and 28 degrees if the most recent triggering event was its closure at 25 degrees.

5. Using different opening and closing thresholds prevents the vent from repeatedly switching states when the temperature fluctuates slightly near a single threshold.

The student wants to explain how the controller's two temperature thresholds keep the vent from repeatedly opening and closing near a single threshold. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Explain a conditional feedback process with accurate trigger and stopping conditions | Review lesson 2.3

Correct choice: The controller opens the vent at 28 degrees Celsius but closes it only when the temperature falls to 25 degrees, so small fluctuations around one threshold do not repeatedly reverse its state.

Notes 2 and 3 give the distinct triggers; note 5 explains their purpose. A connects both thresholds to the prevention of repeated reversals from small fluctuations. The explanation preserves the controller's dependence on which threshold has actually been reached.

B: This accurately describes opening and heat release in note 2, but it omits the closing threshold and cannot explain the two-threshold mechanism.

C: This accurately states the conditional behavior in note 4, but it neither gives the opening trigger explicitly nor explains why two thresholds reduce switching.

D: This reverses the triggers: the notes specify opening at 28 degrees and closing at 25 degrees, not opening above 25 and closing below 28.

If you missed it: Trace one full cycle: closed, opening trigger, open, closing trigger. A process explanation must preserve both the order and the conditions.

Review lesson: 2.3 Explain a concept or process

Question 11

On mornings when a transit app sent more delay alerts, its users also opened the app more often. The data did not show whether the alerts prompted those extra visits or whether a third factor increased both alerts and visits. ______, the analysts withheld a causal conclusion and designed a separate test in which alert frequency could be varied independently.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Connect an evidentiary limitation to a warranted research decision | Review lesson 1.6

Correct choice: For that reason

The limitation is uncertainty about whether alerts caused extra visits or a third factor increased both. Withholding a causal claim and designing an independent test responds to that limitation, so "For that reason" fits. The transition links uncertainty to the analysts' decision; it does not claim that alerts caused visits.

A: The analysts' caution follows from the uncertainty rather than defying it, so a concession would misstate the decision's relation to the evidence.

C: The decision to test independently is a response to the uncertainty, not an example of alerts and visits increasing together or a third factor doing so.

D: No earlier comparable research decision is described; the preceding sentence identifies a limitation.

If you missed it: Locate the actual causal link being asserted. A limitation can cause a methodological decision even when the underlying observed correlation does not establish causation.

3. Pump S moved 1,200 liters using 0.48 kilowatt-hours, an electricity intensity of 0.400 kilowatt-hours per 1,000 liters.

4. Electricity intensity means electricity consumed per 1,000 liters moved; a lower value indicates less electricity used for that volume.

5. The notes make no comparison of the pumps' purchase prices or expected operating lifetimes.

The student wants to compare the pumps' electricity intensities and state the approximate percentage by which Pump R's intensity was lower than Pump S's. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Normalize quantities and compare a relative percentage using the correct baseline | Review lesson 2.11

Correct choice: Pump R used about 0.333 kilowatt-hours per 1,000 liters versus S's 0.400, making R's electricity intensity approximately 16.7 percent lower than S's.

Using notes 2 through 4, R's intensity is 0.30/900 x 1,000 = 0.333...; S's is 0.48/1,200 x 1,000 = 0.400. The relative reduction is (0.400 - 0.333...)/0.400 x 100, approximately 16.7 percent. B uses the correct denominator and units.

A: The raw electricity totals are correct, but the 37.5 percent reduction compares unequal water volumes and therefore is not the reduction per 1,000 liters.

C: These raw test figures and shared condition are accurate, but the sentence neither normalizes by volume nor supplies the requested percentage comparison.

D: The volume difference and missing cost information are accurate, but they do not compare electricity intensity or its relative reduction.

If you missed it: Normalize both measurements to the same quantity first. Then divide the difference by the stated comparison baseline, here Pump S's intensity.

Correct choice: Nevertheless

The earlier sentences build a favorable assessment of execution. The final sentence reports an unfavorable funding decision based on a separate eligibility issue. While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. The intended audience is unfamiliar with explanations for pale bands on ceramic tiles from an invented excavation.

2. Researcher Mira Del proposed that the bands formed when the tiles cooled unevenly after firing, producing differences in their surface appearance.

3. Del fired replicas from the same clay mixture and varied how quickly different sections cooled.

4. Some replicas developed pale bands resembling those on the excavated tiles, but the experiment did not establish how the excavated tiles were cooled.

5. Del described the results as consistent with the uneven-cooling explanation and noted that other processes could also produce similar bands.

The student wants to introduce Del's explanation to the unfamiliar audience, connect it to the replica evidence, and preserve the researcher's uncertainty about the excavated tiles. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Meet audience, attribution, uncertainty, and explanatory constraints together | Review lesson 2.12

Correct choice: Mira Del proposes that uneven cooling produced the excavated tiles' pale bands; similar bands on some replicas support this possibility, although they do not establish how the excavated tiles cooled.

A identifies Del and explains the proposed mechanism from note 2, connects it to the resemblance observed in note 4, and retains the limit in notes 4 and 5. An unfamiliar audience receives the actual explanation rather than an unexplained reference to "a proposed explanation."

B: This accurately describes notes 3 and 4, but it does not explicitly present the explanation for the excavated tiles or preserve the uncertainty about their actual cooling history.

C: The experiment reproduced a possible effect; notes 4 and 5 explicitly deny that it established the excavated tiles' cooling history or uniquely identified the cause.

D: This accurately reports Del's cautious interpretation, but it does not tell the unfamiliar audience what the explanation is or specify the relevant replica evidence.

If you missed it: Use a three-part check: name and explain the proposal, identify its evidence, and state what that evidence still cannot establish.
