Expression of Ideas: diagnostic

12 questions in random order. There is no built-in timer or score. Choose or enter an answer, then press Show solution to see how you did and why. Difficulty stays hidden until you open the solution. Reloading starts a fresh, shuffled session.

Work through the set before checking solutions. Note the evidence for each answer and why the nearest alternative fails. Question numbers follow the shuffled order; Guide question labels match the guide references.

Choose one answer per item. Work untimed, record confidence, and give one short reason. For a transition, identify the relationship; for synthesis, state the goal requirement. Check the explanations after completing the set.

Use results as a starting signal

Six questions per skill cannot establish mastery. Revisit a lesson when an answer is wrong, uncertain, or correct for an unsupported reason. Distinguish a misread relationship from a missed goal requirement; the repairs differ.

Check your reasoning

Check why the answer works. A correct guess or an unsupported explanation calls for more practice. For transitions, reread the complete text with the chosen connector. For synthesis, verify the relevant notes and every part of the stated goal.

Question

  • Guide question D01
  • Foundation

A survey team needed to estimate the width of a marsh without stepping onto its fragile vegetation. Direct measurement on foot would have damaged the very habitat the team was studying. ______, the team chose a method that calculated the width from photographs taken at the marsh's edge.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Identify the direction of a connection | Review lesson 1.1

Correct choice: Therefore

The need to avoid damage explains the decision to measure from photographs. The movement is from a practical constraint to a decision made because of that constraint, so "Therefore" fits.

A: The decision is a response to the constraint, not a second comparable instance of a previously described decision.

B: Photographic measurement meets the stated need; it does not proceed despite an obstacle that would make that decision unexpected.

C: The final sentence gives the resulting choice, not an example of the damage described in the preceding sentence.

If you missed it: Write a short cause-and-response pair before considering the choices: walking would damage habitat; the team selected remote measurement.

Question

  • Guide question D02
  • Foundation

In a trial of two packaging materials, the paper sleeve absorbed moisture and lost its shape after ten minutes in a humid chamber. ______, the fiber-coated sleeve retained its original shape throughout the entire hour-long test. The trial therefore revealed a substantial difference in the materials' resistance to humidity.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Contrast on a shared dimension | Review lesson 1.4

Correct choice: By contrast

Both sleeves undergo a humidity test, but one loses its shape while the other retains it. "By contrast" marks opposite outcomes on that shared dimension. The first sleeve's failure does not cause the second sleeve's success.

B: The second sleeve's performance is a different finding, not a restatement of the first sleeve's performance.

C: Nothing says the failure of one sleeve caused the other sleeve to retain its shape.

D: Retaining shape cannot exemplify the preceding claim that the paper sleeve lost its shape.

If you missed it: Name the comparison dimension: shape retention under humidity. Then record the two opposing results.

Question

  • Guide question D03
  • Transfer

Most of the seedlings in a greenhouse had stopped growing after an unusually cool week, and the gardeners expected the smallest seedlings to fare worst. ______, the smallest seedlings continued to produce new leaves while their larger neighbors remained unchanged. The gardeners began checking whether conditions differed between the two groups' benches.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Overturn an explicitly established expectation | Review lesson 1.5

Correct choice: Nevertheless

The passage predicts that the smallest seedlings will do poorly, then reports that they continue growing. "Nevertheless" signals the failure of the stated expectation. The final investigation does not establish an explanation for that surprising outcome.

A: The unexpected growth contradicts the prediction; the prediction itself did not cause growth.

B: Continuing to grow does not exemplify stopping growth or faring worst.

D: The new observation reverses the earlier expectation instead of expressing the same idea again.

If you missed it: Underline the expectation, then compare it with the observed result. Do not infer a cause that the investigation has not established.

Question

  • Guide question D04
  • Transfer

The theater's new curtain could be lowered safely only when the center of the stage was empty. In the original staging, two performers remained in that area during the scene change. ______, the director revised their exit route so that they left the center before the curtain began to descend.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Connect a documented limitation with its consequence | Review lesson 1.6

Correct choice: For this reason

The safety requirement and the performers' original positions create a problem. The revised route responds directly to that problem, making "For this reason" appropriate. The passage states a reason for the change rather than merely reporting overlapping events.

A: "Meanwhile" presents simultaneous events, but the revision is explained by an earlier staging problem rather than described as occurring during it.

C: No earlier route revision provides a parallel action for the director's change.

D: The revision accommodates the safety restriction; it is not an opposite case or reversal of that restriction.

If you missed it: Check whether the first facts supply a reason for the next action. A response to an explicit constraint can support a causal transition.

Question

  • Guide question D05
  • Foundation

A researcher classifies a sound as intermittent when periods of sound alternate with periods of silence. ______, an intermittent sound stops and starts rather than continuing without a break. This definition concerns the sound's timing, so either a loud signal or a quiet signal can be intermittent.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Recognize a precise restatement | Review lesson 1.8

Correct choice: In other words

The second sentence expresses the same timing pattern in simpler language: alternating sound and silence means stopping and starting. "In other words" marks that equivalence; no particular intermittent sound is introduced.

A: An example would identify a particular sound or situation; this sentence restates the definition for the whole category.

B: The two descriptions agree about the sound's timing rather than opposing one another.

C: Stopping and starting is what the definition means, not a separate event caused by the act of classification.

If you missed it: Test whether the second sentence could replace the first without changing the central meaning. If so, consider clarification.

Question

  • Guide question D06
  • Transfer

An engineer first modeled the conveyor as moving at a constant speed, treating its brief loading stops as negligible. Tests showed that omitting the stops substantially understated the time needed to complete a delivery. The original assumption was therefore discarded. ______, the current model represents each loading stop as a separate interval in its calculation of total delivery time.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Track a replacement across the whole passage | Review lesson 1.12

Correct choice: Instead

The blank links the discarded assumption to its replacement: the current model counts stops that the original model omitted. "Instead" captures that substitution across the passage. The sentence is not an illustration of the discarded assumption; it describes the new approach.

A: The current model treats stops differently from the original model, so similarity would misrepresent the change.

C: Representing stops separately does not exemplify omitting those stops or discarding an assumption; it identifies the replacement method.

D: The sentence explicitly describes the current model, not an earlier stage in the modeling process.

If you missed it: Look beyond the closest sentence. Identify what was abandoned and what took its place before selecting a transition.

Question

  • Guide question D07
  • Foundation

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. A greenhouse team grows native grass seedlings for a restoration project along a riverbank.

2. The team originally placed its seedling trays on flat wooden benches near the greenhouse entrance.

3. Water collected under the trays on those benches and drained away slowly after watering.

4. The team moved the trays to mesh benches because openings in the mesh allowed water to drain more freely.

5. The greenhouse also has a separate room where seeds are stored before planting.

The student wants to explain why the greenhouse team moved its seedling trays to mesh benches. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Select only facts that accomplish a stated explanatory goal | Review lesson 2.1

Correct choice: The team moved its seedling trays to mesh benches because the openings allowed freer drainage than the flat wooden benches provided.

Notes 3 and 4 supply the problem and the reason for the move: slow drainage under the old benches and freer drainage through mesh. C connects the move to that reason. The project's location and seed storage do not explain the bench choice.

A: This accurately combines notes 1 and 5, but it says nothing about drainage or the reason for moving the trays.

B: This accurately gives the original arrangement in note 2, but it does not explain why that arrangement changed.

D: This is consistent with notes 1 and 4, but identifying the seedlings' intended use does not explain the switch to mesh.

If you missed it: Rephrase the goal as a question: Why mesh benches? Keep only facts that answer that question.

Question

  • Guide question D08
  • Transfer

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. Nera Voss is a ceramic artist who makes sculptures inspired by the movement of water.

2. Her sculpture Tidal Fold consists of twelve curved clay panels arranged in a shallow spiral.

3. The panels have a blue-gray glaze that becomes darker toward the center of the sculpture.

4. Voss began making the panels for Tidal Fold in a rented studio in the town of Aven.

5. The sculpture was first displayed in the town's community hall before being moved to a sculpture garden.

The student wants to introduce Tidal Fold to an audience unfamiliar with both the work and its creator. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Introduce a creator and work to an unfamiliar audience | Review lesson 2.2

Correct choice: Tidal Fold, a sculpture by ceramic artist Nera Voss, consists of twelve curved clay panels arranged in a shallow spiral.

Notes 1 and 2 identify the creator's medium, the work's name, and its basic form. A supplies that orientation for an unfamiliar audience by identifying Voss as a ceramic artist and describing the sculpture's clay panels.

B: This accurately describes the surface treatment in note 3, but it never identifies the creator or introduces her artistic medium.

C: This accurately combines production and display details, but it omits the work's name and does not identify Voss as its ceramic artist.

D: This accurately gives display history from note 5, but it omits the creator and the work's physical character.

If you missed it: For an unfamiliar audience, check whether the sentence identifies both who made the work and what kind of work it is.

Question

  • Guide question D09
  • Transfer

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. The fictional languages Orin and Talek both use suffixes to indicate grammatical information about nouns.

2. In Orin, the suffix -ma marks a noun as plural, regardless of whether the noun names a person or an object.

3. In Talek, nouns naming people take the plural suffix -li, while nouns naming objects take the plural suffix -nu.

4. Both languages put the plural suffix after the noun stem rather than before it.

5. The two languages also use separate suffixes to indicate possession, a feature not addressed in this comparison.

The student wants to emphasize how Orin and Talek differ in their choice of plural suffix. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Compare a difference on a shared dimension | Review lesson 2.6

Correct choice: Orin uses the same plural suffix for people and objects, whereas Talek chooses between two plural suffixes according to what a noun names.

Notes 2 and 3 establish the requested difference: Orin uses one plural suffix for both categories; Talek distinguishes the categories. A compares the same feature in both languages. Note 4 rules out the placement contrast asserted in D.

B: This accurately states similarities from notes 1, 4, and 5, but the goal asks for a difference in plural-suffix choice.

C: This accurately describes Talek in note 3, but it gives no Orin comparison and therefore does not state the difference between the languages.

D: This contradicts note 4, which says both languages place plural suffixes after the noun stem.

If you missed it: Make a two-column comparison on the requested feature. The answer must describe both sides of that same comparison.

Question

  • Guide question D10
  • Transfer

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. The city of Bellhaven offered a free bicycle-repair workshop on four Saturdays in one month.

2. The workshop was advertised on the city website and on posters near the railway station.

3. Of the 64 participants, 41 reported that they had never repaired a bicycle before attending.

4. Participants practiced adjusting brakes and replacing inner tubes using bicycles supplied by the organizers.

5. The organizers chose a public square with sufficient space and set up six workstations there for participants to use.

The student wants to support the claim that the workshop reached people without previous bicycle-repair experience. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Choose evidence that directly supports a specified claim | Review lesson 2.8

Correct choice: Of the workshop's 64 participants, 41 reported that they had never repaired a bicycle before they attended the city's free sessions.

Note 3 directly documents participants' previous experience: 41 of 64 reported none. C supplies that relevant evidence while preserving that it was self-reported. Advertising, activities, and cost do not establish the experience of the people reached.

A: The advertising facts are accurate, but the means of promotion does not establish whether attendees had previous repair experience.

B: The activities and available workstations are supported, but they do not identify participants' experience before attending.

D: The notes do not give the number of inexperienced city residents or show that free admission caused attendance.

If you missed it: Identify the exact claim being supported, then ask which note provides evidence about that claim rather than background about the event.

Question

  • Guide question D11
  • Transfer

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. A research team investigated whether the pattern of openings in a shade screen affected the temperature beneath it.

2. The team compared two screens made from the same material and containing the same total area of openings.

3. One screen had evenly spaced openings; the other had openings clustered near its edges.

4. At noon on each of six clear days, the team measured air temperature beneath both screens at the same height.

5. Across those measurements, the mean temperature beneath the evenly spaced screen was 1.2 degrees Celsius lower than beneath the clustered screen.

The student wants to report the study's principal finding while retaining the time of day, weather, and number of days represented in the measurements. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Distinguish a study finding from its purpose and method | Review lesson 2.10

Correct choice: In noon measurements on six clear days, the mean temperature beneath the evenly spaced screen was 1.2 degrees Celsius lower than beneath the clustered screen.

Notes 4 and 5 give the measurement conditions and comparative finding. B combines them and preserves the mean difference. A states the purpose, and C describes the method; both are accurate but omit the principal result.

A: This accurately states the investigation's purpose using notes 1 and 2, but it reports no finding or measurement conditions.

C: This accurately describes the method in note 4, but it does not say what the measurements showed.

D: This turns a result under specified conditions into a universal claim about all screens, weather, and times, which the notes do not support.

If you missed it: Label each note purpose, method, or finding. A request for a qualified finding usually requires both the result and its relevant limits.

Question

  • Guide question D12
  • Challenge

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. Two neighborhood teams surveyed residents about whether they preferred an evening or a morning community meeting.

2. Team East received 80 responses, of which 48 indicated a preference for an evening meeting.

3. Team West received 50 responses, of which 35 indicated a preference for an evening meeting.

4. Each respondent selected exactly one of the two meeting times on the survey.

5. The teams did not record the ages of respondents or determine how many residents in each neighborhood saw the survey.

The student wants to compare the percentages of respondents in the two surveys who preferred an evening meeting. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Compare proportions with different denominators | Review lesson 2.11

Correct choice: Evening meetings were preferred by 60 percent of East respondents and 70 percent of West respondents, a difference of ten percentage points.

Notes 2 and 3 give 48/80 = 60 percent and 35/50 = 70 percent. The difference is ten percentage points. D compares the requested percentages while keeping the denominator as survey respondents rather than all residents.

A: The counts and difference are correct, but a comparison of raw counts does not accomplish the requested comparison of percentages.

B: The East percentage is correct, but the West denominator changes from respondents to all residents, whose preferences are unknown.

C: The total number of responses is correct, but it does not compare the percentages favoring evening meetings.

If you missed it: Compute each percentage using its own sample size. Keep percentage points distinct from counts and preserve the population actually surveyed.
