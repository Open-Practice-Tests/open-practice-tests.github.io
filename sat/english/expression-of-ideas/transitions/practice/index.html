Question 7

  • Guide question 1.07
  • Foundation

During a test of a model weather vane, an engineering team found that trapped water was preventing the vane's base from turning freely. The water had accumulated because paint had sealed the base's drainage hole. ______, the team reopened the hole to let the water escape before testing the vane again.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Trace a problem to the action it prompts | Review lesson 1.1

Correct choice: Accordingly

The first two sentences identify a specific problem and its source: water interferes with rotation because the drain is sealed. Reopening the drain directly addresses that source. 'Accordingly' connects the diagnosis to the team's appropriate response. The final sentence adds an action prompted by the findings; it does not merely repeat the findings or offer another comparable case.

A: 'Similarly' calls for a parallel case or action. Diagnosing trapped water and reopening a hole play different roles in one problem-and-response sequence.

B: 'For instance' would make reopening the drain an example of water accumulating behind a seal. Reopening the drain instead reverses the condition that allowed the accumulation.

D: 'In other words' would treat the repair as a restatement of the diagnosis. Identifying a sealed drain and taking action to open it are distinct events.

If you missed it: Write 'Because the drain was sealed, the team...' and finish with the actual action. This checks the direction of the connection.

Question 8

  • Guide question 1.08
  • Transfer

The director decided against shortening the play's long opening monologue when preparing the revised script. ______, she divided that monologue among three characters, preserving every line while allowing the characters to interrupt one another. The change made the same material sound like a conversation rather than a speech.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Recognize substitution rather than elaboration | Review lesson 1.1

Correct choice: Instead

The director rejects one revision strategy, shortening the monologue, and adopts a different strategy, distributing its existing lines. 'Instead' signals that replacement of approaches. The final sentence confirms that the content remains while its presentation changes. The key does not require interpreting the revisions as chronologically separate productions; both actions concern the choice made for one revised script.

B: 'Conversely' would reverse a stated relationship. Preserving the monologue's length while redistributing its lines does not reverse the decision against shortening; it supplies the alternative chosen after that decision.

C: 'Likewise' would signal matching actions. Choosing not to shorten and choosing to redistribute are distinct strategies with different effects, not repetitions of the same strategy.

D: 'In other words' would equate deciding against shortening with distributing lines among characters. The director could preserve length in many ways; the redistribution adds a particular alternative action.

If you missed it: Separate the rejected action from the adopted action. Ask whether the second replaces the first or simply describes the same action again.

Question 9

  • Guide question 1.09
  • Challenge

A critic argues that changes of narrator in the novel Lantern Street always leave readers unsure whose judgment shapes a scene. The critic's discussion gives particular attention to the transition between the novel's third and fourth chapters. ______, that transition names the new narrator and immediately states her opinion of the dispute, making the source of the judgment unambiguous.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Connect evidence to an earlier generalization across intervening context | Review lesson 1.1

Correct choice: However

The final sentence responds to the critic's generalization in the first sentence. The critic claims every narrator change produces uncertainty, whereas the selected transition makes the judging narrator explicit. 'However' marks that challenge. The middle sentence identifies the passage under discussion without endorsing the critic's interpretation; connecting the blank only to the fact that the critic discusses it would lose the argument.

A: 'For example' would make an unambiguous change of narrator illustrate the claim that changes always create uncertainty. It provides evidence against that generalization instead.

B: 'In other words' would make the final description rephrase the critic's claim or the fact of the critic's attention. Naming a narrator clearly does neither and contradicts the claimed uncertainty.

C: 'Likewise' would imply that this transition repeats the pattern of leaving readers unsure. The sentence explicitly says that the source of judgment is unambiguous.

If you missed it: When a middle sentence only identifies evidence, connect the final interpretation back to the original claim. Preserve absolute words such as 'always.'

Question 10

  • Guide question 1.10
  • Foundation

The town's bicycle-share service charges less for a day pass than the nearby private rental shop does. That low price makes the service affordable for occasional riders. ______, the service keeps bicycles available at the train station throughout the evening, giving arriving passengers a convenient way to complete their trips.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Add a distinct advantage to the same assessment | Review lesson 1.2

Correct choice: Additionally

The service has two separate advantages: a relatively low price and evening availability at a useful location. 'Additionally' adds the second advantage to the favorable assessment. The low price does not itself establish the hours or the station location, and those operating arrangements are not a different way of saying that the price is low.

A: 'As a result' would present the station's evening bicycle availability as caused by the low pass price. No link between price and operating arrangements is given.

C: 'By contrast' would oppose convenient evening access to affordable pricing. The two features are compatible advantages and do not occupy opposite positions on a shared dimension.

D: 'In other words' would make convenient access a restatement of affordability. Location and hours supply new information about the service, separate from its price.

If you missed it: Name each advantage with a different noun phrase, such as 'low price' and 'evening access.' Then check whether one actually produces the other.

Question 11

  • Guide question 1.11
  • Transfer

A workshop rejected a supplier's proposed floor coating after finding that it wore away within a month of ordinary foot traffic. The resulting bare patches would require frequent repairs. ______, the coating gave off a strong odor even before anyone walked on it, making it unsuitable for the workshop's poorly ventilated rooms.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Add independent evidence against a proposal | Review lesson 1.2

Correct choice: Moreover

Rapid wear and a strong odor are independent reasons to reject the coating. 'Moreover' introduces the second reason. The detail that the odor exists before foot traffic matters: the passage specifically separates it from wear, so the odor cannot be treated as a stated consequence of the coating's wearing away. Both problems reinforce the rejection rather than oppose each other.

A: 'Therefore' would make the odor follow from wear or the need for repairs. The passage says the odor was present before the traffic that caused wear, and gives no such causal connection.

B: 'In other words' would equate poor durability with a strong odor. These are different defects affecting maintenance and ventilation, respectively.

D: 'Conversely' would reverse the preceding relationship or introduce an opposing condition. The odor supplies another unfavorable feature rather than an opposite to rapid wear.

If you missed it: Check the timing detail, but use it to test causation rather than to assume the question is about chronology. Both defects support the same decision.

Question 12

  • Guide question 1.12
  • Challenge

The council's plan to retain the old rail station would allow evening classes to use its empty waiting room. Classes would meet after the last departure, so passengers' access would be unaffected. ______, a separate inspection found that retaining the station would avoid the expense of demolishing a wall that also supports the neighboring building.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Accumulate independent reasons across different aspects of a proposal | Review lesson 1.2

Correct choice: Furthermore

The passage gives two reasons to retain the station: its space can serve classes without disrupting passengers, and retaining it avoids a difficult demolition expense. 'Furthermore' adds the inspection's distinct financial reason. The immediate sentence concerns scheduling, but the new finding contributes to the larger discussion of retention. Neither the evening schedule nor the classroom use causes the wall to support another building.

B: 'As a result' would make the inspection's structural finding or demolition expense follow from the class schedule. The finding concerns an existing wall and is presented as independent evidence.

C: 'In other words' would make avoiding demolition costs a rephrasing of avoiding disruption to passengers. Those outcomes concern different groups, constraints, and expenses.

D: 'By contrast' would oppose the financial benefit to the scheduling benefit. Both support retaining the station; the change from educational use to construction costs does not itself establish contrast.

If you missed it: Label the two reasons by their contributions to the council's decision. A shift between scheduling and construction does not alone create opposition.

Question 13

  • Guide question 1.13
  • Foundation

During outdoor rehearsals, a theater director stops the actors whenever wind prevents them from hearing a spoken cue; rehearsal resumes only after the cue can be heard clearly. ______, a field research leader suspends sampling whenever fog hides the team's signal flags and restarts it only when the flags are visible again.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Identify a parallel response in different settings | Review lesson 1.3

Correct choice: Similarly

Both leaders pause coordinated work when a necessary signal cannot be perceived and resume when that signal becomes clear. 'Similarly' identifies that shared operating rule. Sound and visibility differ, but they serve the same role in the two cases. The second situation is a separate example of the same abstract pattern, not an example contained within the first specific theater rehearsal.

A: 'Instead' would make the research leader's practice replace the director's practice. The passage describes separate activities in different settings, with neither replacing the other.

B: 'Consequently' would claim that the theater director's rule causes the field research rule. No interaction or influence between the two leaders is supplied.

C: 'For instance' would present field sampling as an instance of the specific outdoor theater rehearsal just described. It is a different activity that parallels the rehearsal's response to unclear signals.

If you missed it: Write the shared rule without using 'actors,' 'research,' 'wind,' or 'fog.' If one precise rule fits both cases, a parallel signal has support.

Question 14

  • Guide question 1.14
  • Transfer

To guide a reader's attention, illustrator Sana enlarges a minor character's hands far beyond their realistic proportions while leaving the rest of the figure small. ______, mapmaker Eron draws tiny footbridges much larger than their true scale so that walkers will notice these important crossings along a route.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Compare a shared technique across different media | Review lesson 1.3

Correct choice: Likewise

Sana and Eron both enlarge selected features beyond realistic scale to direct an audience's attention. 'Likewise' carries that technique-and-purpose relationship from illustration to mapmaking. The subjects are different, but their use of selective enlargement is comparable. The passage does not claim that the map rephrases the illustration or that one creator's choice caused the other's.

A: 'Instead' would signal that Eron's mapmaking substitutes for Sana's illustration technique. These are independent works by different creators, not competing options in one decision.

B: 'As a result' would make Eron's enlarged bridges an effect of Sana's enlarged hands. The passage establishes a shared technique but no causal influence.

D: 'In other words' would make a claim about footbridges restate a claim about a character's hands. The new sentence introduces a different creator, object, and audience while preserving a parallel technique.

If you missed it: Compare both the action and its purpose: selective enlargement directs attention. Mere difference of medium does not defeat that parallel.

Question 15

  • Guide question 1.15
  • Challenge

Composer Lera describes precision as her chief artistic value, whereas composer Dovan emphasizes spontaneous variation. In rehearsals, Lera requires performers to identify ambiguities in their instructions before a passage is played again. ______, Dovan will not repeat a passage until players have voiced any uncertainty about its instructions.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Track a local parallel within a broader contrast | Review lesson 1.3

Correct choice: Similarly

The composers' artistic values differ, but their stated rehearsal rules match: players must voice uncertainty before the ensemble repeats a passage. 'Similarly' connects those local policies. The negative construction 'will not...until' expresses the same prerequisite as 'requires...before.' The opening contrast cannot be carried automatically into every later comparison; the passage changes from artistic values to a specific rehearsal practice.

A: 'For example' would make Dovan's policy an instance of Lera's policy for her own performers. It is a separate composer's matching rule, not an event within Lera's rehearsals.

C: 'By contrast' would oppose the two rehearsal requirements. Once the negative construction is unpacked, both require uncertainty to be voiced before repetition; the earlier difference in values concerns another dimension.

D: 'Consequently' would claim that Lera's requirement produces Dovan's rule. The text provides no influence or causal relationship between their rehearsals.

If you missed it: Translate both policies into the same form: 'First voice uncertainty, then repeat.' Compare that sequence before returning to the opening difference.

Question 16

  • Guide question 1.16
  • Foundation

In the first version of the poem Harbor Road, the narrator explains exactly why she leaves her hometown, giving readers a clear account of her motives. ______, in the final version, she offers no reason for leaving and refuses another character's request for an explanation. Her motive remains unresolved when the poem ends.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Contrast opposing treatments of the same narrative information | Review lesson 1.4

Correct choice: By contrast

The versions differ on one clear dimension: whether readers receive an explanation for the narrator's departure. The first supplies an explicit motive; the final version withholds it. 'By contrast' marks those opposing treatments of the same narrative information. The poem's title and the fact of revision are background; the answer depends on the difference in access to the narrator's reasons.

B: 'Similarly' would make the final version's refusal to explain parallel the first version's explicit explanation. On the dimension under discussion, the versions do the opposite.

C: 'Therefore' would claim that the first version's clarity caused the final version's withholding. The passage reports a difference between versions but gives no reason for the revision.

D: 'For instance' would make the unexplained departure an example of an explicitly explained departure. The final version withholds the information that the first provides.

If you missed it: Use the same question for both versions: 'Does the narrator explain why she leaves?' Compare the two answers.

Review lesson: 1.4 Mark a meaningful contrast on a shared dimension

Question 17

  • Guide question 1.17
  • Transfer

The fictional city of Daven grants full civic membership only to people born within its boundaries; adult newcomers remain ineligible regardless of how long they live there. ______, the neighboring city of Merev grants membership to every adult after five years of residence, regardless of birthplace. The two charters thus use different tests of belonging.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Compare opposed membership criteria | Review lesson 1.4

Correct choice: By contrast

Daven makes birthplace a necessary condition and does not let residence compensate for it. Merev grants membership through residence without regard to birthplace. 'By contrast' introduces that difference in eligibility criteria. The final sentence confirms the comparison, but the two rules already establish it. Both cities have membership rules; that broad similarity does not erase their opposite treatment of adult newcomers.

A: 'In other words' would make Merev's residence rule a restatement of Daven's birthplace rule. A long-term resident born elsewhere is excluded by one and included by the other.

B: 'For example' would make Merev's policy an instance of Daven's birth-only eligibility. Merev explicitly allows membership regardless of birthplace, so it does not instantiate that rule.

D: 'Consequently' would make Merev's charter a result of Daven's charter. No historical influence, policy response, or other causal relationship is given.

If you missed it: Test one hypothetical adult born elsewhere who has lived locally for six years. The different eligibility outcomes make the contrast concrete.

Question 18

  • Guide question 1.18
  • Challenge

Loran's mobile library recorded 1,200 loans across 20 visits in spring and 1,800 loans across 60 visits in summer. A report highlighted the rise of 600 loans as evidence of growing use. ______, each summer visit generated only half as many loans on average as a spring visit, so the favorable impression did not hold when use was measured per visit.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Contrast a favorable total with a different per-visit trend | Review lesson 1.4

Correct choice: However

The report emphasizes a growing total, but the final sentence limits that favorable interpretation using loans per visit. Spring averaged 1,200 divided by 20, or 60 loans per visit; summer averaged 1,800 divided by 60, or 30. 'However' signals that the second measure points in the opposite direction. Both numerical claims can be true because the number of visits also changed.

A: 'Similarly' would suggest that average loans per visit follow the report's upward pattern. They fall by half, so the direction is different on the measure being introduced.

B: 'For example' would make declining loans per visit illustrate the report's favorable growth claim. The final sentence instead limits that impression by changing to a specified denominator.

C: 'In other words' would equate a rise in total loans with a fall in loans per visit. These are different measurements with different trends, not alternate wording for one claim.

If you missed it: Write both denominators. Do not assume that growth in a total means growth per event, per person, or per unit.

Question 19

  • Guide question 1.19
  • Foundation

Under the orienteering contest's rules, any team that failed to register at even one checkpoint would receive no ranking. Vale's team realized halfway through the course that it had passed a checkpoint without registering. ______, the team completed the remaining course, although its members knew they could no longer earn a place in the results.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Mark persistence despite the loss of a stated incentive | Review lesson 1.5

Correct choice: Nevertheless

The missed checkpoint removes the team's chance of a ranking. The final sentence reports that the team continues despite losing that stated incentive, and 'Nevertheless' marks the concession. Both facts remain true: the team is ineligible for a ranking and completes the course. Completion is not described as repairing the missed registration or restoring eligibility.

A: 'Consequently' would make the loss of ranking eligibility the stated reason for completing the course. The text frames completion as persistence despite that loss and supplies no mechanism by which ineligibility requires completion.

C: 'Previously' would place completion of the remaining course before the team's halfway realization. In this account, that remaining portion is completed after the discovery, as a decision to continue despite it.

D: 'In other words' would equate missing a registration with completing the course. The final sentence reports a separate action; it does not restate the mistake or its rule-based penalty.

If you missed it: State the incentive that has disappeared, then identify the action that continues. Do not change 'completed the course' into 'earned a ranking.'

Review lesson: 1.5 Acknowledge a fact, then reverse its expected implication

Question 20

  • Guide question 1.20
  • Transfer

The last page of novelist Taren's handwritten manuscript is missing, leading a researcher to expect that the final paragraph's exact wording could not be recovered. ______, a complete typescript discovered in a printer's records preserves that paragraph word for word. The handwritten page remains lost, but the wording is no longer uncertain.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Preserve an obstacle while recognizing an independent way around it | Review lesson 1.5

Correct choice: Nevertheless

The missing page creates an explicitly stated expectation that the wording will remain unknown. The complete typescript defeats that expectation without changing the fact that the handwritten page is missing. 'Nevertheless' marks the concession. The passage distinguishes loss of one physical object from loss of the information it contained, so the new evidence can resolve one problem while the other persists.

B: 'For instance' would make a complete copy illustrate the expectation that the wording cannot be recovered. The copy instead supplies the wording and defeats that expectation.

C: 'Consequently' would make the typescript's preservation of the paragraph a consequence of the handwritten page's disappearance or the researcher's expectation. No such causal link is given.

D: 'Likewise' would make the complete typescript parallel the incomplete manuscript on the issue of recoverable wording. One lacks the needed paragraph, while the other preserves it in full.

If you missed it: Separate the physical manuscript from its verbal content. Ask which loss remains true and which expected limitation the new source removes.

Question 21

  • Guide question 1.21
  • Challenge

A town's forecasting model regularly misestimated water demand on individual days, sometimes by a wide margin. Those daily errors led a reviewer to recommend abandoning the model entirely. ______, the model's monthly totals were close enough to actual use for the town's billing estimates, so officials retained it solely for that narrower purpose.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Distinguish a valid limited use from a conceded weakness | Review lesson 1.5

Correct choice: Even so

The daily errors and the reviewer's recommendation create a reason to expect complete abandonment. 'Even so' introduces continued use despite that reason. The continued use has a narrower scope: monthly billing estimates, not daily forecasts. The passage does not retract the daily failures or claim the model is generally accurate; it explains why those failures do not settle every possible use.

A: 'In other words' would make acceptable monthly estimates restate poor daily forecasts or the recommendation to abandon the model. The final sentence introduces a distinct performance level and a different decision.

B: 'For example' would make retaining the model for monthly estimates illustrate abandoning it entirely or its daily errors. The retained use is an exception to the proposed all-purpose rejection.

D: 'As a result' would make the monthly totals' accuracy follow from daily inaccuracy or the reviewer's recommendation. The passage establishes no reason those earlier facts produced accurate monthly totals.

If you missed it: Underline 'individual days,' 'monthly totals,' and 'solely.' Check whether the conclusion preserves the admitted weakness while limiting its practical implication.

Question 22

  • Guide question 1.22
  • Foundation

An inspection rover draws power for both its drive motor and its lamp from a single battery. During an overnight test, a short circuit in the lamp drained that battery completely. ______, the rover's drive motor stopped, even though an inspection found no damage to the motor itself.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Connect an explicit power failure to its consequence | Review lesson 1.6

Correct choice: Consequently

The opening sentence establishes the mechanism: the motor depends on the same battery as the lamp. When the lamp's fault drains that battery, the motor loses its power supply and stops. 'Consequently' signals this supported result. The lack of motor damage does not undo the explanation, because a functioning motor still needs power from the now-empty battery.

A: 'By contrast' would frame the motor's stopping as an opposing result. The shared, fully depleted power source instead explains why the motor also ceases operating.

B: 'Instead' would present the motor's stopping as an alternative to the battery's depletion. The motor stops because the battery is depleted; neither event replaces the other.

C: 'In other words' would equate the battery's depletion with the motor's stopping. The two are distinct events connected through the power supply, not merely two phrasings of one event.

If you missed it: Trace the supplied mechanism from lamp fault to empty battery to motor stoppage. Use that dependency instead of assuming every stopped motor must be damaged.

Question 23

  • Guide question 1.23
  • Transfer

Readers of a language-learning glossary reported that they could find a verb's basic meaning but could not tell how its form changed in the past tense. The entries supplied only the dictionary form of each verb. ______, the editor revised every verb entry to include a past-tense form and a sentence showing that form in use.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Connect a documented user need to a targeted response | Review lesson 1.6

Correct choice: Accordingly

The readers need information that the existing entries omit: past-tense forms. The editor adds precisely that information, along with an example of its use. 'Accordingly' connects the documented limitation to a targeted response. The final sentence changes the glossary in light of the problem; it neither claims the readers were mistaken nor rephrases the original entries.

B: 'On the contrary' would directly reject the preceding claim about what the old entries supplied. The revision accepts the omission and addresses it rather than asserting that the information was already present.

C: 'For instance' would make entries containing past-tense forms illustrate entries supplying only dictionary forms. The revision adds the very information the old entries lacked.

D: 'In other words' would equate the old, limited entries with the revised, fuller entries. The final sentence introduces an editorial action and new content, not a reformulation of the original condition.

If you missed it: Match the reported need with the exact information added. A response that fills an identified gap can support a result or appropriate-action transition.

Question 24

  • Guide question 1.24
  • Challenge

Six classrooms installed dimmable lighting and changed their morning start times during the same week. Average attention ratings rose afterward, but the project included no unchanged comparison classrooms, leaving unrelated influences unaccounted for. ______, the report describes the observed increase without claiming that either modification caused it.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Derive a cautious reporting decision from limits in study design | Review lesson 1.6

Correct choice: Therefore

The design cannot separate the two modifications or rule out unrelated influences. The final sentence reports a decision that follows from those limits: describe the increase while withholding a causal claim. 'Therefore' expresses that justified consequence for reporting. It does not claim that lighting or start times caused the increase; the reasoning concerns what the evidence permits the report to say.

A: 'Similarly' would introduce a parallel case or a matching reporting decision. The passage describes one project's evidence and then the reporting restraint that follows from its limits; it supplies no other report or decision to which this one is parallel.

C: 'Conversely' would reverse a preceding relationship. The final sentence does not reverse the design's limits; it follows them by avoiding a conclusion the design cannot establish.

D: 'By comparison' would introduce another study, group, or contrasting result for comparison. The sentence concerns the report of the same project and the consequence of its evidentiary limits.

If you missed it: Identify what actually follows the blank: a reporting decision, not a causal finding. A limitation can logically cause caution without proving anything about the intervention.

Question 25

  • Guide question 1.25
  • Foundation

Animals may change where they search for food when prey becomes scarce in their usual feeding area. ______, in a classroom simulation, virtual fish that normally fed near the center of a pond began searching along its banks after the central area's supply of insects decreased. Their shift gave students a visible instance of a response to changing food availability.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Recognize a particular case of a general behavior | Review lesson 1.7

Correct choice: For example

The opening sentence describes a general possibility: animals can shift their feeding locations when prey becomes scarce. The simulation supplies a particular instance, in which fish move from the pond's center to its banks after the central food supply declines. "For example" connects that instance to the general statement. The final sentence confirms the illustrative purpose. The reference changes from animals generally to a specific simulation, so the second sentence is not simply an equivalent restatement of the entire first claim.

A: "However" signals opposition. The fish behave exactly as the general statement allows: they change feeding locations after food becomes less available in their usual area.

B: "In other words" signals a restatement of the same claim. One simulated case does not restate the full general possibility; it illustrates that possibility with particular fish and a particular pond.

C: "Instead" signals replacement. The second sentence does describe fish replacing one feeding location with another, but the transition connects whole sentences: the case illustrates, rather than replaces, the opening general statement.

If you missed it: Distinguish the relationship inside the example from the relationship between sentences. The fish substitute a location; the writer supplies an example.

Question 26

  • Guide question 1.26
  • Transfer

The bridge crew's contract covered one precisely defined component of the structure. ______, workers would replace the hinges that attached the eastern drawbridge deck to its support frame. All other components, including the deck's wooden surface and the lifting cables, fell outside the contracted work.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Identify the exact content of a general reference | Review lesson 1.7

Correct choice: Specifically

The first sentence introduces a narrow assignment without naming its component. The next sentence identifies that component and the work to be performed: replacement of the eastern drawbridge hinges. "Specifically" appropriately moves from a general reference to its exact content. The last sentence reinforces the contract's limited scope by excluding the surface and cables. No change of contract occurs; the second sentence explains the assignment that was already introduced.

A: "Even so" would make hinge replacement seem unexpected despite the narrow contract. Replacing one named component is fully consistent with a contract restricted to one component.

C: "By comparison" requires another assignment or component to be evaluated alongside the first. The second sentence identifies the first sentence's unnamed component; it does not describe a separate contract.

D: "Instead" suggests a substitute for the preceding assignment. Hinge replacement is the content of that assignment, so no previously chosen work is being displaced.

If you missed it: Replace "one precisely defined component" with a question: "Which component?" A following answer to that question specifies the earlier reference.

Question 27

  • Guide question 1.27
  • Challenge

A data set can be internally consistent while omitting an entire category of observations. Checking the arithmetic cannot reveal this problem if all the recorded entries agree. ______, a transit questionnaire answered only by weekday riders could yield perfectly consistent estimates of weekday travel while revealing nothing about people who use the system exclusively on weekends.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Connect an illustration to the passage's broader methodological claim | Review lesson 1.7

Correct choice: For example

The passage distinguishes internal agreement from completeness. The questionnaire is an instance of that distinction: its weekday responses can agree even though weekend-only riders are absent. "For example" links the transit scenario to the opening claim and the following statement about arithmetic checks. The second sentence explains why a certain type of error could escape detection. The hypothetical questionnaire demonstrates that error; it does not repeat the general claim in equivalent terms or describe events happening during an arithmetic check.

A: "In other words" presents an equivalent restatement. The questionnaire is one possible data set with a particular omission, whereas the earlier statements concern a general limitation of internal consistency checks.

B: "On the contrary" announces a refutation. A consistent weekday sample that excludes weekend-only riders supports the claim that agreement among entries does not guarantee completeness.

D: "In the meantime" puts the questionnaire scenario within another ongoing activity or interval. The passage states a general limitation of arithmetic checks; it does not describe a check in progress while a questionnaire is being answered.

If you missed it: Name the earlier category: "consistent but incomplete data." Then ask whether the later case belongs to it or merely restates the entire general claim.

Question 28

  • Guide question 1.28
  • Foundation

For a library training exercise, a catalog is called open only if every document it lists can be read without either paying a fee or joining an organization. ______, all the listed documents in an open catalog must be available free of charge and without a membership requirement. A catalog's size is irrelevant to this definition.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Preserve every condition when restating a definition | Review lesson 1.8

Correct choice: In other words

Both sentences state the same access conditions: every listed document must be free to read, and reading cannot require organizational membership. B: "Instead" implies that the second access rule replaces the first. The conditions are identical, so the passage is not abandoning one definition for another.

C: "For example" would introduce a concrete member of the category, such as a named catalog with unrestricted documents. The second sentence remains a general statement about all open catalogs.

D: "Nevertheless" signals that the access conditions run against an expectation created by the definition. They repeat that definition exactly, so they do not present an unexpected qualification.

If you missed it: Check the quantifier and both conditions. An accurate paraphrase must retain every document, no fee, and no membership.
