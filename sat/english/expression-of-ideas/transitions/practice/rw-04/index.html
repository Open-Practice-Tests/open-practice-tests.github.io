Question 1

  • Guide question RW-04.01

A children’s theater production celebrates resourcefulness by showing characters repairing discarded objects. ______ its costumes are assembled from donated fabric scraps.

Which transition most precisely signals the appropriate match between theme and design?

Why this answer

A: No earlier time is being contrasted with the production.

B — correct: The reuse of scraps aptly reflects the theme of repairing and reusing.

C: The costumes support the theme rather than defying an expectation it creates.

D: Costume construction is a related design choice, not a paraphrase of the plot.

Question 2

  • Guide question RW-04.02

In each of the past four seasons, a larger proportion of visitors has requested a quiet tour of the sculpture garden. Visitors are ______ choosing that option.

Which expression captures the trend?

Why this answer

A: No contrary expectation is presented.

B: Previously locates an earlier event but does not express a growing share.

C: No chain of consequences is described.

D — correct: Increasingly states that the choice is becoming more common across seasons.

Question 3

  • Guide question RW-04.03

At first the editor treated the missing pages as an accident. A later inventory suggested deliberate removal, but further records undermined that theory too. ______ the editor concluded that the cause could not be determined.

Which transition best introduces the eventual judgment?

Why this answer

A — correct: The sentence gives the final realization after competing explanations were considered.

B: The final judgment is not an example of the preceding rejected theory.

C: Meanwhile would signal concurrent events, not the endpoint of this investigation.

D: The conclusion is not a step taken toward a previously stated goal.

Question 4

  • Guide question RW-04.04

Extending the rehearsal period allowed the actors to learn the scene changes. Their quicker scene changes, ______ reduced the length of the performance.

Which transition marks the next link in this consequence chain?

Why this answer

A: The reduction follows from quicker changes; it does not occur despite them.

B: The sentence describes an effect of the actors’ improvement, not an independent parallel case.

C — correct: Extra rehearsal led to quicker changes, which led to a shorter performance.

D: A shorter performance is an additional consequence, not a rewording of learning the changes.

Question 5

  • Guide question RW-04.05

Every eligible proposal must include a cost estimate. Proposal K has no cost estimate. It is, then, ineligible.

What does then signal in the final sentence?

Why this answer

A: The passage applies a requirement; it does not narrate successive events.

B — correct: Given the requirement and the missing estimate, ineligibility follows.

C: The proposal conforms to the rule’s consequence rather than creating an exception.

D: The conclusion depends on the missing estimate and does not retract it.

Question 6

  • Guide question RW-04.06

The potter marked each bowl’s base and then wrapped it. Elsewhere she wrote, “Every bowl in this crate is glazed; this bowl, then, needs no glazing.”

How does the meaning of then differ between these contexts?

Why this answer

A: Marking followed by wrapping is a sequence of actions.

B: This reverses the functions: the crate statement supports the inference.

C: The second use draws a conclusion from the all-bowls premise.

D — correct: First, wrapping comes after marking. Second, the known glazing of every bowl supports a conclusion about this one.
