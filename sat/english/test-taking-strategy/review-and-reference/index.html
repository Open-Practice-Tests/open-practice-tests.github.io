A flexible module plan

The standard adaptive Reading and Writing module has 27 questions and 32 minutes. [3] The plan below is a suggested rehearsal baseline. It assumes forward order and a goal of about 3 minutes for review; it is not an official pace, an individual-question deadline, or a score guarantee.

About 22:00

About 12:00

About 3:00

Recalculate from remaining work

Average first-pass budget = (remaining seconds - proposed review reserve) / items not yet first visited. With 14 minutes left, a 3-minute reserve, and 9 unseen items, the average is (840 - 180) /9 = about 73 seconds. One item might need 25 seconds and another 100; the average is a planning tool.

At the start, a 29-minute first pass means about 64 seconds per item on average. Across the whole 32-minute module, the arithmetic average is about 71 seconds. Neither number tells you how long a particular question deserves. Use the formula only while first visits remain; when that count reaches zero, switch to review.

If you change the route

Count items first visited rather than using the displayed question number as a progress measure. A marked answer and an unseen question are different kinds of remaining work. If the reserve becomes unrealistic, revise it deliberately and prioritize selecting responses; do not pretend the earlier plan is still intact.

For an approved nonstandard schedule, build a new plan from the time and navigation rules actually assigned to you. Do not multiply this table mechanically and assume every testing feature is unchanged.

Guessing and elimination

There is no deduction for an incorrect guess. [5] A guess is still not evidence that the answer is correct. If four choices are equally plausible and you select uniformly at random, the chance is 1/4. If you correctly eliminate two and choose uniformly between the remaining two, it is 1/2. Those assumptions matter: an incorrect elimination can discard the right answer, and surviving choices need not be equally plausible.

Use these fractions to understand why a supported elimination helps, not to estimate a scaled SAT score or to run calculations during the test. When no further evidence is available and time is needed elsewhere, select your best-supported remaining choice and move on.

First representation

Final check

Central idea or detail

Main claim and its qualification, or the exact requested fact.

Does the answer cover the passage's point at the right scope, or directly match the requested detail?

Inference

What the text establishes and what follows from it.

Did the answer add an unmentioned cause, setting, motive, or universal claim?

Textual evidence

The claim's subject, relationship, and required outcome.

Does the information support or weaken the precise claim, as the prompt requests, rather than address a neighboring topic?

Quantitative evidence

Relevant rows, columns, units, groups, and conditions.

Did you make the requested comparison with the correct rows, units, and denominators?

Words in context

A plain-language replacement consistent with the local argument.

Does the offered meaning fit this use, including contrast, tone, and grammatical role?

Purpose or structure

The job each major part performs.

Does the answer describe the author's move without turning a qualification into a rejection?

Cross-text connection

One concise position for each text.

What exact proposition is agreed on, challenged, qualified, or left unresolved?

Read technical and literary material for relationships

Keep unfamiliar names as labels. Track what a device measures, what a researcher infers, or what a character does. In a difficult sentence, locate the main subject and verb, then add the qualifications back. This is a comprehension aid, not permission to omit the details that decide the answer.

Avoid automatic rejection rules

Words such as all, never, only, and must require sufficient evidence. They can be correct when the passage supports them at the stated scope. An answer using mild language can still be wrong if it adds an unsupported claim.

Check structure, then meaning

Task

Efficient check

Common trap

Boundaries

Identify independent clauses and inserted descriptions. Include punctuation already outside the blank.

Treating a conjunctive adverb such as however as a coordinating conjunction, or closing an insertion with mismatched punctuation.

Agreement and verb form

Find the subject head and complete verb phrase; inspect time cues when tense is tested.

Matching the nearest noun or choosing the tense that sounds most conversational.

Modifiers and reference

Find the actor described by an opening phrase and the noun a pronoun must refer to.

Accepting a fluent sentence whose modifier or reference points to the wrong thing.

Transitions

Paraphrase the connected propositions and name their actual relationship.

Matching positive/negative tone, repeating a topic word, or ignoring an earlier controlling claim.

Rhetorical synthesis

Turn the goal into required content, comparison dimension, audience, and qualifications.

Selecting true notes that do a different job or satisfy only half the goal.

A final read has a purpose

Insert the choice exactly. Confirm that the whole sentence has the intended grammar and meaning. Check more context when the decision depends on chronology, reference, or the writer's broader claim. Reading the completed sentence is a test of a rule or relationship, not a vote on which version sounds nicest.

Example: if the goal requests both an observed improvement and a study limitation, a sentence giving only the improvement is incomplete even when every stated fact is true. On a verb question, finding a plural noun is not enough; it must be the head of the subject that controls the verb.

Turn a miss into a repair

Complete the record for wrong answers and uncertain correct answers. Write a diagnosis that would help you handle a fresh item. If you are unsure why the error happened, state a testable possibility rather than pretending the cause is known.

Test one change fairly

A strategy should help you make better decisions on unfamiliar work. A single higher raw score on a different set is suggestive at most: the material, prior exposure, and conditions may have changed too.

Before the comparison

Record

Specific change

For example: a short subject-verb check before reading all verb choices, or less extensive annotation.

What will stay similar

Fresh material, comparable skill mix and challenge, timing, device, and testing conditions as far as practical.

What improvement would look like

Fewer process errors or unfinished items while maintaining accuracy; enough time saved to justify any added step.

What could mislead you

Remembered questions, an easier set, a small sample, selective counting of successes, or more generous timing.

Decide what the evidence supports

  • Retain a change when repeated fresh work shows useful benefits without a concerning accuracy cost.
  • Modify a change when it helps one skill but creates unnecessary work elsewhere.
  • Remain undecided when forms differ substantially or the result is small and inconsistent.
  • Move to mixed or full-length official practice to see whether the routine survives the actual context. [8]

Arrive with a rehearsed plan

Use the current official instructions for your administration. The dates below describe SAT Weekend preparation as checked September 12, 2026; School Day students should follow their school's setup instructions. Device requirements and alerts can change.

When

Prepare

At least 30 days before

Begin device preparation and resolve school-managed-device needs. If needed, request a College Board loaner by its deadline; availability and approval matter. [12]

During the final 5 days

Complete SAT Weekend exam setup in Bluebook and obtain the admission ticket. For a College Board loaner, follow the approved early-arrival setup procedure. [13], [16]

Before leaving home

Check your admission ticket's time and location, current notices, device charge, and required items. Have your acceptable physical photo ID, current ticket, approved prepared device, and writing supplies. [14]

At the test center

Follow staff instructions and assigned timing. Use supplied scratch paper. Request help promptly for a technical problem rather than inventing a recovery procedure. [2], [16]

During the standard break

The 10-minute break is between Reading and Writing and Math. Follow the restrictions on phones, notes, and test-content discussion during breaks as well as testing. [2], [15]

Coverage and limits

The guide contains 24 worked examples and 135 independent practice items: 111 multiple-choice questions and 24 written drills. Independent practice includes a 12-question strategy diagnostic, 16 items in each of six chapters, and a 27-question pacing rehearsal. The rehearsal has 8 Craft and Structure, 7 Information and Ideas, 7 Standard English Conventions, and 5 Expression of Ideas items. This composition is an instructional choice, not a claim that every official module follows these counts.

For current official practice scores, use the appropriate College Board tools. Do not convert a local result out of 27, a chapter score, or a number of mistakes in this guide into a scaled SAT score.
