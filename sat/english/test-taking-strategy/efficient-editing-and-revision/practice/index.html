Question

  • Guide question 4.05
  • Foundation

Although the first survey covered only the southern half of the marsh______ volunteers documented more than forty bird species. The group plans to survey the northern half next spring, when several migratory species are expected to return.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Why this answer

Separate a dependent opener from the main clause | Review lesson 4.1

Correct choice: , the

The opening "Although" makes the first clause dependent. A comma closes that introductory clause, and the independent main clause begins "the volunteers documented." A supplies that structure.

B: The semicolon would place a dependent clause on its left. A semicolon cannot make "Although the first survey covered only the southern half of the marsh" stand alone.

C: The period turns the "Although" clause into a fragment. Capitalizing "The" does not repair the incomplete first sentence.

D: The colon requires an independent clause before it. The opening dependent clause cannot provide that complete statement.

If you missed it: Check the first word of the clause: a subordinating word can make a subject-verb group dependent.

Strategy in use: The contrast between limited coverage and many sightings helps the meaning, but the opening "Although" decides the punctuation structure.

Question

  • Guide question 4.06
  • Challenge

The manufacturer values the new alloy because it resists ______ the alloy is relatively light, it requires specialized tools to shape. Engineers are therefore testing several methods that might make the material easier to use in small workshops.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Why this answer

Track a dependent clause inside a new independent clause | Review lesson 4.1

Correct choice: corrosion; although

The first independent clause ends at "corrosion." The next independent clause is "although the alloy is relatively light, it requires specialized tools to shape." Its introductory dependent clause does not prevent the whole unit from being independent. D correctly places a semicolon between the two independent units.

A: The comma before "although" cannot join the two main clauses. Here "although the alloy is relatively light" introduces the following main clause, "it requires specialized tools to shape"; it does not eliminate that second main clause.

B: This inserts a comma immediately after "although," separating that subordinating word from its clause, and supplies no valid boundary after the first main clause.

C: With no mark after "corrosion," the text runs the first independent clause into a second independent unit containing an "although" opener. The comma after "light" belongs inside the second unit.

If you missed it: When a proposed second clause begins with "although," keep reading until you find whether a main clause follows it.

Strategy in use: Do not stop the structural analysis at the first dependent clause. The decisive fact is that "it requires" supplies a second main subject and verb.

Question

  • Guide question 4.07
  • Transfer

In her account of the restoration project, the curator emphasizes two practical ______ the stability of the pigments and the strength of the original backing. Both influenced the team's choice of materials for repairing the damaged panels.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Why this answer

Distinguish a list from an independent clause | Review lesson 4.1

Correct choice: considerations:

Before the blank, "the curator emphasizes two practical considerations" is a complete clause. What follows names those considerations in a two-part noun list. A colon introduces that list, making B correct.

A: A semicolon would require an independent clause after it. "The stability of the pigments and the strength of the original backing" contains two noun phrases but no finite verb.

C: The period leaves the list as a sentence fragment. The initial lowercase "the" also cannot begin the intended new sentence as printed.

D: Without punctuation, the complete clause runs directly into an explanatory list without the needed boundary after "considerations."

If you missed it: After a possible colon or semicolon, check whether you have a list or an independent clause.

Strategy in use: A long list can look like a clause. Searching for its finite verb takes less time than repeatedly reading it for rhythm.

Question

  • Guide question 4.08
  • Transfer

A laboratory uses two sensors to measure small changes in air pressure. After comparing the readings from both sensors with those from a reference instrument, ______. The next set of measurements showed much closer agreement.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Why this answer

Attach an introductory modifier to its actor | Review lesson 4.2

Correct choice: the researchers recalibrated both sensors

The introductory modifier describes the act of comparing readings. The subject immediately following that modifier must logically perform the comparison. C supplies "the researchers," who then recalibrated the sensors.

A: This makes "both sensors" the subject attached to "After comparing." The sensors did not perform the described comparison; placing "the researchers" later in a by-phrase does not fix that attachment.

B: The grammatical subject is "a recalibration," which cannot compare readings. Naming the researchers later leaves the introductory modifier attached to the wrong subject.

D: This makes "the readings" the subject that supposedly compared readings. The researchers appear only inside the following predicate.

If you missed it: Ask "Who did the introductory action?" and match that actor to the following main-clause subject.

Strategy in use: The choices all describe recalibration. The information is similar, but the grammatical placement of the actor decides the question.

Question

  • Guide question 4.09
  • Transfer

The museum opened its new photography exhibition in October 2022. By the time the first visitors entered the gallery, curator Leila Nasser ______ the arrangement of every photograph, including several large prints that had arrived only the previous afternoon.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Why this answer

Use chronology to select a verb form | Review lesson 4.2

Correct choice: had finalized

The opening and visitors' entrance are established past events. The arrangement was already complete by that past point. "Had finalized" expresses the earlier completed action, so D fits the chronology.

A: "Will finalize" places the action in the future relative to the present rather than before the stated past entrance of the visitors.

B: "Finalizes" uses the present tense, conflicting with the passage's established past timeframe and the "By the time" relationship.

C: "Has finalized" relates the completion to the present. The sentence instead locates it before another event in the past.

If you missed it: Write a two-event timeline: arrangement completed first, visitors entered second.

Strategy in use: Read the prior sentence here. The question cannot be solved safely by considering the four verb phrases without their time context.

Question

  • Guide question 4.10
  • Challenge

The pattern of narrow channels that carry water toward the roots of several desert plants ______ the subject of a new imaging study. Researchers hope the study will reveal how these channels remain functional when the surrounding soil becomes extremely dry.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Why this answer

Distinguish the main verb from verbs inside descriptions | Review lesson 4.2

Correct choice: has become

The main subject is singular "pattern." The clause "that carry water toward the roots of several desert plants" describes the plural "channels" and contains the verb "carry." The main clause still needs its own finite verb: "The pattern ... has become the subject." B provides that verb and agrees with the subject.

A: "Have become" agrees with a plural subject, but the main subject is "pattern," not "channels," "roots," or "plants."

C: "Becoming" alone is nonfinite and leaves the main clause without its required finite verb. The earlier "carry" belongs to the descriptive clause and cannot serve as the main verb.

D: "Become" is the present-tense form for a plural subject in this context. The singular subject "pattern" would require "becomes," which is not this choice.

If you missed it: Mark one core for the descriptive clause and another for the main clause before testing agreement.

Strategy in use: The trap combines two tasks: finding the true subject and recognizing that the earlier verb does not complete the main clause.

Question

  • Guide question 4.11
  • Foundation

The conservation team expected the fragile textile to tear when it was lifted from its storage frame. ______ the textile remained intact during the move, allowing the team to examine its underside for the first time in decades.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Recognize a defeated expectation | Review lesson 4.3

Correct choice: Nevertheless,

The expected outcome was tearing, but the actual outcome was that the textile remained intact. "Nevertheless" marks this defeated expectation, making A correct.

B: "Consequently" presents the intact textile as a result of the team's expectation. The expectation did not cause the actual outcome, which ran against it.

C: "Similarly" suggests that the second situation resembles the first. Here an expected tear and actual intactness differ in the very feature being discussed.

D: The actual outcome is not an example of the predicted tearing. It contradicts that prediction.

If you missed it: State the predicted result and actual result separately before choosing a connector.

Strategy in use: Use the relation between the two outcomes, not a general impression that both sentences concern conservation.

Question

  • Guide question 4.12
  • Challenge

Early descriptions treated the shield's metal rim as uniformly thick. Recent scans instead show that the rim's thickness varies substantially around its circumference. ______ the lower edge is almost twice as thick as the upper edge, while the two sides fall between those extremes.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Connect an elaboration to the current finding | Review lesson 4.3

Correct choice: Specifically,

The final sentence gives precise details of the variation reported immediately before it. "Specifically" introduces those details. The contrast with the earlier descriptions has already been expressed; the current sentence elaborates on the new scans' finding.

A: "Conversely" would signal a reversal of the preceding claim. The unequal thicknesses support, rather than reverse, the immediately preceding finding of variation.

B: The thickness differences are the content of the finding, not an effect caused by the scans' showing variation. "As a result" changes elaboration into an unsupported causal sequence.

D: The sentence states details of the measured rim, not an event occurring before the scans or another named event.

If you missed it: Identify the current claim before selecting a transition that reacts to an earlier claim.

Strategy in use: This paragraph has two moves: old view → new finding, then new finding → exact detail. Solve the move at the blank.

Question

  • Guide question 4.13
  • Transfer

For its final survey of the canyon path, the team selected a single mapping method. It rejected the proposed aerial survey because the overhanging rock concealed much of the route. ______ team members mapped the entire path on foot, using handheld instruments to record its position.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Why this answer

Distinguish a replacement from additional action | Review lesson 4.3

Correct choice: Instead,

The final sentence names the method used in place of the rejected aerial survey. "Instead" expresses this replacement. The statement that the team selected a single method reinforces the distinction between the proposed approach and the actual one.

A: "In addition" presents the mapping as another action added to the team's rejection. It misses the more precise relationship: mapping on foot is the method used in place of the rejected aerial survey.

C: Mapping on foot is not a restatement of rejecting an aerial survey. The first action rules out one approach; the second identifies the replacement approach.

D: Mapping the entire path on foot is not an example of the concealed route or of an aerial survey. It is the alternative chosen for the survey.

If you missed it: Ask whether the second action accompanies the first or replaces it.

Strategy in use: Notice the status of each method: "proposed," "rejected," and actually used. Topic similarity alone misses that sequence of decisions.

Question

  • Guide question 4.14
  • Transfer

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. A wicking bed is a planter that stores water in a reservoir below its soil.

2. A barrier separates the main body of soil from the lower reservoir.

3. Absorbent material draws water upward from the reservoir into the soil around plant roots.

4. Gardeners refill the reservoir through a vertical pipe, and an overflow opening limits the water level.

5. This design reduces the need to apply water directly to the soil surface.

The student wants to introduce a wicking bed to readers unfamiliar with it and explain how it supplies water to plants. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Introduce an unfamiliar device and explain its function | Review lesson 4.4

Correct choice: A wicking bed is a planter with a lower water reservoir from which absorbent material draws water upward into the soil around plant roots.

D identifies a wicking bed as a type of planter and explains the mechanism by which water reaches the soil around the roots. The first note supports the definition, and the third note supports the upward movement through absorbent material. Both parts of the goal are fulfilled.

A: This explains the water movement and one benefit, but it does not identify what a wicking bed is for an unfamiliar reader. D supplies that missing introduction as well as the mechanism.

B: This describes components and how a gardener refills the reservoir, but it does not explain how stored water reaches the plants. Refilling the device is not the requested supply mechanism.

C: This accurately describes refilling and overflow control. It neither identifies the bed as a planter nor explains movement of water to the roots.

If you missed it: Split a compound goal into its two requirements and point to the phrase satisfying each.

Strategy in use: The unfamiliar audience makes the definition useful. Do not discard it as extra wording merely because another choice states a true mechanism more briefly.

Question

  • Guide question 4.15
  • Transfer

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. Elena Vale created Harbor Wall in 2017 as a wall-mounted arrangement of discarded colored glass.

2. Vale often explores the changing appearance of industrial waterfronts in her artwork.

3. Tomas Ren completed Evening Grid in 2020, also using discarded colored glass in a wall-mounted work.

4. Evening Grid includes several shades of blue and green and was first displayed in a railway station.

5. Both works appeared in a 2024 exhibition about contemporary artists' use of salvaged materials.

The student wants to emphasize a specific similarity between Harbor Wall and Evening Grid. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Compare the named works using supported shared features | Review lesson 4.4

Correct choice: Both Harbor Wall and Evening Grid are wall-mounted works that incorporate discarded colored glass, although they were created by different artists.

A directly states a specific shared feature: both named works are wall-mounted and incorporate discarded colored glass. Each work's material and placement are explicitly supported by its own note. The sentence therefore accomplishes the requested comparison.

B: This gives a material for one work and a display location for the other. Both facts are supported, but the mismatched features do not emphasize a similarity.

C: This gives different creation dates and a color detail for only Evening Grid. It does not state a shared feature of the works.

D: This gives a general interest of Vale and a color detail from Ren's work. The facts are supported, but they do not establish a specific similarity between the two named works.

If you missed it: For a shared-feature claim, verify the same feature separately for each named object.

Strategy in use: Compare the actual works, not assumptions drawn from the artists' general interests.

Question

  • Guide question 4.16
  • Challenge

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

1. Researchers compared two trays with identical dimensions but different vent widths.

2. They predicted that the tray with wider vents would lose moisture faster.

3. Both trays began with equal amounts of water and remained together in the same test chamber for six days.

4. The narrow-vent tray lost eighteen grams of moisture, while the wide-vent tray lost twelve grams.

5. The researchers stated that the finding applied only to the trial conditions and should not be extended to other conditions without further testing.

The student wants to contrast the trial result with the researchers' prediction while retaining the stated limitation on the finding. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Why this answer

Contrast a result with a prediction without widening its scope | Review lesson 4.4

Correct choice: Contrary to the prediction, the narrow-vent tray lost more moisture than the wide-vent tray, although the finding applies only to the conditions of the single-chamber trial.

C explicitly contrasts the observed result with the prediction and preserves the limit on its scope. The researchers predicted greater loss from the wider vents, but the narrow-vent tray lost eighteen grams versus twelve. The final clause retains the stated restriction to this trial's conditions.

A: This states the prediction and design, but it omits the contrary result. It also does not convey the caution against applying the finding to other conditions.

B: The numbers accurately report the outcome and identify the setting. However, the sentence never states the prediction or explains that the result contradicted it, and merely naming the setting does not state the requested limitation.

D: This preserves the limitation and describes the comparison, but it does not state which tray lost more moisture or how that result differed from the prediction.

If you missed it: For a result-versus-expectation goal, identify the predicted direction, observed direction, and required qualification separately.

Strategy in use: Here you only need to compare eighteen with twelve. Avoid turning the task into an explanation of why the trays behaved differently; the notes supply no cause.
