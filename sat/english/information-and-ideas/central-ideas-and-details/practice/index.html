Question 5

  • Guide question 1.05
  • Foundation

A town's repair fair began with volunteers fixing broken lamps and bicycles. Soon visitors brought damaged jackets, asked how to sharpen tools, and exchanged spare parts. Organizers added short demonstrations so people could perform simple repairs themselves. The fair remained a place to mend belongings, but it also became a place to share practical knowledge.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Central idea across examples

Correct choice: The repair fair expanded from fixing objects to sharing repair knowledge.

The passage connects the original repair service with exchanges and demonstrations that spread knowledge. B covers that expansion.

A: Clothing was added; the original work was not abandoned.

C: No popularity comparison is reported.

D: The demonstrations cover simple repairs, not every object or problem.

If you missed it: Group the examples by what they collectively add to the original activity.

Question 6

  • Guide question 1.06
  • Foundation

For a hypothetical exhibition, curator Nia offered visitors either a printed map or a map on a handheld screen. Visitors using either version located the same number of rooms. Those using the printed map more often added handwritten notes. Nia recorded the behavior but did not ask why visitors made notes or whether the notes improved their later recall.

According to the text, what did Nia observe?

Why this answer

Precise reported detail

Correct choice: Printed-map users added handwritten notes more frequently.

The reported difference is the frequency of handwritten note-taking. The passage separates this observation from unmeasured reasons and effects.

A: Both groups located the same number of rooms.

B: Later recall was not assessed.

C: Nia did not ask visitors why they made notes.

If you missed it: Separate the recorded behavior from an explanation or benefit you might expect.

Question 7

  • Guide question 1.07
  • Transfer

The word 'restoration' can suggest returning an object to one original condition. Yet the imagined bell tower of Harrow Quay had been altered repeatedly: a clock was added, a stairway replaced, and storm damage repaired with different stone. Conservators chose to preserve evidence of these changes. For this tower, restoration meant protecting a history of alteration rather than recreating a single early appearance.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Finding the organizing comparison

Correct choice: Restoring the tower involved preserving its accumulated changes.

The examples of alteration support the conservators' decision to preserve the tower's layered history.

B: The text does not establish that the earliest appearance could be recovered.

C: This reverses the decision to preserve evidence of later changes.

D: The clock is an example of an addition; no malfunction is mentioned.

If you missed it: Identify which approach the passage contrasts with the approach actually chosen.

Question 8

  • Guide question 1.08
  • Transfer

Eli had planned to show his aunt the finished kite as soon as she arrived. When her car stopped outside, he slid the frame beneath his bed. One corner still sagged. His aunt called from the hall, and he answered that he would be down shortly. Then he lifted the frame again and tested the knot he had tied three times already.

According to the text, what did Eli do after answering his aunt?

Why this answer

Detail in literary prose

Correct choice: He checked a knot on the frame again.

The final sentence explicitly describes Eli lifting the frame and testing an already retied knot.

A: The passage does not show him going downstairs or finishing the kite.

B: A sagging corner is mentioned, but no replacement occurs.

D: He does not ask for assistance.

If you missed it: For a literal detail, follow the stated sequence before interpreting a character's feelings.

Question 9

  • Guide question 1.09
  • Transfer

In a hypothetical greenhouse study, seedlings beside pale walls grew more evenly than seedlings beside dark walls. The research team proposed that reflected light reached the pale-wall seedlings from more directions. Because the study did not measure the direction of incoming light, the team treated this account as a possible explanation rather than a confirmed mechanism.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Finding versus explanation

Correct choice: Seedling growth differed by wall color, while the suggested cause remained unconfirmed.

The growth difference is reported as an observation; the reflected-light mechanism is proposed but unmeasured.

A: Light direction was not measured, and equality in every direction is not claimed.

B: The passage reports a growth difference.

D: The study did not make the measurements this answer invokes.

If you missed it: Label each statement 'observed' or 'proposed' before summarizing the experiment.

Question 10

  • Guide question 1.10
  • Transfer

Historian Ada examines an imagined merchant's ledger listing pottery sales from three workshops. The ledger records prices and quantities but omits buyers' names. Ada can compare the workshops' recorded sales, yet she cannot use this document alone to determine whether their customers came from different social groups. Other surviving documents might help answer that separate question.

According to the text, what can Ada determine from the ledger?

Why this answer

Preserving a source limitation

Correct choice: How the three workshops' recorded prices and quantities compared.

The ledger contains prices and quantities, so it supports comparisons of those recorded sales features.

B: Buyer identities and social groups are not supplied.

C: This is specifically identified as a question the ledger alone cannot answer.

D: The ledger does not record buyer identities or motivations.

If you missed it: List the source's available fields and match the answer to those fields.

Question 11

  • Guide question 1.11
  • Transfer

On her first evening in the apartment, Sora unpacked nothing but a cup. The rooms seemed to belong to the previous tenant: pale rectangles marked absent pictures, and a chair had worn a hollow in the carpet. Then her neighbor knocked, holding a package delivered to the wrong door. They laughed over the nearly identical names on their mailboxes. Afterward, Sora opened the box containing her photographs.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Central idea of an emotional shift

Correct choice: A brief friendly encounter helps Sora begin making an unfamiliar apartment her own.

The initially unclaimed space, friendly encounter, and decision to unpack photographs together support a movement toward feeling at home.

A: The neighbor is not identified as the previous tenant.

B: The package prompts an encounter; the ending shows unpacking beginning.

C: Nothing indicates that Sora will copy the earlier arrangement.

If you missed it: Connect the character's initial behavior with the action at the end.

Question 12

  • Guide question 1.12
  • Challenge

In a hypothetical translation workshop, bilingual readers rated a literal version of a story as closer to the source's sentence structure than a freer version. A second group, who could read only the translation language, preferred the freer version's readability. Asked about conversational tone, the first group also favored the latter version. Yet neither group answered plot questions more accurately after reading that version. The organizers did not ask either group to rank the translations' overall accuracy.

According to the text, which statement accurately describes the workshop findings?

Why this answer

Tracking groups, references, and distinct evaluation criteria

Correct choice: Bilingual readers judged the literal version structurally closer to the source but preferred the freer version's tone; neither group answered plot questions better with the freer version.

"The first group" means bilingual readers, and "the latter version" means the freer translation. The bilingual readers judged the literal version structurally closer to the source but preferred the freer version's tone. Neither group answered plot questions more accurately with the freer version. Similarity ratings, preferences, and test performance are distinct outcomes.

A: The second group's reported preference concerns readability, not conversational tone. No tone preference from that group is reported.

C: The bilingual group also favored a feature of the freer version: its conversational tone.

D: Feature-specific judgments about structure, readability, and tone do not establish an overall preference. No overall preference is reported.

If you missed it: Resolve each group and version reference, then keep structure, tone, readability, and comprehension in separate columns.

Question 13

  • Guide question 1.13
  • Challenge

An imagined survey of an island's songs once counted every tune absent from printed collections as a recent composition. Later researchers found recordings of older singers performing several such tunes decades earlier. These recordings did not show that every unprinted tune was old; some had demonstrably recent composers. They did show that absence from print could no longer serve as sufficient evidence of recent origin.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Distinguishing a revised claim from its opposite

Correct choice: New evidence undermined a dating shortcut without showing that all unprinted tunes were old.

The recordings disprove the blanket dating shortcut for some songs. The passage expressly retains the possibility, and examples, of recent unprinted compositions.

B: No general comparison of printed and unprinted tunes' dates is established.

C: Several older performances do not provide complete composition dates.

D: The issue is the validity of a dating criterion, not a change in whose work receives emphasis.

If you missed it: State exactly which rule the evidence defeats, then avoid replacing it with an equally broad opposite rule.

Question 14

  • Guide question 1.14
  • Challenge

A hypothetical public workshop offered participants either a finished model or a partly assembled model to examine before building their own. Those given the finished model completed construction sooner. Those given the partial model were better at explaining why the pieces fit as they did, although the final constructions were equally accurate across the two groups. The organizers concluded that the better demonstration depended on whether speed or structural explanation was the priority.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Central relationship across two outcomes

Correct choice: Each model supported a different goal, with neither improving final construction accuracy over the other.

The finished model benefited speed; the partial model benefited explanation. Final construction accuracy was equal, so D captures the trade-off without making an overall ranking.

A: It invents an accuracy benefit.

B: It assigns better structural explanation to the wrong group and invents a mechanism for speed.

C: Similar accuracy does not establish similar overall learning; explanation differed.

If you missed it: Keep speed, explanation, and accuracy as separate outcomes rather than merging them into 'learning.'

Question 15

  • Guide question 1.15
  • Challenge

A critic describes an imagined weaver's early rugs as departures from local practice because they arrange familiar motifs in unusually open spaces. The weaver's notebooks reveal extensive study of older rugs, including sketches of their densely repeated borders. Her finished work rarely copies those borders. Instead, she enlarges the gaps between selected motifs, making the inherited forms appear isolated and newly prominent.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Synthesis with continuity and change

Correct choice: The weaver creates distinctive arrangements by changing the spacing of motifs she studied in local traditions.

The motifs are inherited, while their spacing is transformed. B explains how study of tradition and distinctive finished arrangements coexist.

A: The motifs are inherited, but their open arrangement differs from the older dense layouts. The notebooks do not show that the new arrangement was inherited.

C: The text describes greater visual prominence, not recovery of lost or obscured meanings. It does not explain her motivation in these terms.

D: Changing the spacing does not establish reduced dependence on local traditions; inherited forms remain central to the finished work.

If you missed it: Separate the motif, its arrangement, and its possible meaning. The text supports a change in arrangement, not every further claim about influence or intention.

Question 16

  • Guide question 1.16
  • Challenge

In a hypothetical memory study, adults learned names paired with unfamiliar faces. Half practiced by selecting names from a list; the rest practiced without seeing any options. The following day, everyone took two tests. The first offered answer choices and revealed no performance difference between the practice groups. The second required unaided naming, and those whose practice had omitted options scored higher. Researchers did not test the participants again, so they could not determine whether this difference persisted.

According to the text, which statement accurately describes the later tests?

Why this answer

Qualified detail about measured performance

Correct choice: Practice without options produced an advantage on unaided naming the next day, but not on the test that supplied choices.

The advantage belongs to practice without options, on the unaided test, the following day. The choice-based test showed equality. The two formats were tested on that day; they do not establish persistence over a longer period.

A: A matching-format advantage appeared for unaided naming, but the group practicing with choices had no advantage on the choice-based test.

B: Similar scores on a choice-based test do not establish equally durable memories. Persistence beyond the following day was not tested.

D: The choice-based test occurred first, not later. Equality on a different format also cannot show that the unaided-naming advantage faded over time.

If you missed it: Track practice format, test format, and testing time separately. A format difference is not evidence that an advantage grew or faded.

Question 17

  • Guide question 1.17
  • Challenge

An imagined city's market license records suggest that stalls selling prepared food became less common during a decade of street rebuilding. A historian also finds neighborhood diaries describing growing numbers of unlicensed evening food sellers. The diaries cannot establish a citywide total, but they complicate the apparent decline: the official records may partly reflect a shift toward activity they did not capture.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Central idea without flattening mixed evidence

Correct choice: Additional sources qualify a recorded decline without determining the overall extent of food selling.

The diaries identify activity missing from the licensing data, but their limited coverage prevents a citywide count. D retains both the challenge and its limit.

A: The diaries cannot establish the citywide direction of change.

B: Different coverage does not establish general superiority or citywide measurement accuracy.

C: The passage neither identifies the same sellers nor establishes that rebuilding caused a transition.

If you missed it: Distinguish evidence that a measure is incomplete from evidence establishing the true total.

Question 18

  • Guide question 1.18
  • Transfer

In an imagined essay on translating jokes, translator Mira calls explanatory notes useful when humor depends on an unfamiliar custom. She objects, however, when a note merely spells out a joke's already accessible surprise. In that case, she writes, an explanation can replace the reader's discovery with instruction. Her objection concerns notes that explain what readers can grasp themselves, not notes as a category.

According to the text, under which circumstance does Mira object to an explanatory note?

Why this answer

Precise detail about an author's bounded position

Correct choice: When the note explains a surprise that readers could understand without it.

Mira objects specifically to unnecessary explanation that displaces an accessible discovery. B states that condition.

A: This is the circumstance in which Mira calls notes useful.

C: The passage does not discuss replacing customs in translation.

D: Providing background beyond literal wording can be useful; this description is too broad to match her objection.

If you missed it: Extract the exact condition attached to the author's objection rather than generalizing the objection.

Question 19

  • Guide question 1.19
  • Challenge

My brother said the old radio was beyond repair. I kept it on the kitchen shelf anyway, turning its silent dial while the kettle warmed. One morning he arrived carrying a small speaker. 'For the shelf,' he said. I moved the radio a few inches to make room. Soon music filled the kitchen, and his shoulders loosened. I had thought he wanted the radio gone. Perhaps he had only wanted the silence gone.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Central idea through an understated literary shift

Correct choice: The narrator reconsiders the brother's concern after seeing how music affects him.

The relaxed shoulders and final contrast lead the narrator to reinterpret what the brother wanted. The old radio remains, and the new interpretation is tentative.

A: The narrator makes room for the speaker while keeping the radio. Acceptance of additional music is not willingness to replace the cherished object.

B: The narrator revises an interpretation of the brother's concern; the brother does not retract his judgment about the radio or admit error.

D: The brother's relief suggests a wish for music, not a shared attachment to the radio. This transfers the narrator's attachment to him.

If you missed it: Distinguish the narrator's revised interpretation from a change in the brother's position, and retain which person is attached to which object.

Question 20

  • Guide question 1.20
  • Challenge

A hypothetical museum trial replaced chronological labels in one room with labels grouping objects by use. Visitors more often connected objects made centuries apart, but they less often remembered which objects came first. Curators retained the thematic groupings and added a small timeline. They wanted to support comparisons across periods while making chronological relationships easier to recover.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

Why this answer

Adequate central idea versus true but narrow alternatives

Correct choice: Curators adjusted a thematic display to retain its comparative benefit while addressing a chronological weakness.

A covers the mixed trial result and the purpose of the adjustment. The passage reports the decision to add a timeline, not the adjustment's eventual effectiveness.

B: It is true but omits the memory drawback and the curators' response.

C: The two reported outcomes differed; equivalence is unsupported.

D: It turns the intended benefit of the timeline into a demonstrated result.

If you missed it: Separate the trial's observed result, the subsequent decision, and the outcome the decision aims to produce.
