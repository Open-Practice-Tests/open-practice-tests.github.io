Four checks for close answers

Check

Ask

Common failure

Direction

Does the answer preserve which thing affects, exceeds, precedes, or supports which?

A reversed cause, comparison, trend, or response.

Strength

Does the degree of certainty or quantity match the evidence?

A possible explanation becomes the only explanation; some becomes most.

Scope

Does the answer keep the same group, variable, time, and conditions?

A finding for one measured outcome is extended to all outcomes or settings.

Relevance

Does the statement answer this question or bear on this exact claim?

A true detail replaces the central idea, or accurate data support a different claim.

When two choices seem right

  • Name the smallest difference between the choices. Does one add a cause, remove a limitation, cover only an example, or use a different population?
  • Return to the sentence or data that decides that difference. Do not reread everything with no specific question in mind.
  • Keep an answer only if all of its essential parts are supported. One accurate clause cannot rescue a second unsupported clause.
  • A main-idea answer should have the right breadth. A detail answer can be narrow. A strengthening finding need not prove the claim. The task determines what counts as enough.
  • Do not reject a statement merely for strong wording. Strong evidence can justify a strong conclusion. Likewise, may or some cannot rescue an irrelevant or reversed claim.

Limits that matter in data questions

A displayed mean does not establish every individual result. A higher total need not be a higher rate. A before-and-after change alone does not isolate its cause. A graph without information about uncertainty or a statistical test does not, by itself, establish statistical significance. Read what is measured before extending what it means.

Three useful retest prompts

  • Central ideas and inferences: Which phrase would have to change for the closest wrong answer to become supportable?
  • Textual evidence: What claim would the tempting wrong finding support? How does that differ from the actual claim?
  • Quantitative evidence: Which row, series, denominator, or interval did I need? Recreate that comparison without looking at the choices.

Move from accuracy to speed

First aim for correct reasoning on unfamiliar items. Then reduce unnecessary rereading and annotation. If the same misconception recurs, revisit the worked example and explain the correction aloud before adding more timed practice. Use official practice tests to assess performance under the actual test conditions. [1]

Every multiple-choice example and practice item explains the correct choice and all three alternatives.
