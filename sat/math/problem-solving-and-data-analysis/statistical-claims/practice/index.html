Question 1

  • Guide question E7A

Researchers randomly sample adults from a town, record self-selected reading habits and vocabulary scores, and find an association. All selected adults respond. Which inference is supported by this design?

Why this answer

Answer: A

Random sampling supports generalizing the observed association to the town’s adults. Reading habits were observed, not assigned, so other factors may explain some or all of the association. Random assignment is not required to describe an association, but is central to the randomized causal comparisons taught here.

Question 2

  • Guide question E7B

Volunteers are randomly assigned to two training programs. The study is sound and finds convincing evidence of a mean improvement difference. Which conclusion is best?

Why this answer

Answer: D

Random treatment assignment can support a causal comparison among the participating volunteers. It does not make their recruitment representative of all people. Separate the assignment stage from the sampling stage.

Question 3

  • Guide question E7C

Which feature most directly reduces systematic confounding in a treatment comparison?

Why this answer

Answer: B

Random assignment tends to balance other characteristics across treatment groups, preventing treatment from being systematically assigned to one kind of participant. It does not guarantee exact balance or a successful treatment. Selecting only successful participants would introduce bias.

Question 4

  • Guide question E7D

Only 30 of 200 randomly selected people respond to a survey. Which concern is most relevant?

Why this answer

Answer: C

The initial random sample can be legitimate while the final responding subset is systematically different. This creates possible nonresponse bias. Neither its existence nor its direction is established merely by the low response count, but it is an important concern.

Question 5

  • Guide question E7E

An instructor uses a new method only in morning classes and the old method only in afternoon classes. The morning classes perform better. Which limitation is most direct?

Why this answer

Answer: A

The treatment groups differ systematically in both method and class time; student composition may also differ. The observed advantage cannot be assigned solely to the method from this comparison. A better design would randomize methods within relevant class-time groups when feasible.
