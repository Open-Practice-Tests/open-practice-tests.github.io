Evaluate 11 − 32 + 2.

Why this answer

2.

Question 2

  • Guide question M02

If f (x) = 3x2 + 2x − 5, what is f (−2)?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

3.

Substitute −2 using parentheses: 3(−2)2 + 2(−2) − 5 = 12 − 4 − 5 = 3. A definition f(x)=3*x^2+2*x-5 followed by f(-2) is also valid. The squared negative is positive; the linear term remains negative. Review Example 1.04.

Question 3

  • Guide question M03

The graph of y = x2 − 6x + 8 intersects the x-axis twice. What is the greater x-coordinate?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

4.

The equation factors as (x − 2)(x − 4) = 0, giving intercept inputs 2 and 4. Thus the greater is 4. The vertex at (3, −1) is a different feature and does not answer the intercept question. A graph should be used to inspect the zeros, not simply the lowest point. Review Example 2.03.

Question 4

  • Guide question M04

For the data set 6, 8, 10, 16, what is the median?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

9.

There are four ordered values, so average the middle two: (8 + 10)/2 = 9. A median command agrees. The mean is 40/4 = 10, which is different and illustrates why a statistics menu does not choose the requested statistic for you. Review Example 3.10.

Question 5

  • Guide question M05

A circle has radius 18 meters. Find its area to the nearest tenth of a square meter.

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

56.5 m2.

The exact area is π(18)2 = 18π ≈ 56.5486678. Rounding to the nearest tenth gives 56.5. Enter 18*pi; rounding the radius before squaring would introduce unnecessary error. Review Example 4.08.

Question 6

  • Guide question M06

For real x, y, suppose 3x + y ≤ 15, y ≥ 2x + 5, and x ≥ 0. What is the greatest possible x?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

2.

The bounds on y require 2x + 5 ≤ 15 − 3x, so 5x ≤ 10 and x ≤ 2. At x = 2, choose y = 9; both boundary equations hold, so the upper bound is attainable. Graphing the overlap gives the same rightmost point (2, 9). Review Example 2.13.

Evaluate 500(1.04)5 to the nearest whole number.

608.

Question 8

  • Guide question M08

In a right triangle, an acute angle has opposite side 12 and hypotenuse 20. Find the angle in degrees to the nearest tenth.

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

36.9°. The angle is sin−1(12/20) = sin−1(0.6) ≈ 36.86989765°. Set degrees and use the inverse-sine function, not the reciprocal of sine. The answer rounds to 36.9° and is acute as required. Review Example 1.11.

Question 9

  • Guide question M09

The curves y = x2 − 1 and y = 2x + 2 intersect at a point with positive x-coordinate. At that point, what is y − x?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

5.

Set x2 − 1 = 2x + 2, giving (x − 3)(x + 1) = 0. The positive input is x = 3, and then y = 8. Therefore y − x = 8 − 3 = 5. The other intersection is (−1, 0) and violates the positive-input condition. Review Example 4.12.

Question 11

  • Guide question M11

How many real solutions does (x2 − 25)/(x − 5) = 10 have?

Why this answer

A. 0.

The original expression excludes x = 5. Cancelling for allowed inputs gives x + 5 = 10, whose only candidate is the forbidden input 5. Therefore there are no solutions. A line-shaped display after cancellation does not make the original rational expression defined at the hole. Review Example 4.03.

Question 12

  • Guide question M12

The values 0, 3, and 6 occur with frequencies 2, 5, and 3, respectively. Find the mean of all observations.

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

3.3.

The weighted total is 0(2) + 3(5) + 6(3) = 33 and the count is 10. Thus the mean is 33/10 = 3.3. Averaging the three distinct values would give 3, but would discard their different frequencies. Review Example 3.11.

Question 13

  • Guide question M13

If 2x + 3y = 14 and 4x + y = 18, what is 6x + 4y?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

32.

Adding the entire equations directly gives 6x + 4y = 14 + 18 = 32. Neither variable needs to be isolated. This is an example in which recognizing the requested combination is shorter than graphing both lines and reconstructing the expression from their intersection. Review Example 5.03.

Question 15

  • Guide question M15

A model is P(t) = 60(0.7)t/2. Find P(6).

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

20.58.

At t = 6, the exponent is 6/2 = 3. Thus P(6) = 60(0.7)3 = 60(0.343) = 20.58. The factor 0.7 is applied once every two time units, so using exponent 6 would double the number of decay periods. Review Example 3.08.

Each bus seats at most 40 passengers. What is the least number of buses required for 294 passengers?

8.

294/40 = 7.35. Seven buses provide 280 seats, which is insufficient; eight provide 320. The capacity inequality requires increasing to the next integer, not ordinary rounding to 7. Review Example 4.10.

Question 17

  • Guide question M17

How many distinct real solutions does (x − 4)2 = 0.0004 have?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

2.

Taking both square-root signs gives x − 4 = ±0.02, so the roots are 3.98 and 4.02. They are distinct even if a broad graph window makes them appear merged. The exact positive right side guarantees two roots; replacing it with zero would change the question. Review Example 4.13.

Question 18

  • Guide question M18

A linear function has values 5, 11, and 17 at inputs 1, 4, and 7. What is its value at input 10?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

23.

Each input increase of 3 corresponds to an output increase of 6, giving slope 2. Moving from input 7 to 10 adds another 6 to the output: 17 + 6 = 23. Equivalently the model is y = 2x + 3. A table makes the input spacing visible and prevents mistaking 6 for the slope. Review Example 3.04.

Question 19

  • Guide question M19

A random sample of students reports study hours and scores. A fitted line has positive slope, but study hours were not randomly assigned. Which conclusion is supported by that description?

Why this answer

B.

A positive fitted slope describes an association in the observed data. The design does not establish a causal effect, a universal rule about every student, or an absence of confounding. Random sampling addresses selection for population inference; it is not the same as random assignment of a treatment. Review Example 5.12.

Question 20

  • Guide question M20

A rental costs a fixed $25 plus $17 per hour. With a budget of $200, what is the greatest whole number of hours that can be rented?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

10.

The budget condition is 25 + 17h ≤ 200, so h ≤ 175/17 ≈ 10.2941. The greatest allowable whole number is 10. Ten hours cost $195; eleven cost $212 and exceed the budget. Here the inequality requires moving down to an integer, unlike a minimum-capacity problem. Review Example 4.10.

Question 22

  • Guide question M22

How many intersection points do x2 + y2 = 4 and y = x2 − 2 have?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

3.

Substitute y = x2 − 2 and let u = x2 ≥ 0. Then u + (u − 2)2 = 4, which simplifies to u(u − 3) = 0. For u = 0, there is the point (0, −2). For u = 3, the points are (3, 1) and (−3, 1). Count ordered pairs, not just the two possible values of u. Review Example 2.14.

Question 23

  • Guide question M23

A quadratic model passes exactly through (0, 1), (1, 6), and (2, 15). What is its value at x = 3?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

28.

Write y = ax2 + bx + c. The first point gives c = 1; the others give a + b = 5 and 4a + 2b = 14. Subtraction yields a = 2, then b = 3. Thus f (3) = 2(9) + 3(3) + 1 = 28. Quadratic regression on these exact pairs provides the same model. Review Example 3.07.

Question 24

  • Guide question M24

For a nonnegative integer n, let V(n) = 90(0.9)n. What is the least n for which V(n) < 50?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

6.

V(5) = 53.1441 > 50 and V(6) = 47.82969 < 50. The factor 0.9 makes the model decrease as n increases. These adjacent checks show that 6 is the first whole-number input meeting the strict inequality. A decimal crossing alone is not the requested integer. Review Example 5.15.

Question 26

  • Guide question M26

For real x, what is the maximum value of g(x) = −2(x − 5)2 + 18?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

18.

Because (x − 5)2 ≥ 0, the term −2(x − 5)2 is at most zero. Therefore g(x) ≤ 18, with equality at x = 5. Vertex form supplies the answer directly. The maximum input is not the question; report the output 18 rather than 5. Review Example 5.05.

Question 29

  • Guide question M29

A fitted model is y = 3.2x + 0.5. An observed point is (4, 15). Using residual = observed minus predicted, find the residual.

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

1.7.

The prediction at input 4 is 3.2(4) + 0.5 = 13.3. The residual is 15 − 13.3 = 1.7, positive because the observation lies above the fitted line. Subtracting in the reverse order would reverse the sign and change the interpretation. Review Example 3.06.

    Guide question M21

    A student claims that (x + 4)2 = x2 + 16 for every real x because both expressions equal 16 at x = 0. Is the claim valid? Give a decisive check.

    Show model answerHide model answer for question M21

    No; x = 1 is a counterexample.

    At x = 1, the left side is 25 while the right side is 17. Algebra explains the mismatch: (x + 4)2 = x2 + 8x + 16, so the claimed form omits 8x. One successful input does not establish an identity; one valid counterexample disproves it. Review Example 4.14.

    Guide question M25

    The exact answer is −7/3. Give a suitable decimal entry under the numeric-response character limit.

    Show model answerHide model answer for question M25

    -2.333.

    −7/3 = −2.3333.... The entry -2.333 uses six characters including the minus sign and keeps the available decimal precision. The exact fraction -7/3 is another suitable entry, though this question requests a decimal. Do not enter a mixed number or omit the negative sign. Review Example 4.06.

    Guide question M27

    A default graph window shows no intersections between y = x2 and y = 1600. What is the positive solution of x2 = 1600, and what was wrong with the visual conclusion?

    Show model answerHide model answer for question M27

    40; the relevant intersections were outside the window.

    402 = 1600, so the positive root is 40; the other root is −40. A useful window must include both the input scale and output level, such as −50 ≤ x ≤ 50 and 0 ≤ y ≤ 1800. Absence from a particular viewport is not absence from the mathematical graph. Review Example 2.08.

    Guide question M28

    For which real values of k does x2 + kx + 16 = 0 have exactly one distinct real root?

    Show model answerHide model answer for question M28

    k = −8 or k = 8.

    The coefficient of x2 is fixed at 1, so this equation is always quadratic. A single distinct real root requires k2 − 64 = 0, hence k = ±8. The equations become (x + 4)2 = 0 and (x − 4)2 = 0. Unlike a variable-leading-coefficient problem, there is no additional linear case. Review Example 5.06.
