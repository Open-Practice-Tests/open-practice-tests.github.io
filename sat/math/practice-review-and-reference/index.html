How to use this guide

This capstone turns the seven earlier guides into a repeatable practice system. It is not another content domain. Use it to find a specific gap, repair the gap, and test whether the repair survives a fresh question without a chapter heading giving away the method.

Three kinds of work, three different purposes

Targeted practice Learn or repair a particular skill. Hints, notes, and untimed work can be appropriate, but record their use.

Mixed practice Choose the skill and method yourself. Work with lessons closed before checking the explanations.

Timed rehearsal Measure what you can produce with fixed time, permitted tools, and genuine module boundaries. Preserve the original responses.

What is included

There are 90 worked examples, probes, and review cases: 15 in each of six sections. Examples 3.11–3.15 and 5.13–5.15 are labeled workflow scenarios. Section 4 contains 15 flawed-solution case studies; these are diagnostic learning tasks, not ordinary SAT multiple-choice questions.

There are also 166 independent questions: 48 targeted questions, 30 mixed questions, and 88 questions in two full-length Math-section rehearsals. Every independent question has a correct answer, an explanation, a skill tag, and a link back to a relevant worked example or reference page. These counts do not include the worked examples a second time.

Protect the practice from spoilers Solutions stay hidden until you choose to review them. Open a solution only after you have recorded an answer and your method. A recognized answer is not the same evidence as a fresh, independent solution.

What these rehearsals do not claim

Both rehearsals are original, fixed, nonadaptive Math practice. They are not official SAT forms, are not psychometrically calibrated, do not reproduce Reading and Writing, and cannot yield a supported 200–800 score conversion. Difficulty labels elsewhere in this guide are instructional judgments, not official classifications.

The SAT itself is adaptive, and students with the same number correct can earn different scores because question characteristics matter. Use raw results here as learning evidence, not a substitute for an official scaled score. [3]

A suggested sequence

Begin with one fresh mixed set, not by rereading the entire guide. Use the misses to select targeted questions. Review the relevant worked examples, then solve a different question without help. After repairing the clearest patterns, take Rehearsal 1. Review it fully before taking Rehearsal 2 on another day.

For a digital-environment check, use a fresh official Bluebook practice test. Full-length Bluebook practice is scored; the short test preview is untimed and does not provide answer feedback or scores. [4]

Record more than right or wrong

Next to each practice ID, record your answer, confidence, approximate time, and whether you used help. Keep a wrong answer with good reasoning distinct from a lucky correct answer with no defensible method. Both need review, but for different reasons.

How much practice is enough? There is no guaranteed question count. A useful working standard is independent success on fresh questions that change the numbers, representation, or target, followed by a later mixed check. The schedules and evidence levels in this guide are adjustable study recommendations, not official thresholds or score promises.
