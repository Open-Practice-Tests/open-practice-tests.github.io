Mixed practice: Algebra

30 questions · Guide order · Untimed practice

Instructions and review guidance

Work through the set before checking solutions. Guide question labels match the source lesson references.

Guide question labels stay the same when the order changes. Check answers when you are ready to review.

This practice lasts until you reload. Reloading clears answers. Written responses are self-review exercises.

These original questions mix the five Algebra skill areas so that you must select the method yourself. There are six questions per area. No question requires mathematics from the Advanced Math domain.

First attempt: work without the lessons or worked solutions in view. Use scratch paper and a calculator as needed. Circle or record uncertain answers even when you reach a result; a lucky answer is useful information only if you identify it as uncertain.

Optional timing: use 25 minutes for Questions 1–15 and 25 minutes for Questions 16–30. This is an editorial practice target, not an official module structure. The second half emphasizes harder structural reasoning. Use untimed practice first when a method is still unfamiliar.

Response formats: choose one answer for multiple choice. For student-produced responses, give only the requested number. Work with exact fractions when practical. All necessary context restrictions are stated or follow from the objects being counted.

After the attempt

Check your attempted questions, then read each explanation for a missed or uncertain item. Write the first decision you should have made, not just the arithmetic you should have performed. Use the domain map below to return to the relevant lesson.

Question 1

  • Guide question M01

If 3(2x − 1) = 4x + 11, what is x?

Why this answer

B, 7 Section 1

Expand to get 6x − 3 = 4x + 11. Subtract 4x and add 3: 2x = 14, so x = 7. Substitution gives 39 on both sides. Choice A stops at a wrong sign; choice C reports 2x rather than x.

Question 2

  • Guide question M02

What is the y-intercept of the line 2x − 5y = 15?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

−3 Section 2

At the y-intercept, x = 0. Then −5y = 15, so y = −3. The point is (0, −3). Setting y = 0 instead would find the x-intercept, not the requested intercept.

Question 3

  • Guide question M03

The table gives values of a linear function f . What is f (10)?

Why this answer

C, 42 Section 3

The rate is (21 − 12)/(3 − 0) = 3, consistent with (33 − 21)/(7 − 3) = 3. Because f (0) = 12, the rule is f (x) = 3x + 12. Thus f (10) = 30 + 12 = 42. The table does not have equal input gaps, so compare rates rather than raw differences.

Question 4

  • Guide question M04

The system x + y = 17 and 2x − y = 4 has solution (x, y). What is y?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

10 Section 4

Add the equations to eliminate y: 3x = 21, so x = 7. Then y = 17 − 7 = 10. The second equation checks: 2(7) − 10 = 4. Reporting 7 would answer the wrong variable.

Question 5

  • Guide question M05

Which inequality is equivalent to 8 − 3x ≥ 20?

Why this answer

A, x ≤ −4 Section 5

Subtract 8 to get −3x ≥ 12. Division by −3 reverses the relation, giving x ≤ −4. The endpoint is included because the original comparison includes equality. At x = −5, the left side is 23, confirming the allowed side.

Question 6

  • Guide question M06

A printer charges a $14 setup fee plus $0.08 per page. A job costs $30, with no other charges. How many pages are in the job?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

200 Section 1

Let n be pages. The equation is 14 + 0.08n = 30, so 0.08n = 16 and n = 16/0.08 = 200. Check: the variable charge is $16, and the fixed charge raises the total to $30.

Question 7

  • Guide question M07

Which equation represents the line shown?

Why this answer

A, y = 32 x − 2 Section 2

The line passes through (0, −2) and (4, 4). Its slope is (4 − (−2))/(4 − 0) = 6/4 = 3/2, and its y-intercept is −2. Therefore y = (3/2)x − 2. Both a correct slope and a correct intercept are required.

Question 8

  • Guide question M08

A tank's water volume, in liters, is modeled by W(t) = 250 − 5(t − 4), where t is minutes after observation began. What does the model give for W(0)?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

270 Section 3

Substitute zero into the entire input expression: W(0) = 250 − 5(0 − 4) = 250 + 20 = 270. The value 250 is anchored at t = 4, not at t = 0. Expanding to W(t) = 270 − 5t makes the initial value visible.

Question 9

  • Guide question M09

An event sells 120 tickets. General tickets cost $18 and reduced-price tickets cost $10. Total revenue is $1,760. How many general tickets were sold?

Why this answer

C, 70 Section 4

Let g + r = 120 and 18g + 10r = 1760. Subtract ten times the count equation from the revenue equation: 8g = 1760 − 1200 = 560, so g = 70. The other 50 tickets bring in $500; the general tickets bring in $1,260.

Question 10

  • Guide question M10

A poster order has a $22 setup charge plus $3.75 per poster. What is the greatest number of posters that can be purchased for at most $100?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

20 Section 5

The count satisfies 22 + 3.75n ≤ 100, so n ≤ 78/3.75 = 20.8. The greatest integer allowed is 20. Twenty posters cost $97; 21 cost $100.75, which exceeds the budget.

Question 11

  • Guide question M11

The equation a(2x + 1) = 5x + 13 has the solution x = 3. What is a?

Why this answer

D, 4 Section 1

Substitute the known solution: a(7) = 15 + 13 = 28. Hence a = 4. This is a parameter question, but a supplied solution turns it into a simple equation for the parameter. There is no need to derive a general formula for x.

Question 12

  • Guide question M12

A line passes through (2, 7) and is perpendicular to y = −2x + 9. What is its y-intercept?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

6 Section 2

The perpendicular slope is 1/2 because (−2)(1/2) = −1. Write y = (1/2)x + b and use the point: 7 = 1 + b, so b = 6. Check both features: the point lies on the line and the slopes multiply to −1.

Question 13

  • Guide question M13

A linear function satisfies f (2) = −1 and f (8) = 17. What is f (5)?

Why this answer

B, 8 Section 3

The slope is (17 − (−1))/(8 − 2) = 18/6 = 3. Moving from input 2 to input 5 adds 3, so the output adds 3(3) = 9: f (5) = −1 + 9 = 8. Equivalently, input 5 is the midpoint of 2 and 8, so its linear output is the midpoint of −1 and 17.

Question 14

  • Guide question M14

If 4x + y = 19 and x + 4y = 11, what is x + y?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

6 Section 4

Add the equations: 5x + 5y = 30. Factor and divide by 5 to get x + y = 6. The individual values are x = 13/3 and y = 5/3, but computing them is unnecessary and introduces fractions that the requested sum avoids.

Question 15

  • Guide question M15

Which inequality describes the shaded half-plane, including its solid boundary?

Why this answer

D, y ≤ 2x − 1 Section 5

The line passes through (0, −1) and (2, 3), giving slope (3 − (−1))/2 = 2. Its equation is y = 2x − 1. The shaded side is below the line and the boundary is solid, so equality is allowed: y ≤ 2x − 1. For example, (2, 0) is shaded and gives 0 ≤ 3.

Question 16

  • Guide question M16

For what value of k does k(x − 3) = 4x + 9 have no solution?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

4 Section 1

Expand to kx − 3k = 4x + 9. Matching the variable coefficients requires k = 4. Then the constants are −12 and 9, which disagree, so the equation has no solution. If k ≠ 4, the nonzero coefficient k − 4 instead gives one solution.

Question 17

  • Guide question M17

A bakery spends exactly $240 on x kilograms of flour priced at $3 per kilogram and y kilograms priced at $8 per kilogram. If x increases by 16 while the total stays fixed, what change in y is required?

Why this answer

B, A decrease of 6 kilograms Section 2

The total is 3x + 8y = 240. Changes therefore satisfy 3Δx + 8Δy = 0. With Δx = 16, 48 + 8Δy = 0, so Δy = −6. The negative sign means a decrease of 6 kilograms. The extra $48 spent on one flour is offset by $48 less of the other.

Question 18

  • Guide question M18

A service costs C(h) = 72h + 25 dollars for h hours. Written as C(t) = mt + 25 for t minutes, what is m?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

1.2 Section 3

One hour is 60 minutes, so h = t/60. Substitute to get C(t) = 72(t/60) + 25 = 1.2t + 25. Therefore m = 1.2 dollars per minute. The fixed $25 stays unchanged because it is not measured per hour.

Question 19

  • Guide question M19

The system (a − 2)x + 10y = 30 and 3x + 5y = b has infinitely many solutions. What is a + b?

Why this answer

C, 23 Section 4

The y coefficients show that the first equation must be twice the second. Hence a − 2 = 6 and 30 = 2b, giving a = 8 and b = 15. Their sum is 23. Matching the whole equation verifies coincidence, not merely parallelism.

Question 20

  • Guide question M20

Volunteers need at least 428 meal trays. They already have 53 trays and can buy cartons of 24. What is the least number of cartons they need to buy?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

16 Section 5

Let c be cartons. Then 53 + 24c ≥ 428, so 24c ≥ 375 and c ≥ 15.625. The least integer is 16. Fifteen cartons produce only 53 + 360 = 413 trays; sixteen produce 437.

Question 21

  • Guide question M21

The equation a(2x − 3) + b = 10x + 7 is true for every real x. What is a + b?

Why this answer

B, 27 Section 1

Expand to 2ax − 3a + b = 10x + 7. Matching x coefficients gives 2a = 10, so a = 5. Matching constants gives −15 + b = 7, so b = 22. Thus a + b = 27. The constant on the left is −3a + b, not merely b.

Question 22

  • Guide question M22

The graph of 3x + 2y = 12 is translated 2 units left and 5 units down. What is the y-intercept of the translated line?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

−2 Section 2

The old coordinates are x+2 and y+5 in terms of the new ones. The translated equation is 3(x+2)+2(y+5) = 12, or 3x + 2y = −4. Setting x = 0 gives y = −2. As a check, the original point (0, 6) moves to (−2, 1), which satisfies the new equation.

Question 23

  • Guide question M23

The linear function f (x) = mx + b satisfies f (a) = b − 12 and f (a + 5) = b + 8. What is a?

Why this answer

A, −3 Section 3

The output change is (b + 8) − (b − 12) = 20 for an input change of 5, so m = 4. The first condition then gives 4a + b = b − 12, so 4a = −12 and a = −3. The intercept cancels and need not be known.

Question 24

  • Guide question M24

Every solution of 3x − 2y = 9 and 6x − 4y = 18 gives the same value for kx − 8y. What is k?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

12 Section 4

The system describes the single line 3x − 2y = 9. Four times its equation gives 12x − 8y = 36, so k = 12 works. For uniqueness, compare the allowed points (3, 0) and (0, −9/2): the expression gives 3k and 36, respectively. Equality forces k = 12.

Question 25

  • Guide question M25

A nonnegative integer x and a real number y satisfy y > 3x − 2 and y ≤ −x + 10. What is the greatest possible x?

Why this answer

C, 2 Section 5

An allowed y requires 3x − 2 < −x + 10, so 4x < 12 and x < 3. The greatest permitted integer is 2. It is attainable, for example with y = 5, since 5 > 4 and 5 ≤ 8. At x = 3, the system would require y > 7 and y ≤ 7, impossible.

Question 26

  • Guide question M26

If 4(5x − 2) + 73 = 2(5x − 2) + 1, what is 10x − 4?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

4 Section 1

Let u = 5x − 2, so the target is 2u. Multiply the equation (4u + 7)/3 = 2u + 1 by 3 to get 4u + 7 = 6u + 3. Thus 4 = 2u, already the desired value. There is no need to solve for x.

Question 27

  • Guide question M27

The line ax + by = 30 has y-intercept 5 and slope −2/3. What is a + b?

Why this answer

D, 10 Section 2

The intercept point (0, 5) gives 5b = 30, so b = 6. In standard form the slope is −a/b, so −a/6 = −2/3 and a = 4. Therefore a + b = 10. Check: 4x + 6y = 30 rearranges to y = −(2/3)x + 5.

Question 28

  • Guide question M28

A linear function has f (1) = 5 and f (4) = 14. The function g is defined by g(x) = f (2x + c) − 1. If g(3) = 28, what is c?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

3 Section 3

The slope of f is (14−5)/(4−1) = 3, and its intercept is 2, so f (x) = 3x+2. Thus g(3) = 3(6+c)+2−1 = 19+3c. Setting this equal to 28 gives 3c = 9, so c = 3. Check directly: g(3) = f (9) − 1 = 29 − 1 = 28.

Question 29

  • Guide question M29

The lines 2x + 3y = 18 and kx − y = 4 intersect at a point satisfying y = 2x. What is k?

Why this answer

C, 349 Section 4

Use y = 2x in the equation without k: 2x + 3(2x) = 18, so x = 9/4 and y = 9/2. The other equation gives (9/4)k − 9/2 = 4, so (9/4)k = 17/2. Therefore k = (17/2)(4/9) = 34/9. Retaining exact fractions avoids rounding a parameter that must satisfy the condition precisely.

Question 30

  • Guide question M30

Nonnegative real numbers x and y satisfy x + y ≥ 9 and 3x + y ≥ 17. What is the least possible value of 4x + 2y?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

26 Section 5

Add the constraints to obtain 4x + 2y ≥ 26. To show that 26 can be reached, solve the boundary equations x + y = 9 and 3x + y = 17. They give x = 4 and y = 5, both nonnegative. This pair satisfies the original inequalities and makes the target 16 + 10 = 26.
