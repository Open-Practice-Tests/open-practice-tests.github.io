Question

  • Guide question M01

What is the value of −6 + 2(5 − 8)?

Why this answer

A; −12. Evaluate the parentheses first: 5 − 8 = −3. Multiply next: 2(−3) = −6. Finally, −6 + (−6) = −12. A common error is to multiply the initial −6 by 2, but those terms are separated by addition. The expression’s structure, not the order of the printed numbers alone, determines the calculation. Repair: Example 2.01.

Question

  • Guide question M02

What is 710 − 14?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

9/20. Convert both fractions to twentieths: 7/10 = 14/20 and 1/4 = 5/20. The difference is 14/20 − 5/20 = 9/20. A decimal check gives 0.70 − 0.25 = 0.45 = 9/20. Fractions are subtracted by counting like-sized parts, which is why a common denominator is necessary. Repair: Example 2.04.

Question

  • Guide question M03

The decimal 0.48 is equal to p%. What is p?

Why this answer

D; 48. The statement 0.48 = p% means 0.48 = p/100. Multiply by 100 to obtain p = 48. The question asks for the number before the percent sign, not the original decimal. Choice B would incorrectly turn 0.48 into 0.48% = 0.0048. Repair: Example 2.10.

Question

  • Guide question M04

Which expression is equivalent to 5 − 2(3x − 4)?

Why this answer

B; −6x + 13. Distribute the signed factor −2: 5 − 2(3x − 4) = 5 − 6x + 8 = −6x + 13. The product (−2)(−4) is positive 8. At x = 0, the original expression equals 13, which immediately rules out options whose constants are −3 or 1. That check supports the expansion; the distributive argument proves it. Repair: Example 3.05.

Question

  • Guide question M05

The function f is defined by f (t) = 4t + 7. For what value of t is f (t) = 31?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

6. The output is fixed at 31, so solve 4t + 7 = 31. Subtract 7 to get 4t = 24, then divide by 4 to get t = 6. Verify the input by evaluating f (6) = 31. Computing f (31) would confuse the supplied output with an input. Repair: Example 4.05.

Question

  • Guide question M07

A conveyor moves an object 180 meters in 3 minutes at a constant speed. How many meters does the object move in 8 minutes?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

480 meters. A constant speed of 180 meters in 3 minutes is 180/3 = 60 meters per minute. In 8 minutes the distance is 60(8) = 480 meters. The units verify the operation: meters per minute multiplied by minutes gives meters. A proportion 180/3 = d/8 produces the same result. Repair: Example 5.08.

Question

  • Guide question M08

A larger box contains four fewer than twice the number of balls in a smaller box. The boxes contain 32 balls altogether. How many balls are in the smaller box?

Why this answer

B; 12 balls. Let b be the smaller box’s count. The larger count is 2b − 4, so b + (2b − 4) = 32. Then 3b = 36 and b = 12. The larger count is 2(12) − 4 = 20, and 12 + 20 = 32. Choice D gives the other box’s count, not the target. Repair: Example 5.03.

Question

  • Guide question M09

What is the value of −42 + (−2)3?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

−24. An exponent is evaluated before a leading negative sign, so −42 = −16. The parenthesized base −2 is cubed: (−2)3 = −8. Add the two terms to obtain −16 − 8 = −24. An even power makes a parenthesized negative base positive; an odd power leaves it negative. Repair: Example 2.02.

Question

  • Guide question M10

Of 60 survey responses, 18 select option A. What percentage of the responses select option A?

Why this answer

C; 30%. The fraction selecting A is 18/60 = 3/10 = 0.3. Convert that proportion to a percent by multiplying by 100: 30%. The denominator is the total number of responses, not the number that chose other options. Choice A mistakes the proportion for the percent number. Repair: Example 2.10.

Question

  • Guide question M11

What is 35 ÷ 920?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

4/3. Invert the divisor and multiply: (3/5)(20/9) = 60/45 = 4/3. This is greater than 1 because 3/5 is greater than 9/20: more than one divisor-sized group fits in the dividend. Decimal checking gives 0.6/0.45 = 1.333.... Repair: Example 2.07.

Question

  • Guide question M12

Which expression is equivalent to 7.2×1053×102?

Why this answer

A; 2.4 × 103.

Divide coefficients and subtract exponents:

7.2 × 1053 × 102 = 7.23 × 105−2 = 2.4 × 103.

The coefficient 2.4 is already in the standard range. Adding the exponents, as in choice B, would use the multiplication rule for a division problem. Repair: Example 2.12.

Question

  • Guide question M13

If x − 3 = 4, what is the value of 2(x − 3)2 − 5?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

27. The given equation already supplies the group used in the target. Substitute x −3 = 4 directly: 2(x −3)2 −5 = 2(4)2 − 5 = 32 − 5 = 27. Solving for x = 7 and then substituting also works, but it adds an unnecessary step. Repair: Example 3.14.

Question

  • Guide question M14

The graph shown is y = f (x). What is the zero of f ?

Why this answer

C; 3. A zero of f is an input where the output equals zero. The graph crosses the horizontal axis at (3, 0), so the zero is 3. The point (0, 6) gives the vertical intercept and the value f (0), not a zero. Repair: Example 4.06.

Question

  • Guide question M15

Which condition describes the real domain of h(x) = x+4x−2?

Why this answer

C; x ≥ −4 and x ≠ 2. For a real square root, x + 4 ≥ 0, which gives x ≥ −4. The denominator must also be nonzero: x − 2 ≠ 0, so x ≠ 2. The endpoint x = −4 is allowed because its numerator is zero and its denominator is −6. Both restrictions are necessary. Repair: Example 4.11.

Question

  • Guide question M16

A linear function L satisfies L(2) = 11 and L(6) = 23. What is L(0)?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

5. Because L is stated to be linear, its rate is constant. From (2, 11) to (6, 23), the rate is (23 − 11)/(6 − 2) = 3. Moving from input 2 back to input 0 lowers the output by 3(2) = 6, so L(0) = 11 − 6 = 5. Equivalently, L(x) = 3x + 5. Repair: Example 4.07.

Question

  • Guide question M17

A value of 45 is decreased by 20%, and the resulting value is then increased by 25%. What is the final value?

Why this answer

C; 45. A 20% decrease multiplies by 0.80, producing 45(0.80) = 36. A 25% increase then multiplies the new value by 1.25, producing 36(1.25) = 45. Here 0.80(1.25) = 1, so the changes exactly reverse. They do not cancel merely because one is a decrease and the other an increase; the bases differ. Repair: Example 5.06.

Question

  • Guide question M18

A printer produces 36 pages in 90 seconds at a constant rate. How many pages does it produce in 150 seconds?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

60 pages. The rate is 36/90 = 0.4 page per second. At that constant rate, 150 seconds produces 0.4(150) = 60 pages. You can also scale time by 150/90 = 5/3 and scale pages by the same factor: 36(5/3) = 60. Repair: Example 5.08.

Question

  • Guide question M19

An order costs a fixed $11 plus $4 per item. With a $40 budget, what is the greatest number of whole items that can be ordered?

Why this answer

A; 7 items. Let n be the whole-item count. The cost condition 11 + 4n ≤ 40 gives n ≤ 29/4 = 7.25. The greatest nonnegative integer allowed is 7. Seven items cost $39; eight cost $43 and exceed the budget. Rounding 7.25 down here follows the constraint, not a universal rule for all word problems. Repair: Example 5.10.

Question

  • Guide question M20

Which relation defines y as a function of x?

Why this answer

B.

In option B, the inputs 0, 1, and 2 each have exactly one output. The shared output 4 is allowed. In A, input 1 has outputs 2 and 3; in C, input 0 has outputs 1 and 2; in D, input 2 has outputs 6 and 7. Those repeated-input conflicts violate the function definition. Repair: Example 4.09.

Question

  • Guide question M21

Which of the following numbers is least?

Why this answer

A; −0.7. Convert the fractions for comparison: −5/8 = −0.625 and −2/3 = −0.666.... On the number line, −0.7 lies to the left of both and is therefore least. For negative numbers, the greater absolute value corresponds to the smaller number. Zero is greater than all three negatives. Repair: Example 2.11.

Question

  • Guide question M22

Which statement correctly simplifies x2−36x−6 while preserving its original domain?

Why this answer

C; x + 6 for x ≠ 6. Factor the numerator as (x − 6)(x + 6) and divide out the nonzero factor x − 6. The original denominator excludes x = 6, and that restriction survives simplification. The value x = −6 is allowed and produces zero, so choice D excludes the wrong input. Repair: Example 3.08.

Question

  • Guide question M23

The function f is defined by f (x) = kx + 9, where k is constant. If f (−2) = 3, what is f (4)?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

21. Use the known input-output pair to find the parameter: f (−2) = −2k + 9 = 3. Thus −2k = −6 and k = 3. Now f (4) = 3(4) + 9 = 21. Check the parameter against the original information: 3(−2) + 9 = 3. The target is a function value, not k. Repair: Example 3.13.

Question

  • Guide question M24

A theater sells 20 tickets. Adult tickets cost $12 and child tickets cost $7. Total revenue is $185. How many child tickets were sold?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

11 child tickets. Let c be the number of child tickets, so 20 − c are adult tickets. Revenue gives 7c + 12(20 − c) = 185. Simplifying yields 240 − 5c = 185, so c = 11. Check: 11 child tickets and 9 adult tickets give 77 + 108 = 185. An all-adult baseline gives the same result: the $55 reduction requires eleven $5 substitutions. Repair: Example 5.13.

Question

  • Guide question M25

How many real solutions does 2(3x − 1) = 6x + 5 have?

Why this answer

A; no real solutions. Distribute to get 6x − 2 = 6x + 5. Subtracting 6x leaves −2 = 5, a false statement independent of x. No value can repair that contradiction. Variables disappearing does not automatically mean infinitely many solutions; that requires a true statement such as 5 = 5. Repair: Example 3.11.

Question

  • Guide question M26

A straight graph segment joins a closed point at (−3, 2) to an open point at (1, −2). What is its range?

Why this answer

B; (−2, 2]. The range consists of outputs. Along the segment, y decreases from the included value 2 to the excluded value −2. Write those outputs from least to greatest: (−2, 2]. The domain would instead be [−3, 1); it answers an input question. Repair: Example 4.12.

Question

  • Guide question M27

A tank is initially full. After 512 of the water is used, 27 of the remaining water is used. There are then 50 liters left. What was the initial volume of water?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

120 liters. Let V be the initial volume. After 5/12 is used, 7/12 remains. Using 2/7 of that remainder leaves 5/7 of it. Therefore the final amount is (5/7)(7/12)V = (5/12)V = 50, so V = 120. Check: 50 liters are used first, then 20 of the remaining 70, leaving 50. Repair: Example 5.14.

Question

  • Guide question M28

An area is 2.4 square meters. How many square centimeters is that? Use 1 meter = 100 centimeters.

Why this answer

C; 24,000 square centimeters.

An area conversion uses the squared length factor:

2.4 m2 ( 100 cm1 m )2 = 2.4(10,000) cm2 = 24,000 cm2.

A one-meter-by-one-meter square contains 100 × 100 square centimeters, not 100. Repair: Section 5.3.

Question

  • Guide question M29

Four measurements have mean 18. Three of the measurements are 16, 21, and 14. What is the fourth measurement?

Enter an integer, decimal or fraction without units. Use up to 5 characters, or 6 including a leading minus.

Why this answer

21. Mean equals total divided by count, so the four measurements total 4(18) = 72. The known values total 16 + 21 + 14 = 51. The missing value is 72 − 51 = 21. Verification: (16 + 21 + 14 + 21)/4 = 18. Do not average the three known values and treat that result as the missing fourth. Repair: the mean relationship in Section 5.3.

Question

  • Guide question M30

A function f satisfies f (−1) = 2 and f (3) = 10. Which additional condition is sufficient to determine that f (1) = 6?

Why this answer

B; f is linear. For a linear function, the rate is (10 − 2)/(3 − (−1)) = 8/4 = 2, giving f (x) = 2x + 4 and f (1) = 6. The other conditions are too weak: f (x) = x2 + 1 also meets the two given values and has positive outputs at 0 and 2, yet f (1) = 2. Thus those positivity conditions do not determine the requested value. Repair: Example 4.15.

Stop before opening the solutions

Mark answers that were guessed or slow even when you expect them to be correct. For each uncertain item, name the missing step rather than assigning a broad topic label. Then use the explanations to test whether your reasoning, not only your final number, was sound.

What this set can tell you

A mixed set tests whether you can recognize a method without being told the topic. Compare three measures: correct answers, secure explanations, and completion under your chosen conditions. A faster attempt with more unexplained guesses is not necessarily progress.

A concrete review rule

For a missed item, cover the solution and reconstruct the model in your own words. Then change one feature: the sign, the target quantity, the axis unit, the percent base, or the integer constraint. Explain how the method changes. Finish by solving a fresh independent question. Do not use this guide’s accuracy rate to predict a digital SAT score.
